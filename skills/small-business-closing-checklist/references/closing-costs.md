# Closing costs

Mark each line known (amount and source document), estimated (labelled as an estimate) or unknown (who can say). Don't quote typical amounts as facts; fees vary by lender, state and deal. Amounts set by the lender or by SBA rules come from the lender's documents; confirm them with the lender because they change.

## Financing
- Lender origination or packaging fees.
- SBA guaranty fee, where an SBA-guaranteed loan is used (set by the SBA and passed through by the lender; often financed into the loan; confirm the current amount with the lender).
- Lender's legal fees, if the borrower pays them.
- Appraisal or business valuation.
- Real estate appraisal and survey, if real estate is part of the deal.
- Environmental report, if required.
- Credit reports, lien and litigation searches, UCC filing fees.
- Interest from funding to the first payment date.
- Life insurance on the borrower, if the lender requires it (ongoing premium).

## Advisors
- Buyer's lawyer: purchase agreement, ancillary documents, lease review, entity formation.
- CPA or diligence provider: quality of earnings or financial review, tax structure, purchase price allocation advice.
- Insurance broker: usually paid through premiums.
- Other specialists: equipment inspection, IT or software review, industry consultant.

## Government and filings
- Entity formation and registered agent fees.
- License and permit applications or transfer fees.
- Sales tax and payroll registrations.
- Transfer taxes, where the state charges them on business assets or real estate.
- Title insurance and recording fees, if real estate transfers.

## Operating cash at closing
- Working-capital reserve or cash left in the business for the first payroll and supplier payments.
- Security deposit on a new lease, or reimbursement of the seller's deposit.
- Utility and vendor deposits for new accounts.
- Prepaid insurance premiums (first installment or full year).
- Payroll provider setup.

## Adjustments on the closing statement
- Prorations: rent, utilities, property taxes, prepaid contracts.
- Inventory count adjustment, if inventory is priced separately.
- Customer deposits credited to the buyer.
- Accrued payroll and vacation, if assumed.
- Working-capital estimate at closing, if the agreement uses one.

## Often forgotten
- Escrow agent fee, if a third party holds an escrow.
- Rekeying locks, new signage, updated vehicle registrations.
- Software license transfers or new subscriptions in the buyer's name.
- Bank wire fees.
