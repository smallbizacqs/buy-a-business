# Industry selection worksheet

Goal: two to four industries the user can explain, finance and operate. This worksheet produces evidence for each; it does not rank industries and gives no "best industries" list.

## For each candidate industry, answer in writing

| Question | Why it matters | Where to look |
|---|---|---|
| What's my unfair advantage here (work history, customers, network, skills)? | Brokers, owners and lenders all ask. A clear answer makes the buyer more credible. | The user |
| Who are the customers, and why do they buy again? | Repeat or contracted demand steadies cash flow. | Trade association reports, owner interviews, public company filings in the sector |
| What does the work need: licenses, certifications, bonding? | A license only the owner holds can block a sale or a loan. | State licensing board sites; ask a lawyer about the specific situation |
| How capital-heavy is it? | Equipment and vehicles compete with debt for cash. | Owner interviews, equipment dealers, the CIM's depreciation schedule |
| Can I hire the people it needs here? | Labor shortages cap growth and raise wages. | Local job postings, trade schools, government labor statistics |
| How exposed is it to rates, tariffs, housing starts, government budgets? | Macro swings move earnings. | Public data; later `small-business-risk-assessment` |
| Could software or AI do much of the work or let customers do it themselves? | Can compress prices or volume. | Later `ai-disruption-risk-check` |
| Are larger buyers consolidating it? | More competition for deals now; possibly an exit buyer later. | Trade press, news searches |
| Would I enjoy the daily work and the people? | Owner-operators live in it. | The user; a day shadowing an owner |

## Quick ways to learn an industry before committing

- Read three to five listings and one CIM in the industry to see how businesses describe earnings and risks.
- Talk to two owners who are **not** selling (via trade groups or local networks) about what they wish they'd known.
- Talk to a lender who finances the industry about what it looks for.
- Read the trade association's annual survey if one exists.

Write what the user learned and the decision into `decisions.md`.

## Keep it reversible

Industries are a hypothesis. Recommend a review date (for example, after the first 60 days of search or the first 50 listings reviewed) and note in `decisions.md` what evidence would add or drop an industry.
