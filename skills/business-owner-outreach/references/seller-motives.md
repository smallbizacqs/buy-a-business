# Why the owner is selling, and what they want besides price

## Common reasons owners give, and what to confirm

| Stated reason | What to confirm, gently, over several conversations |
|---|---|
| Retirement | Their age and plan; whether they have tried to sell before; whether results have been flat or slipping as they wind down. |
| Health | How long it has affected the business; who has been running things in the meantime. |
| Burnout, "tired" | Whether the business needs more hours than it can sustain; whether a manager could do the job. |
| Partner dispute or divorce | Who has authority to sell; whether all owners agree; timing pressure. |
| Moving | Customer relationships tied to the owner personally. |
| "Want to do something else" | Whether something changed in the market, a big customer, a competitor, a license, a lease. |
| "Not really selling, just curious" | What price would change their mind; that they may be testing value. |

The stated reason is often true and incomplete. Ask the same question more than once, at different
meetings, in different words, and compare answers. Then check the numbers (`sde-and-add-backs-analysis`)
and the risk picture (`small-business-risk-assessment`) against the story.

## Questions that get at the real reason

- "What made now the right time to think about this?"
- "If you didn't sell, what would the next five years look like?"
- "What would you be doing a year after the sale?"
- "Has anything changed recently with customers, staff or competitors?"
- "Who else knows you're thinking about this?" (family, employees, landlord)
- "Have you talked to anyone else about buying it? What happened?"
- "What worries you most about selling?"

## What owners often care about besides price

Ask; do not assume. Write down their answers as "owner said".

- **Employees:** that key people keep their jobs and are treated well.
- **Customers:** that long-time customers keep getting the same service.
- **Legacy and name:** that the business keeps its name, stays local, stays independent.
- **Their role after sale:** a transition period, a consulting role, a clean exit, or a defined
  number of weeks.
- **Certainty and speed:** a buyer who will actually close, with financing lined up.
- **Confidentiality:** that staff, customers and competitors don't hear early.
- **Simplicity:** few surprises, reasonable diligence, a buyer who returns calls.
- **Terms:** how much cash at closing vs a seller note or earnout; tax treatment (a question for their
  CPA and the user's CPA, never answered in this skill).
- **Respect:** being treated as the expert on their business.

## Being the buyer an owner chooses without the highest price

Levers that do not cost the user price, but each one must be true before it is said:

- Show up, follow up when promised, and reply fast.
- Show the financing is real (lender conversation started, equity identified).
- Explain plainly what will stay the same and what the user does not yet know.
- Respect their timeline; give them a plan for the transition they want.
- Keep confidentiality visibly (no visits to staff or customers without permission).
- Keep the offer simple and avoid re-trading price later without a material reason.

Never suggest the user promise to keep every employee, the name or the location unless the user has
decided that. Promises made to win a deal become disputes later.
