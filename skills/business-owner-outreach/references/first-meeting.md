# The first meeting with an owner

A first meeting is usually in person at the business, one to three hours, sometimes over a meal.
Some buyers run a long meeting covering the business's past, present and future before any offer;
others keep it short and come back several times. Either works; the structure below fits both.

## What to bring

- A notebook (writing by hand reads as listening; ask before recording anything, and follow the
  user's local recording-consent rules).
- A one-page summary of who the user is, if the owner might want something to keep.
- Proof the user can close, ready but not offered unprompted (a lender letter or proof of funds, see
  `business-broker-relations` references).
- No NDA unless the owner asked for one; if financials will be discussed, see `screen-business-cim`.

## Agenda: past, present, future

### 1. Past (about 30% of the time)

- How did the business start? What were the hard years, and what got it through?
- What changed in the last five years: customers, services, prices, staff, competitors?
- Any big events: a lost customer, a lawsuit, a move, a partner leaving, COVID-era changes, PPP or
  other relief loans?

### 2. Present (about 40%)

- Walk-through: ask to see the shop, trucks, office, systems. Who does what?
- Customers: what kinds, how they find the business, how many come back, any that are very large.
- People: who the key employees are, how long they have stayed, who knows what only the owner knows.
- Owner's week: what the owner personally does (sales, estimating, licenses held, key relationships).
  This is the start of the owner-dependence picture for `small-business-risk-assessment`.
- Money in broad strokes only: is the business busier or quieter than a few years ago? How are
  prices set? Do not ask for a P&L on the tour unless the owner offers.
- Licenses, certifications and permits the business needs, and whose name they are in.
- Landlord and lease: owned or rented, and how long is left.

### 3. Future (about 30%)

- What would the owner do next? When?
- What would they want for the employees, the customers, the name, the building?
- How involved would they like to be after a sale, and for how long?
- What would a good outcome look like for them, in their words?
- What has stopped them from selling so far?

## What to watch for

- Who the employees go to with questions during the visit.
- Whether the owner answers questions about numbers with specifics or deflects.
- Equipment condition, clutter, how the phone is answered.
- Signs of a second agenda: a family member who works there and expects a role, a co-owner who is
  not in the room.

## Ending the meeting

- Summarize what you heard in two or three sentences. Owners notice when they've been listened to.
- Propose the next step plainly: "Would you be comfortable sharing the last three years of tax
  returns and this year's P&L so I can understand the numbers? I'm happy to sign an NDA first."
- Agree the date of the next contact.

## After the meeting

- Write the notes the same day in `deals/<slug>/notes.md` (owner said / I observed / I infer / open
  questions).
- Draft a thank-you note for the user to approve.
- List what's needed before an offer and hand off to `screen-business-cim`.
