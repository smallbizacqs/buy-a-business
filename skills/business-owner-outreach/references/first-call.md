# The first call with an owner

Goal of a first call: earn a second, longer conversation. Not a price, not financials, not a
commitment. Most first calls run 10 to 30 minutes. The owner should talk most of the time.

## Before the call (10 minutes)

- Read everything already in `deals/<slug>/`: the letter or email that started contact, the website,
  public reviews, how long the business has existed, how many locations or trucks are visible.
- Write down the one sentence that explains why this business fits the user (from `buy-box.yaml`).
- Decide the one ask for the end of the call (usually: "Could I come see the business and buy you
  lunch? Would the week of the 8th work?").
- Have the calendar open so a date can be agreed on the call.

## Opening

Ask permission before anything else. Owners are busy and often on a job site.

> "Hi, is this Jane? This is Sam Lee. I sent you a letter about the shop last week. Do you have a
> couple of minutes, or is there a better time to call back?"

If it is a bad time, agree a specific callback time and end the call.

## The 30-second introduction

Four parts, in the user's own words. Write them out once; do not read them aloud word for word.

1. **Who I am:** background in one sentence, no résumé.
2. **Why this kind of business:** a real reason (grew up around it, worked in a related field, likes
   the customers it serves, wants a business with steady repeat work).
3. **What I'd do:** "I'd run it myself, day to day" (or the truth if the user plans a manager).
   Owners worry about who will look after their customers and staff.
4. **How I'd pay:** "I'd buy it with my own savings and a bank loan; I'm not a big fund." Keep it
   accurate to `profile.yaml`. Do not overstate available funds.

Then stop and hand the conversation back: "But I'd much rather hear about you. How did you get
started?"

## Open questions (pick three to five)

- How did you start (or come to own) the business?
- What does a normal week look like for you?
- What are you proudest of?
- Who are your customers, broadly? How do they find you?
- How big is the team, and how long have the key people been with you?
- Have you thought about what comes next for you, and for the business?
- If you did step back one day, what would matter most to you about how it's handled?
- Has anyone approached you about buying it before? How did that go?

Listen for: timeline words ("in a few years", "my wife wants me to"), health, a partner who wants
out, fatigue, a family member who was supposed to take over and isn't, a big customer change. Note
them; do not probe hard on a first call.

## Things not to do on a first call

- Do not name a price, a multiple or a valuation method.
- Do not ask for tax returns or a P&L yet (that comes after trust and usually after an NDA).
- Do not criticize how the business is run or promise to "grow it 3x".
- Do not say "I'm looking for a deal" or "acquisition target". Say "the business" and "your company".
- Do not promise anything about staff, location, the owner's role or timing.

## Deflections when the owner asks about price

- "I honestly don't know yet. I'd need to understand the business first, and I'd want it to be fair
  to you. Can I come and see it?"
- "Every business is different. Once I understand how it runs, I'll be straight with you about how I
  think about value."
- If pressed for a range: "I'd rather not guess and have it be wrong in either direction. What number
  would make this worth considering for you?" (Write down whatever they say as "owner said".)

## Closing the call

- Make the one ask, with a specific date range.
- Confirm how they prefer to be reached (phone, text, email) and when.
- Thank them for their time. Send nothing until the user approves a draft follow-up.

## Draft follow-up after the call (for the user to send)

> Jane, thank you for the time on the phone today. I enjoyed hearing how you started the shop in
> 1998. As we discussed, I'd like to visit and take you to lunch; Tuesday the 8th or Thursday the
> 10th both work for me. Whatever you share stays between us.
> Sam

Adjust: never claim confidentiality terms the user has not agreed to formally; "stays between us" is
a personal promise the user must be willing to keep.
