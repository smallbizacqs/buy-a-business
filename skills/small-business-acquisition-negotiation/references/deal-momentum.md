# Keeping a deal from stalling

Small deals die of delay as often as of findings. Sellers get tired of requests, employees start to suspect, the lender's file goes stale, exclusivity runs out. Momentum is something the buyer manages.

## The weekly routine

1. **One open-items list** for the whole deal: diligence requests (from `diligence-tracker.csv`), lender conditions (`lender.md`), legal items, landlord, licenses. Each item: owner, due date, status.
2. **A standing weekly call** with the seller (and broker if there is one), 30 minutes, same time, walking the list.
3. **A short written summary** after each call: what came in, what's late, what's next, the date we're aiming at. Draft it for the user to send.
4. **Calendar counted back from exclusivity end and target closing**: the diligence skills and `sba-lender-package` build these. Flag any slippage the week it happens.

## Why deals stall, and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | Response (drafted for the user) |
|---|---|---|
| Documents slow to arrive | Seller overwhelmed; bookkeeper or CPA slow; records don't exist | Smaller, prioritized requests; offer to collect from the bookkeeper directly with the seller's OK; ask what doesn't exist |
| Seller goes quiet | Seller's remorse, family doubts, another buyer, fear about employees | Direct, kind question about how they're feeling; restate what stays the same for them and staff |
| Broker pushes speed, seller doesn't respond | Misaligned broker and seller | Ask for a meeting with the seller |
| Lender quiet | File incomplete; underwriter queue | Ask the lender for the outstanding list and a date (see `sba-lender-package`) |
| Landlord slow | Not a priority for them | Seller makes the introduction; buyer's lawyer handles the paper |
| Buyer slow | Paid work not started; advisors not hired | Hire now (`choose-acquisition-advisors`); start paid work at the phase-1 gate |
| New findings every week | Scope creep, or real problems | Separate must-know from nice-to-know; take real problems to `acquisition-go-no-go-review` |

## Exclusivity running out

- Ask for an extension in writing well before the end, with a reason and a new date tied to the lender's timeline.
- Offer something in exchange if needed: a firmer timeline, a modest refundable deposit, proof of lender approval progress.
- Never let it lapse silently; the seller may take that as a signal.

## Signs the deal should not be saved

- Repeated refusals to provide basic records (bank statements, tax returns).
- The seller's story about earnings keeps changing.
- The seller won't allow customer or employee contact at any point before closing in a business that depends on them.

Those go to `acquisition-go-no-go-review`.
