# Professionalize toolkit

## A. Owner-dependence checklist

Score each line 0 (nobody but the owner), 1 (someone can, with the owner's help) or 2 (runs without the owner). Total out of 40. The score matters less than the reasons; list the five lowest lines.

| Area | Question |
|---|---|
| Customers | 1. Do the top 10 customers deal day to day with someone other than the owner? |
| | 2. Would the top customers stay if the owner left for three months? |
| Sales | 3. Does someone other than the owner produce most quotes or sales? |
| | 4. Are prices set from a written price list or method, not the owner's judgment each time? |
| Operations | 5. Can the team schedule and deliver a normal week without the owner? |
| | 6. Are quality problems caught by someone other than the owner? |
| | 7. Are the core processes written down? |
| People | 8. Is there at least one manager or lead who hires, schedules and handles problems? |
| | 9. Do employees bring routine questions to someone other than the owner? |
| | 10. Would a key employee's departure be survivable within a month? |
| Money | 11. Does someone other than the owner invoice, collect and pay bills, with owner review? |
| | 12. Is there a monthly close with financial statements by a set date? |
| | 13. Is there a cash forecast someone other than the owner maintains? |
| Knowledge | 14. Are supplier terms, customer special terms and equipment quirks written down? |
| | 15. Are logins and admin rights held by the company, not the owner's personal accounts? |
| Licenses | 16. Is every required license held by the company or by more than one person? |
| Planning | 17. Is there a written annual budget and plan? |
| | 18. Does the team know the quarterly priorities? |
| Records | 19. Are customer and job records in one system rather than the owner's head or phone? |
| | 20. Could a buyer or lender get three years of clean financials in a week? |

## B. Core processes and SOP template

Typical core processes (pick the ones that exist): lead intake; quoting and pricing; scheduling and dispatch; delivering the service or making the product; quality check and callbacks; invoicing and collections; purchasing and inventory; hiring and onboarding; payroll; month-end close; safety; customer complaints; equipment maintenance; license and insurance renewals.

Rank each by: how often it runs (daily scores higher), cost of a mistake, and whether only the owner can do it. Document the top three first.

### SOP template (one page if possible)

```markdown
# SOP: <process name>   (owner: <role>; last reviewed YYYY-MM-DD)
Purpose: why this matters, in one line.
Trigger: what starts it (a call, a date, an order).
Steps:
1. …
2. …
Quality check: how we know it was done right, and who checks.
Tools and where to find them: software, templates, forms.
When something goes wrong: who to call, what to do.
```

Ways to draft quickly: record the person doing the job explaining it out loud, transcribe, then edit into steps; or film a screen recording for software tasks. The person who does the work reviews the SOP; the owner approves. Review each SOP at least yearly or when the process changes.

## C. Delegation ladder

Move a task up one rung at a time, with a date for each move.

1. **Watch me:** the person observes the owner do it.
2. **Do it, I check before:** they do it; the owner approves before it goes out.
3. **Do it, I check after:** they do it and it goes out; the owner reviews a sample.
4. **Do it, report results:** they own it; results show on the scorecard.
5. **Own it:** they own it and improve it; the owner hears only about exceptions.

Common traps: skipping straight to rung 5 and taking it back after the first mistake; delegating a task without the authority to do it; not deciding what "good" looks like before handing off.

## D. Systems and records a lender or buyer will ask for

- [ ] Accounting kept on an accrual basis with a monthly close (method choice is the CPA's call).
- [ ] Financial statements reconciled to bank statements and tax returns.
- [ ] Revenue by customer and by service line, for three years.
- [ ] Job costing or gross margin by job or product line where it applies.
- [ ] Customer contracts or service agreements in writing where useful; assignable terms reviewed by a lawyer.
- [ ] Employee files, job descriptions, pay history, classification reviewed.
- [ ] Asset register: equipment, vehicles, age, condition, replacement plan.
- [ ] Licenses, permits and insurance policies with renewal dates.
- [ ] Logins and domains in the company's name.
- [ ] Lease, supplier and key contracts filed and summarized.
- [ ] Organizational chart and SOP library.

## E. Value levers

Pick at most three at a time. Write the baseline from the business's own numbers before changing anything.

| Lever | What it changes | Measure |
|---|---|---|
| Pricing discipline | Price increases where costs rose or the business is under market; minimum charges; charging for extras | Average ticket, gross margin |
| Recurring revenue | Maintenance agreements, memberships, service contracts | Share of revenue that's recurring, renewal rate |
| Customer concentration | Win more mid-sized customers so no one customer dominates | Top customer and top five share |
| Gross margin | Job costing, purchasing, reducing rework, better scheduling | Gross margin by line |
| Sales process | Lead tracking, follow-up, close rate, someone other than the owner selling | Leads, close rate, sales by person |
| Second-tier management | A manager or leads who run operations | Owner-dependence score, owner hours |
| Cash conversion | Faster invoicing, deposits, collections | Days to collect, AR over 60 days |
| Capital discipline | Maintenance plan, buy vs lease decisions | Repair costs, downtime |
| Records and reporting | Clean, timely financials | Days to close the month |

How these tend to show up: higher or steadier earnings, and lower perceived risk (less owner dependence, less concentration, more recurring revenue). What a buyer would actually pay for that is a market question for `small-business-valuation`; don't promise a multiple.
