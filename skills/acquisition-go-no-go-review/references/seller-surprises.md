# Seller problems that commonly surface in diligence

Use as a check that each area has actually been looked at; for each, find the tracker row. If there's no row, it hasn't been checked.

## Money

- Revenue reported to buyers differs from tax returns.
- Add-backs that are really ongoing costs (a family member who works, deferred repairs).
- Cash revenue the seller says isn't on the books.
- A strong final few months before sale.
- Merchant cash advances or loans not on the debt schedule.
- Past-due payroll, sales or property taxes.
- Customer deposits spent on operations (work owed with no cash to fund it).

## Customers and revenue

- One customer much larger than disclosed, or about to leave.
- Contracts expiring soon, or customers who only stay because of the owner.
- Price increases that haven't stuck, or discounts not on invoices.

## People

- Key employees planning to leave, or not yet told.
- Contractors who look like employees.
- The owner works far more hours, or does far more of the skilled work, than described.
- A licensed person the business depends on is the owner or a single employee.

## Legal and operations

- The lease ends soon, or the landlord wants changes.
- Licenses or permits in the owner's personal name, expired, or with open violations.
- Pending or threatened claims not disclosed.
- Domains, phone numbers, review profiles in personal names.
- Equipment older or in worse shape than represented.
- Environmental history at the site.

## Process signals

- Documents arrive late, edited or incomplete.
- The seller resists bank statements, transcripts or talking to their CPA.
- The broker answers questions the seller should answer.
- Story changes when numbers are questioned.

A surprise that the seller discloses promptly and explains with documents is different from one the buyer digs out. Record which it was.
