# Sorting diligence findings

Each finding goes into one category, with the evidence (tracker id and document) and a quantified effect where possible.

| Category | Meaning | Typical response |
|---|---|---|
| Deal-killer | Changes what the business is, or can't be fixed by money or terms | Walk, unless the user records why not |
| Reprice | A measurable shortfall in earnings or assets versus the LOI basis | Price change at the LOI multiple (recurring) or dollar for dollar (one-time); see `small-business-acquisition-negotiation` |
| Structure fix | A risk that may or may not happen | Holdback, escrow, specific indemnity, seller fixes before closing, longer transition, seller note (lawyer drafts) |
| Accept | Known, priced or manageable | Note it in the 100-day plan |
| Unknown | Not answered yet | Get the answer, extend exclusivity, or decide without it knowingly |

## Examples

| Finding | Usually | Why | Quantify as |
|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue on P&L doesn't tie to deposits or tax returns, no credible explanation | Deal-killer | The earnings basis isn't real | — |
| Seller won't provide bank statements or authorize tax transcripts | Deal-killer | Can't verify; lenders verify seller data against IRS records (source `sba-sop-50-10-8-1-seller-tax-verification`) | — |
| A license the business needs can't be transferred or reissued in time | Deal-killer, or delay closing | Can't operate | — |
| Landlord won't consent and location is essential | Deal-killer | Can't operate there | — |
| Largest customer says they'll put the work out to bid | Reprice or structure; deal-killer if above the walk line | Revenue at risk | Lost gross profit from that customer |
| Add-backs don't hold up; SDE lower | Reprice | Earnings lower than the basis | Recurring SDE change × LOI multiple |
| Deferred maintenance: a truck fleet or roof needs replacing | Reprice | Cash the buyer must spend | One-time cost |
| Unpaid sales or payroll taxes | Structure fix (seller pays at closing) | Liability, possibly following the business (counsel) | One-time cost |
| Workers likely misclassified | Structure fix and accept going-forward cost | Back liability (counsel), higher labor cost after | Recurring cost of reclassifying + one-time exposure estimate from counsel |
| Working capital below the peg | Reprice at closing via true-up | Covered by the peg mechanism | Shortfall |
| Owner does all estimating and sales | Structure fix (longer transition, consulting) and accept cost of a hire | Owner dependence | Recurring cost of the replacement hire |
| Pending lawsuit, insured | Structure fix (indemnity, escrow) | May or may not cost money | Counsel's estimate |
| Old software, messy records | Accept | Fixable after closing | Transition time and cost |

## Pattern check

One finding may be priced. Several findings that all point the same way (numbers keep moving, documents slow, explanations change) say something about the seller and the records as a whole. Name the pattern in the memo.
