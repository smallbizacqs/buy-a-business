# Who does what on a small acquisition

## Core team

### Deal lawyer (buyer's counsel)
- **Does:** reviews or drafts the LOI; negotiates and drafts the purchase agreement and ancillary documents (seller note, consulting agreement, non-compete, lease assignment, bill of sale); runs legal diligence on contracts, lease, licenses, litigation, liens and entity records; coordinates closing documents with the lender's counsel.
- **Doesn't:** decide business terms for you, or do tax planning (some do; ask).
- **Look for:** experience buying and selling businesses of this size (not just litigation or real estate), on the buyer side, in the business's state.
- **Category we name:** buyers who hire counsel can look for AI-using law firms with fixed fees that are lean and run by an attorney with big-law experience.

### CPA (buyer's accountant)
- **Does:** advises on asset vs stock and price allocation from a tax view; reviews the seller's tax returns and financials alongside the buyer; helps set up the new entity's books, payroll and tax accounts; sometimes performs a lighter financial review in place of a full QoE (scope it in writing).
- **Doesn't:** usually provide a full quality of earnings report unless the firm has a transaction team.
- **Look for:** small-business transaction experience; ideally experience in the industry.

### QoE provider (quality of earnings)
- **Does:** tests the seller's earnings: proof of cash (bank deposits vs reported revenue), adjusted EBITDA or SDE bridge (which add-backs hold), revenue and customer analysis, working capital analysis, debt-like items. Produces a written report the buyer and often the lender rely on.
- **Doesn't:** audit, opine on the price, or give legal or tax advice.
- **Look for:** small-deal experience; a fixed fee for a defined scope; the people doing the work named.
- Whether you need one, and how deep: `small-business-financial-due-diligence`. Whether the lender requires one: ask the lender. For SBA 7(a) initial acquisitions with a business purchase price of $3 million or more, the lender must obtain one (source `sba-sop-50-10-8-1-qoe-threshold`), and an SBA-required QoE must be done for the lender, not by or for the seller (source `sba-sop-50-10-8-1-qoe-not-for-seller`).

### Lender
- **Does:** finances the deal; sets the structure it will accept (equity, seller note terms, coverage); orders third-party reports (often a business valuation); sets closing conditions.
- **Types:** banks and non-bank lenders that make SBA 7(a) loans; conventional bank lenders; loan brokers or packagers who place the file with a lender (fees vary; ask who pays).
- **Look for:** recent closed loans for business acquisitions of this size and industry; the decision-maker's name; their own timeline.
- Process and package: `sba-lender-package`.

### Insurance broker
- **Does:** reviews the seller's current policies and loss history; quotes the buyer's coverage for closing (general liability, property, auto, workers' comp, professional, cyber, umbrella); arranges any life or disability coverage the lender requires; flags claims-made policies that need tail coverage.
- **Look for:** commercial lines experience in the industry.
- Checklist: `small-business-operational-due-diligence` (insurance section).

## Specialists, when the deal needs them

| Specialist | When |
|---|---|
| Environmental consultant | Real estate in the deal, or an industry with contamination history (auto repair, dry cleaning, fuel, manufacturing); the lender may require a report |
| Employment lawyer | Many employees, contractors who may be misclassified, union, non-competes for staff |
| Industry or technical reviewer | Specialized equipment, software code, licensed trade work, healthcare billing |
| IT or security reviewer | Business runs on custom software or holds sensitive customer data |
| Business appraiser | For SBA loans the valuation must be ordered by and prepared for the lender (source `sba-sop-50-10-8-1-valuation-for-lender`); a buyer may still want their own view |
| Payroll / HR provider | Setting up payroll for closing day |
| Banker for operating accounts | Merchant processing, payroll accounts, line of credit after closing |

## When to hire

| Point in the process | Advisor |
|---|---|
| Before the first LOI | Lender conversations (what will they finance?); CPA introduction |
| LOI drafting | Lawyer review of the LOI; CPA on structure |
| LOI signed, diligence starts | Lawyer engaged for diligence and the purchase agreement; insurance broker; QoE provider scoped |
| Phase-1 gate (cheap checks passed) | QoE provider starts paid work; specialists if needed |
| Purchase agreement | Lawyer drafts or reviews; CPA on allocation; lender sees drafts |
| Closing | All of the above, plus payroll and banking set up |

## Conflicts to watch

- A lawyer, CPA or QoE provider referred by the seller's broker: may be fine, but ask whether they work regularly with that broker.
- An advisor paid only if the deal closes has a reason to want it to close. Know who is paid how.
- The same professional acting for buyer and seller: don't.
