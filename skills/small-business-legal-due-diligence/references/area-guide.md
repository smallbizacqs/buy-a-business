# Legal diligence by area: what to collect and what to flag

For each area: why it matters, what to extract, and what to put in front of the lawyer. "Flag" means a factual line plus a question, never a conclusion.

## Entity and authority

- **Why:** The person signing must have the right to sell; in a stock purchase, the buyer inherits the entity's history.
- **Extract:** owners and percentages; managers or officers; any other owner (spouse, former partner, investor); good standing status.
- **Flag:** owners not on the seller side of the LOI; a former partner with a buyout right; lapsed good standing.

## Contracts and assignment

- **Why:** In an asset purchase each contract must move to the buyer; in a stock purchase, change-of-control terms may still apply. Key customers often have no written contract at all, which is its own risk.
- **Extract** (into the contract inventory): counterparty, type, start and end dates, renewal mechanics, revenue or spend, and the section numbers that address assignment, change of control, termination for convenience, exclusivity, most-favored pricing, minimum purchases, personal guarantees.
- **Flag:** any of those sections in a top-10 customer or critical supplier contract; contracts in the owner's personal name; verbal arrangements the business depends on.
- **Counsel decides:** whether consent is needed, how to ask, and whether to make it a closing condition. Approaches to customers and suppliers for consent are made at an agreed time, usually with the seller.

## Lease and landlord

- **Why:** Losing the location can end the business; lenders commonly need the lease to run with the loan. For 7(a) loans the lender must get a copy of the lease, and the lease term including the borrower's renewal options should equal or exceed the loan term (source `sba-sop-50-10-8-1-lease-term`, SOP 50 10 8.1; check its `review_by` date).
- **Extract:** landlord, premises, start and end dates, renewal options and how they're exercised, rent and increases, security deposit, who guarantees it, the sections addressing assignment, subletting, change of control, use, relocation, and default.
- **Flag:** a personal guarantee by the seller (landlord may want one from the buyer); remaining term plus renewals shorter than the loan; landlord is the seller or a relative (rent at market?); defaults or arrears.
- **Process:** the seller usually introduces the buyer to the landlord once diligence has passed the phase-1 gate. The lawyer requests consent or negotiates a new lease. An estoppel letter from the landlord confirming the lease is in effect and not in default is a common request; counsel decides.

## Licenses and permits

- **Why:** Which licenses a business needs depends on its activities and location, at federal, state, county and city level (sources `sba-licenses-permits`, `sba-licenses-permits-federal`). Some move with the business, some must be reissued to the new owner, some belong to a person.
- **Extract** (into the license inventory): license, issuer, holder (entity or person), number, expiry, renewal status.
- **Flag:** licenses held by the owner personally; anything the issuer must reissue (record the issuer's published process and timeline as a question, don't contact the issuer without the user's OK); expired or soon-expiring items; open violations.
- In an SBA change of ownership the lender must take as collateral all assets conveyed, including transferable licenses such as a liquor license (source `sba-sop-50-10-8-1-transferable-licenses`). Tell the lender about any such license early.

## Litigation, liens, taxes

- **Why:** Hidden liabilities follow the entity in a stock purchase and some follow the assets in an asset purchase (counsel advises on which).
- **Extract:** seller's litigation schedule vs public court searches; UCC filings and tax liens vs the debt schedule; tax notices.
- **Flag:** anything on a public search that isn't on the seller's schedule; liens from lenders not on the debt schedule (including merchant cash advance providers); payroll or sales tax notices.

## IP, domains, software, data

- **Why:** The brand, website, phone numbers and customer data are often the business's real assets, and are often registered to the owner personally or to a web developer.
- **Extract:** domain registrar and registrant of record; hosting; social accounts and their admin; Google and review profiles; phone numbers and carrier; trademarks; key software licenses; custom code authorship.
- **Flag:** anything in a personal name or a vendor's name; custom code written by contractors without a written assignment; customer data held in a personal account; prior data breaches.

## Environmental

- **Why:** Contamination liability can attach to an owner or operator of property, and some operations carry risk even on leased property.
- **When it comes up:** real estate in the deal; past or present uses such as fuel storage, auto repair, dry cleaning, manufacturing, printing, chemical handling, underground tanks.
- **Process:** ask the lender whether it requires an environmental report and of what type; an environmental consultant scopes it. Record the lender's answer in `lender.md` and the tracker.
- **Flag:** storage tanks, permits for hazardous materials, prior reports with findings, neighbors with known contamination.

## Regulatory (industry-specific)

Add industry items to the request list. Examples of where to look, as prompts for the user and counsel:

| Industry | Regulators and records to ask about |
|---|---|
| Home services and trades | State contractor and trade licenses, local permits, licensing board complaints, vehicle and DOT rules if heavy vehicles |
| Healthcare, dental, veterinary | Professional licenses, facility licenses, payer contracts and credentialing, privacy compliance, controlled substances registrations |
| Food and restaurant | Health permits and inspections, liquor license, fire permits |
| Childcare and education | State licensing, staff background check records, ratios |
| Transport and logistics | Operating authority, safety ratings, driver files |
| Financial and insurance agencies | Agent licenses, carrier appointments, book transfer terms |
| Government contractors | Set-aside certifications, contract novation, registrations |
| Manufacturing | Environmental permits, safety records, product liability claims |

## Employees (legal side)

- Employee agreements, non-competes and handbook go to counsel. Worker classification, I-9s, WARN questions and payroll mechanics are covered in `small-business-operational-due-diligence`.
