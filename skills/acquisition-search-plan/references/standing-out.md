# Competition and standing out

Whether the market for small businesses is "crowded" depends on industry, size and place, and changes over time. Don't assert either way. Help the user see their own evidence and what they can control.

## Evidence the user can gather

- How quickly listings in their box go "under contract" or disappear (save copies with dates).
- What brokers say about the number of NDAs and offers on recent deals (ask; record as the broker's view).
- How often owners in outreach mention other buyers contacting them.
- Whether larger acquirers are buying in the industry locally (news, trade press).

## What buyers control

| Lever | What it looks like |
|---|---|
| Specificity | A narrow, credible buy box and a real reason for the industry and place |
| Readiness | Proof of funds, a lender conversation done, advisors identified (`business-broker-relations`) |
| Speed | Quick, clear responses to CIMs; a decision in days |
| Certainty | Realistic offers the buyer can close; few retrades (re-cutting the price after LOI) |
| What the seller cares about besides price | Employees, customers, name, the owner's role after the sale (`business-owner-outreach`) |
| Channel | Direct outreach and referral networks reach owners before a listing exists (`off-market-deal-sourcing`) |
| Patience | Staying in touch with owners not ready yet |

Where practitioners disagree about how crowded the market is, show the views from the leaf answer (`competition-crowded`) with their sources rather than picking one.
