# When the asking price is above the range

Most gaps come from one of four places. Find which before responding.

| Source of the gap | How to spot it | What to ask |
|---|---|---|
| Unsupported add-backs | Asking multiple looks normal on claimed SDE but high on supported SDE | "Can you share documents for these add-backs?" (list them) |
| A high multiple | Supported SDE is right, but the multiple is above the comparables | "Which recent sales is the price based on?" |
| Things in the price you aren't counting | Real estate, excess inventory, working capital, new equipment included | "What exactly is included at this price?" |
| The seller's number | Retirement need, an old valuation, a friend's sale | Listen: "What would you do with the proceeds?" (owner conversations: `business-owner-outreach`) |

## Options, with trade-offs (present all; the user picks)

1. **Show the gap with evidence.** Share the earnings bridge and comparables, framed as "here's what we can support and why". Works best when the broker is reasonable and the gap is mostly add-backs. Risk: the seller digs in.
2. **Offer the supported price.** A clean offer at the range, with reasons (`loi-for-small-business-acquisition`). Risk: rejected; often reopened later if the business doesn't sell.
3. **Bridge with structure.** Part of the price in a seller note, a consulting or transition agreement, or contingent terms where the deal's financing allows them (`small-business-deal-structure`, `seller-note-structuring`; check the current SBA rules with `sba-sop-rule-check` in an SBA deal). Gives the seller their headline number while the buyer pays the gap only from future cash. Risk: more debt, more coverage strain (rerun `sba-dscr-and-affordability`).
4. **Wait.** Stay in touch; listings that don't sell often come back with a lower price or a more motivated seller. Risk: someone else buys it.
5. **Pass.** Record the reason in `pipeline.csv` (`stage` = `passed`, reason in `next_step`) and `decisions.md`, with what would change the answer.

## What not to do

- Don't raise the multiple to make the asking price "fit". Write down the price that works and why.
- Don't count the buyer's planned improvements as a reason to pay the seller for them.
- Don't re-trade later on something known now: raise it before the LOI. Many practitioners say cutting the price after the LOI without a new finding costs the seller's trust.
