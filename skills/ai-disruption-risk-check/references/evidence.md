# Evidence for AI exposure

Ratings rest on evidence, ranked roughly by strength. Record each piece with its type, source and date.

## 1. Customer behavior in the records (strongest)

What customers are already doing, from the seller's data (unverified until tied to bank deposits or invoices).

- Churn or lost accounts by service line over 2–3 years, and the reasons recorded.
- Order or engagement size trends (smaller retainers, fewer hours billed per client).
- New-customer volume by source; a drop in inbound leads from search can mean AI answers are intercepting it.
- Customer requests to lower prices or cut scope, especially when they mention doing it in-house or with software.

**Request:** revenue by service and by customer for 3 years plus TTM; lost-customer list with reasons; hours billed per client (for hourly businesses); web traffic and lead source reports (for online businesses).

## 2. Price trend

- The business's own price list or average price per job/hour over time.
- Whether price increases stuck.
- Bids lost on price, and by how much.

Falling prices for the same work, while costs rise, is a commoditization signal. Rising prices that stick is evidence against it.

## 3. Competitor offerings

The user's research, or a web search the agent actually ran with the user's OK. Record what was found and where; never claim a search you didn't run.

- Are there new competitors selling the same outcome at a much lower price using AI or software?
- Have established competitors added AI-assisted tiers or cut prices?
- Are the business's customers being sold self-serve tools for the same job?

Note: marketing claims by competitors are weak evidence. Prices and actual customer wins are stronger.

## 4. A test of current tools

A quick test of whether a general-purpose AI tool can do a representative task to the standard customers pay for. It's useful because it's concrete, and weak because one test isn't a market.

How to run one:
1. Pick a representative task from the inventory (a monthly report, a first-draft proposal, an answer to a typical customer question). Use a sample with no confidential or personal data; never paste the seller's documents or customer data into an outside tool.
2. Write down the input, the tool category used (not a product pick), and the date.
3. Compare the output to what the business delivers: quality, accuracy, effort to fix.
4. Label it: **"test the agent ran in this session"**, **"test the user ran (as reported)"** or **"not tested"**. If you can't run a test in this environment, say so and give the user the steps.

## 5. Owner and staff accounts

What the owner says about AI in the business (have they adopted any, lost any work to it). Useful context; weak evidence, because the seller wants a good price. Mark as "owner said, unverified".

## 6. General commentary (weakest)

Articles, forecasts and opinions about AI and industries. Use only to generate questions to check, never as the basis of a rating. Don't quote statistics unless they have a source in `data/sources/`.

## Questions to draft for the owner

- Which tasks take the most staff hours? Which of those are reading, writing, data entry or answering routine questions?
- Have any customers reduced scope, asked for discounts or left saying they'd do it in-house or with software?
- Have you seen new low-price competitors in the last two years? How did you respond?
- Do you use any AI or automation now? What changed in hours or cost?
- How do new customers find you, and has that changed?
