# Key terms of a small-business purchase agreement

Plain descriptions of what each part is for and what buyers commonly ask about it. This is background for preparing questions; it is not a reading of any particular contract. Terms differ by state, by structure and by draft. Counsel decides what the user's draft means.

## Asset purchase or stock purchase

- **Asset purchase agreement (APA):** the buyer's company buys listed assets (equipment, inventory, customer lists, trade name, phone number, website, goodwill) and takes on only the liabilities it agrees to. Most small deals are asset purchases.
- **Stock (or membership interest) purchase agreement (SPA):** the buyer buys the owner's shares in the company, and the company keeps its history: contracts, licenses, and liabilities, known and unknown.
- Why it matters for the agreement: in a stock purchase, reps, indemnity and escrow carry more weight because the history comes with the company. Structure and tax effects are settled earlier (`small-business-deal-structure`); the agreement should match.

## The main sections

| Section | What it's for | What buyers often ask counsel |
|---|---|---|
| Parties, recitals, definitions | Who is buying and selling. Definitions decide what many other clauses mean. | Is the buyer my new company (not me personally)? Are key definitions ("Business", "Losses", "Knowledge", "Material Adverse Effect") drawn in a way that narrows my protections? |
| Purchased and excluded assets (APA) | Exactly what transfers. | Is everything I'm paying for listed: phone numbers, domains, social accounts, software licenses, vehicles, permits that can transfer? What's excluded and why? |
| Assumed and excluded liabilities (APA) | Which debts and obligations the buyer takes on. | Are all pre-closing liabilities (taxes, lawsuits, warranty claims, accrued payroll and vacation) left with the seller? Which contracts am I assuming, and from what date? |
| Purchase price and payment | How much, when, in what form. | Does the price and its split (cash at close, seller note, holdback, earnout) match the LOI? When and how is each paid? |
| Working-capital adjustment | Trues up the price if working capital at closing is above or below the agreed target (peg). | Is the peg the LOI number? How is closing working capital measured, by whom, by when, and how are disputes settled? (`working-capital-peg-for-small-business`) |
| Allocation of price | How the price is split across asset classes for tax. | The CPA sets this, not the agreement drafter. Is the allocation agreed with my CPA? |
| Representations and warranties | Seller's statements of fact about the business as of signing and closing. If one is false, it triggers indemnity. | Do the reps cover financial statements, taxes, compliance, employees, contracts, litigation, permits, title to assets, condition of equipment, customers and suppliers, environmental? Which are limited "to the seller's knowledge"? |
| Disclosure schedules | The seller's list of exceptions to the reps. | Does anything in the schedules surprise me against diligence? Anything disclosed here usually can't be claimed later; counsel should read them against the diligence record. |
| Covenants | Promises: how the seller runs the business before closing; what each side does after (non-compete, cooperation, transition). | If signing and closing are the same day, the pre-closing covenants matter less; the post-closing ones matter more. |
| Closing conditions | What must be true before either side has to close: lender funding, landlord consent, key contract consents, license transfers, no material adverse change, delivery of documents. | Is my loan funding a condition? Landlord consent and new lease? Key customer or supplier consents? Do I have a way out if a condition fails? |
| Indemnification | Who pays for losses from breached reps, covenants, or excluded liabilities after closing. | What's the cap (a percentage of price or the full price)? Is there a basket or deductible before claims start? How long do claims survive (general reps vs fundamental and tax reps)? Is fraud carved out? Can I set off claims against the seller note? |
| Escrow or holdback | Part of the price held back to fund indemnity claims. Escrow is held by a third party; a holdback is kept by the buyer. In small deals the seller note often serves this role through a set-off right. | How much, for how long, released how? If set-off against the seller note, does the lender allow it, and does it conflict with any standby agreement? |
| Non-compete and non-solicit | Keeps the seller from competing or poaching staff or customers. | Scope, length, geography. Enforceability depends on the state and the facts; counsel answers that. (`small-business-deal-structure`) |
| Termination | When either side can walk away before closing. | If signing and closing are the same day, this may be short. If there's a gap, what's my exit if financing or consent fails? |
| Dispute resolution, governing law | Court or arbitration, which state's law. | Where would a dispute be heard, and what does that cost me? |

## Ancillary documents (often signed at closing)

Bill of sale; assignment and assumption agreement; assignment of lease or new lease plus landlord estoppel; seller promissory note and, for SBA loans, the lender's standby agreement; security agreement for the seller note; non-compete agreement; transition, consulting or employment agreement for the seller; intellectual property and domain assignments; consents from customers, suppliers or franchisors; resignations (stock deals); payoff letters and lien releases; officer's certificates; the closing statement (funds flow).

## R&W insurance (high level)

A policy the buyer buys that pays for losses from certain breaches of the seller's reps. It can let the seller cap indemnity lower and can shorten negotiation. It is used mostly in larger deals: underwriters set minimum premiums, retentions (the amount the buyer bears first) and diligence expectations that often make it uneconomic in small ones. Known issues found in diligence are typically excluded. If interested, ask counsel and an insurance broker whether it's available at the deal's size and what it would cost against the escrow or holdback it replaces.

## Diligence findings → possible protections

Use this to write questions, not answers. Counsel decides which tool fits.

| Finding | Questions to ask counsel |
|---|---|
| Sales tax or payroll tax not fully filed | Specific indemnity for pre-closing taxes? Escrow sized to the exposure? Tax clearance certificate as a closing condition (where the state offers one)? |
| Pending or threatened lawsuit | Excluded liability? Specific indemnity outside the cap and basket? |
| Worker classification questions | Rep on classification and wage-hour compliance; specific indemnity for pre-closing periods? |
| Key customer contract needs consent to assign | Consent as a closing condition, or price protection if the customer leaves? |
| Lease shorter than the loan, or landlord consent pending | New lease or consent and estoppel as a closing condition? |
| License or permit doesn't transfer | Closing conditioned on the buyer obtaining it? Seller's cooperation covenant? |
| Equipment condition disputed | Rep on condition; inspection before closing? |
| Customer deposits or prepaid work | Credited to the buyer in the working-capital adjustment or at closing? |
| Earnings rest on seller's statements more than records | Financial statements rep, survival period, escrow size, set-off against the seller note? |
| Environmental history | Environmental rep, specific indemnity, report as a lender condition? |
