# The seller's real estate: lease it or buy it

When the seller owns the building the business runs from, the buyer chooses between leasing from the seller and buying the property, sometimes with an option to buy later. The seller may push for a sale to cash out completely.

## Questions that decide it

1. Can the buyer afford both the business and the property, and does the lender's structure support both? Ask the lender.
2. Does the business need this specific location (customer traffic, permits, zoning, special build-out), or could it move?
3. What is the rent in the P&L today, and is it market rent? An owner who charges themselves below-market rent makes earnings look better than they will be under a market lease. Adjust earnings in `evaluation.md` (see `sde-and-add-backs-analysis`).
4. What condition is the building in? Roof, HVAC, parking, accessibility, environmental history.
5. Would owning the property tie up equity the business needs for working capital?

## If leasing: terms to ask for

| Term | Ask for | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Length | An initial term plus renewal options that together cover at least the loan term; for 7(a) loans the lease term including the borrower's renewal options should equal or exceed the loan term (source `sba-sop-50-10-8-1-lease-term`) | Losing the location mid-loan |
| Rent | Market rent, fixed increases stated | Predictable costs; earnings adjusted to it |
| Assignment and subletting | Buyer can assign to a future buyer of the business with reasonable consent | Your own exit |
| Purchase option or first right | Option to buy at a set price or formula, or right to match any offer | Control of the location |
| Repairs and maintenance | Who pays for roof, structure, HVAC, parking | Large surprise costs |
| Personal guarantee | Limited in amount or time, or none | Personal exposure |
| Use clause | Covers the business's current and likely future uses | Growth |
| Landlord's lender | Non-disturbance agreement if the property has a mortgage | Lease survives a foreclosure |

## If buying

- Appraisal and, depending on the property and its past uses, an environmental review (the lender usually decides; see `small-business-legal-due-diligence`).
- Title search, survey, zoning confirmation.
- Financing: the lender may finance the property with the business or separately; ask about terms and down payment for each.
- Ownership: many owners hold the property in a separate entity and lease it to the operating business. That is a lawyer and CPA question.
- Inspection: building condition, capital needs over the next five years.

## Middle paths

- Lease now with an option to buy within a set number of years at a set price.
- Seller-financed purchase of the property on a separate note.
- Buy the property later after the business has a track record under the buyer.

Record the choice and reasoning in `decisions.md` and the terms in the structure section of `loi-draft.md`.
