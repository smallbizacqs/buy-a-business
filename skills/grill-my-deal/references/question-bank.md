# Question bank

Use every group. Drop a question only when the workspace answers it with evidence (a third-party record, a signed document, the user's own decision). Keep it when the only answer is the seller's word, and say so in the "why".

Each entry: the question, the recommended answer (worded so "yes" accepts it), why, and the skill that closes the gap. Adapt the wording to the deal; keep the recommended answer specific. A recommended answer is a sensible default to react to, not advice.

"Late" marks questions that should have been settled before the LOI. If one is still open after signing, say it is late, and what it now costs to settle (time in exclusivity, leverage, money).

## 1. Price and earnings

1. **Which earnings number is the price built on, and who produced it?** Recommended: "Yes: I'll rebuild SDE (or EBITDA) myself from the tax returns and bank statements, and treat the broker's figure as a claim." Why: listing earnings are the seller's case. → `sde-and-add-backs-analysis`
2. **Does every add-back have a document behind it?** Recommended: "Yes: I'll accept an add-back only with an invoice, payroll record or statement, and drop the rest from my price." Why: unsupported add-backs are the most common way earnings are overstated. → `sde-and-add-backs-analysis`
3. **Do deposits in the bank statements tie to reported revenue?** Recommended: "Yes: I'll tie at least 12, ideally 24, months of deposits to revenue before paying for a QoE." Why: it is cheap and catches the biggest problems early. → `verify-seller-financials`
4. **Have you priced the owner's replacement?** Recommended: "Yes: I'll subtract a market salary for every job the owner does that I won't do." Why: if you hire a manager, that pay comes out of the earnings you're buying. → `sde-and-add-backs-analysis`
5. **Is the trend up, flat or down over three years and the last 12 months?** Recommended: "Yes: I'll price on the last 12 months or the three-year average, whichever is lower, unless I can explain the drop." Why: buyers who pay for a peak year carry the decline. → `sde-and-add-backs-analysis`
6. **What capital spending is coming (vehicles, equipment, software, building)?** Recommended: "Yes: I'll list the assets with age and condition and treat overdue replacement as a cost in year one." Why: SDE ignores the truck that needs replacing next spring. → `sde-and-add-backs-analysis`
7. **What multiple are you paying, and what justifies it beyond the asking price?** Recommended: "Yes: I'll write down the value drivers that justify the multiple and the ones that cut it." Why: an asking price is an opening position. → `small-business-valuation`
8. **Is any cash income being claimed that isn't on the returns?** Recommended: "Yes: I'll value the business only on reported income." Why: lenders lend on reported income, and unreported income is a legal problem for the seller and a pricing problem for you. → `sde-and-add-backs-analysis`

## 2. Financing and coverage

9. **What is debt service coverage on historical earnings with all debt (bank loan plus seller note) counted?** Recommended: "Yes: I'll compute it on the last fiscal year and the two-year average, with every loan payment included." Why: under SOP 50 10 8.1 (effective for loans numbered on or after 2026-10-01), historical DSC is EBITDA divided by combined post-transaction debt service, and the lender may not rely on projections to meet it (see `references/sources.md`; check its `review_by` date). → `sba-dscr-and-affordability`
10. **What happens to coverage if earnings fall 15 to 20 percent?** Recommended: "Yes: I'll run a downside case and know which month I'd run out of cash." Why: the loan payment doesn't fall when revenue does. → `sba-dscr-and-affordability`
11. **After loan payments, what do you take home, and can your household live on it for two years?** Recommended: "Yes: I'll compute take-home after debt service, taxes and reinvestment, and compare it to my household budget." → `business-owner-take-home-pay`
12. **Where does the equity come from, and is any of it borrowed?** Recommended: "Yes: I'll list every source and ask the lender which ones count." Why: the lender decides what counts toward the equity injection. → `acquisition-equity-sources`, `sba-7a-acquisition-loan`
13. **What are the seller note's terms, and does the lender require it to be on standby?** Recommended: "Yes: I'll get the lender's requirement in writing before the purchase agreement." → `seller-note-structuring`
14. **What is the lender's status: term sheet, conditions, appraisal, timing?** Recommended: "Yes: I'll list every lender condition with an owner and a date." → `sba-lender-package`
15. **Have you confirmed the deal's eligibility questions with the lender rather than assumed them?** Recommended: "Yes: I'll ask the lender directly; nobody else can say a deal is eligible." → `sba-sop-rule-check`

## 3. Customers and concentration

16. **What share of revenue comes from the top customer and the top five?** Recommended: "Yes: I'll get revenue by customer for three years from the accounting system, not a summary." Why: concentration changes both price and lendability. → `small-business-risk-assessment`
17. **Are the big customers under contract, and do those contracts survive a change of owner?** Recommended: "Yes: I'll have counsel review assignment and change-of-control terms in the top contracts." → `small-business-legal-due-diligence`
18. **Are customers loyal to the business or to the owner personally?** Recommended: "Yes: I'll ask for an introduction to the top customers before closing, with the seller present, after the purchase agreement is near-final." → `small-business-operational-due-diligence`
19. **What is churn or retention, by year?** Recommended: "Yes: I'll compute it from the customer list rather than accept a stated rate." → `small-business-financial-due-diligence`
20. **Where do new customers come from, and what does each cost?** Recommended: "Yes: I'll map lead sources and check whether any depend on the owner's network." → `small-business-operational-due-diligence`

## 4. Owner dependence and transition

21. **What does the owner do every week that nobody else can?** Recommended: "Yes: I'll get a written list of the owner's tasks, relationships, licenses and passwords." → `small-business-risk-assessment`
22. **How long will the seller stay, doing what, paid how?** Recommended: "Yes: I'll put the transition period, hours, duties and pay in a written agreement signed at closing." Why: a verbal promise to "help out" is the usual source of disputes. If an SBA loan is involved, ask the lender what the current SOP allows. → `seller-transition-plan`, `small-business-deal-structure`
23. **Will the seller sign a non-compete and non-solicit?** Recommended: "Yes: scope, length and geography set with counsel, since enforceability depends on the state." → `small-business-deal-structure`
24. **Why is the owner really selling?** Recommended: "Yes: I'll test the stated reason against the trend and what employees and customers say." → `business-owner-outreach`

## 5. Employees

25. **Who are the two or three people the business can't run without, and will they stay?** Recommended: "Yes: I'll meet them before closing (with the seller's agreement) and plan retention for each." → `small-business-operational-due-diligence`, `seller-transition-plan`
26. **Are wages at market, and is any raise overdue?** Recommended: "Yes: I'll compare pay to local market rates and budget the gap as a cost." → `small-business-operational-due-diligence`
27. **Are workers classified correctly (employees vs contractors) and is payroll tax current?** Recommended: "Yes: my CPA and counsel will check classification and payroll filings." → `small-business-legal-due-diligence`
28. **Can you hire enough skilled workers if two leave?** Recommended: "Yes: I'll check local hiring time and wage for the key roles." → `small-business-risk-assessment`

## 6. Lease and real estate

29. **Does the lease run long enough to cover the loan, with renewal options?** Recommended: "Yes: I'll ask the lender what lease term it needs and get the landlord's assignment or new lease as a closing condition." → `small-business-legal-due-diligence`
30. **Has the landlord been asked to consent, and on what terms (rent increase, personal guarantee)?** Recommended: "Yes: I'll ask the seller to approach the landlord once the purchase agreement is near-final." → `small-business-legal-due-diligence`
31. **If the seller owns the building, is the rent at market?** Recommended: "Yes: I'll get a market rent check and price earnings at market rent." → `small-business-deal-structure`
32. **Any environmental risk (fuel, chemicals, auto, dry cleaning, manufacturing)?** Recommended: "Yes: I'll ask the lender whether it needs an environmental report." → `small-business-legal-due-diligence`

## 7. Legal and licensing

33. **Does the business need a license, certification or permit that you don't hold, and does it transfer?** Recommended: "Yes: I'll confirm with the licensing body and counsel before signing." → `small-business-legal-due-diligence`
34. **Any lawsuits, claims, liens, tax notices or regulatory complaints?** Recommended: "Yes: I'll run lien, litigation and tax searches and ask for a written disclosure." → `small-business-legal-due-diligence`
35. **Asset or stock purchase, and do you understand what liabilities come with each?** Recommended: "Yes: I'll settle structure with counsel and my CPA before the purchase agreement." (Late if after LOI.) → `small-business-deal-structure`
36. **Who is your lawyer, and have they seen the LOI?** Recommended: "Yes: I'll engage counsel before diligence spending starts." → `choose-acquisition-advisors`

## 8. Working capital

37. **Is there a working-capital target (peg) in the LOI, and how was it set?** Recommended: "Yes: I'll set the peg from a 12-month average of the business's normal working capital, agreed in the purchase agreement." (Late if missing after LOI.) → `working-capital-peg-for-small-business`
38. **Who keeps receivables, payables, inventory and customer deposits at closing?** Recommended: "Yes: I'll write each one down and make sure deposits for work I'll do are credited to me." → `working-capital-peg-for-small-business`
39. **How much cash does the business need on day one to make payroll and pay suppliers?** Recommended: "Yes: I'll budget at least one full payroll cycle plus a month of operating costs, and ask the lender about a working-capital line." → `small-business-closing-checklist`

## 9. Your own capacity and downside

40. **If this business failed, what would you lose: savings, home, guarantee exposure?** Recommended: "Yes: I'll write down the worst case in dollars and talk it through with my family before signing." → `personal-guarantee-risk-review`
41. **Can you do this job: the hours, the skills, the industry?** Recommended: "Yes: I'll spend time working in the business (or a similar one) before closing." → `am-i-ready-to-buy-a-business`
42. **How much cash will you have left after closing, outside the business?** Recommended: "Yes: I'll keep a personal reserve and won't put it into the deal." → `business-owner-take-home-pay`
43. **What would make you walk away, decided now while you're calm?** Recommended: "Yes: I'll write three walk-away conditions in decisions.md today." → `acquisition-go-no-go-review`
44. **Are you buying this because it's good, or because you're tired of searching?** Recommended: "Yes: I'll compare it to my buy box and say out loud where it falls short." → `business-acquisition-criteria`

## 10. Timing and process

45. **When does exclusivity end, and will diligence and the loan finish before then?** Recommended: "Yes: I'll build a dated plan back from the exclusivity end and ask for an extension early if needed." → `loi-for-small-business-acquisition`
46. **Is diligence spending staged so you don't pay for everything before the cheap checks?** Recommended: "Yes: bank tie-out and key questions first, QoE and legal after." → `small-business-financial-due-diligence`
47. **AI exposure: could software or AI take a meaningful share of this business's work within the loan term?** Recommended: "Yes: I'll run a quick AI exposure check on the main revenue lines." → `ai-disruption-risk-check`

## Search plan (when there's no deal yet)

- Is the buy box specific enough that you can say no to most listings in a minute? → `business-acquisition-criteria`
- How many owners or listings will you review per week, and how many offers do you expect to make before one closes? → `acquisition-search-plan`
- How many months of runway do you have, and what happens on the last day? → `acquisition-search-plan`
- Is your financing path settled, and has a lender looked at you as a borrower? → `search-fund-vs-sba-loan`, `sba-7a-acquisition-loan`

## Deal-killer patterns to test against

Write these as "works only if…". The record either shows it or doesn't.

- Earnings don't survive the bank tie-out or the add-back review.
- Coverage on historical earnings, with all debt counted, is below what the lender requires, and the fix needs a lower price or more equity than the user has.
- One customer, supplier, license or person the deal can't survive losing, with no contract or plan holding them.
- The landlord won't assign or renew on terms that cover the loan.
- The seller won't put the transition or non-compete in writing.
- A legal or tax liability that can't be left with the seller or covered by escrow.
- The user's downside (guarantee, home, savings) is more than they decided they could bear.
