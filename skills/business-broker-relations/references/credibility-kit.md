# The buyer credibility kit

Brokers usually represent the seller and get paid at closing. A broker with many inquiries per
listing has to guess which buyers will actually close. Make that guess easy, and keep every claim
true.

## 1. Buyer one-pager (template)

Write it in plain words. One page. No logo needed.

```markdown
# Sam Lee: buyer profile
Updated 2026-11-26 · sam@example.com · (555) 010-0000

**Who I am.** Ten years in operations at a regional distribution company; managed a team of 40.

**What I'm looking for.**
- Industries: commercial services, HVAC, plumbing, electrical, pest control
- Size: $500K to $1.5M SDE; price up to about $4M
- Geography: within 90 minutes of Columbus, OH (will relocate for the right business: no)
- I will run the business full time.
- Not a fit: restaurants, retail, businesses with one customer over 30% of revenue

**How I'll pay.** Personal equity of $400K available, plus an SBA 7(a) loan. I've spoken with a
lender about my background and the size of deal I'm looking at; no deal-specific approval yet.
Open to seller financing.

**Timeline.** Can sign an NDA same day, review a CIM within a week, and give an indication of
interest within two weeks of receiving financials.

**Advisors.** CPA and acquisition lawyer identified.
```

Rules for the one-pager:

- Every number matches `profile.yaml` and `buy-box.yaml`.
- Say "spoken with a lender" only if it happened. Never say "pre-approved" or "SBA-eligible"; lenders
  rarely issue anything binding before seeing a specific deal.
- Name advisor categories, not firms, until the user has engaged someone and wants to name them.

## 2. Proof of funds: what and when

Common forms (the broker or seller may ask for one):

- A recent bank or brokerage statement showing the balance, with account numbers redacted.
- A short letter from the user's bank or financial adviser confirming funds available.
- A lender's letter of interest or prequalification (language and weight vary; it is not an approval).
- For investor-backed buyers: a commitment letter or a summary of investor support.

When:

- Not in the first email. Offer it: "Happy to provide proof of funds once we're under NDA."
- Share after an NDA, when asked, and only what is needed. The user decides.
- Never send full account numbers, tax returns of the buyer, or a personal financial statement
  unless the user chooses to and knows who will see it.

## 3. Speed and reliability norms (what the user commits to)

| Event | Target | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Broker email or call | Reply same business day | Brokers move on to the next buyer. |
| NDA received | Sign or send questions within 1 to 2 business days | Delays read as low interest. |
| CIM received | Short reply with first questions within a week | Shows the user read it. |
| Request for a call | Offer two specific times | Removes back and forth. |
| Decision to pass | Tell the broker promptly with a one-line reason | Brokers remember buyers who close the loop. |

## 4. Habits that build reputation with brokers

- Ask specific questions that show the CIM was read (not "send me everything").
- Do what you said, when you said.
- Pass politely and say why; brokers often bring the next deal to buyers who were clear.
- Don't re-trade price late without a real finding.
- Don't contact the owner, employees or customers directly unless the broker arranges it.

## 5. Things that hurt credibility

- A generic note that could be about any listing.
- Asking for the asking-price multiple before reading the CIM.
- Overstating funds, experience or lender status.
- Silence after receiving confidential information.
- An unrealistic first indication that the broker has to talk the seller out of.
