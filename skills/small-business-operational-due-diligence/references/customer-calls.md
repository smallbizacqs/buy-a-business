# Customer calls before closing

Talking to the top customers is often the best single test of whether revenue will stay. It is also the step most likely to upset a seller or leak the sale. Do it late, with consent, and carefully.

## When

- After the phase-1 gate: the financials tie, the deal looks likely, and the buyer is about to spend on paid work.
- Before the decision date on the diligence calendar, leaving time to act on what customers say.
- If the seller refuses all customer contact before closing in a business where a few customers are most of the revenue, that is a finding for `acquisition-go-no-go-review`. Alternatives to discuss: calls after signing but before closing, calls with the seller present, an earnout tied to those customers (not with SBA 7(a) financing: seller earnouts are prohibited in a 7(a) change of ownership, source `sba-sop-50-10-8-1-earnouts`), or a longer seller transition.

## Consent and framing (agree with the seller, in writing)

1. **Which customers:** usually the top 5 to 10 by revenue, plus any with a contract renewal coming up. Ask for one or two former customers if the seller is willing.
2. **Who introduces:** the seller, by phone or in person, before the buyer calls.
3. **How the buyer is introduced:** agree an honest framing with the seller. Options sellers commonly accept: "the person who will be taking over the business", "a new partner joining the business", or, before the sale is announced, a framing the seller is comfortable with that doesn't mislead. The buyer should not lie to customers; if the seller wants a false cover story, raise it with the user.
4. **What the buyer won't discuss:** price, the seller's reasons, employees, or any change in pricing or terms.
5. **Confidentiality:** customers may tell others; the seller decides when the risk is worth it.

Record the agreement in `notes.md` and `decisions.md`.

## Script (15 to 20 minutes)

Open:
> Thanks for taking the time. As [seller] mentioned, I'm [framing agreed with seller]. I'd like to understand how you work with [business] so nothing changes for you. This is not a sales call.

Questions:
1. How long have you worked with [business], and how did you start?
2. What do they do for you? Roughly how often?
3. What do they do well? What would you change?
4. Who do you deal with there day to day? (Owner, a manager, a technician.)
5. How do you decide each year whether to keep using them? Is there a contract or do you just keep calling?
6. Are you planning any changes in the next year or two that would affect how much you buy (moving, growing, cutting back, bringing it in-house, bidding it out)?
7. Have you looked at other providers recently? Why or why not?
8. If [owner] stepped back, what would matter most to you about how things are handled?
9. Anything you'd want a new owner to know?

Close:
> That's very helpful. [Seller] and I will make sure the handover is smooth.

## Notes template (notes.md)

```markdown
## YYYY-MM-DD Customer call: <customer> (consent: seller agreed YYYY-MM-DD; introduced by <seller> on YYYY-MM-DD)
- Spoke with: <name, role>. Caller: <user>.
- Relationship: <years, services, frequency>; day-to-day contact at business: <name>.
- Satisfaction (their words): "..."
- Contract / renewal: <facts>.
- Plans that change volume: "..."
- Owner dependence signal: <fact>.
- Risk to revenue (our analysis): low / medium / high, because <reason>.
- Follow-up: <item, tracker id>.
```

## Reading the results

- Customers who deal mostly with the owner: build introductions into `seller-transition-plan`.
- A customer planning to cut back or bid the work out: quantify in `evaluation.md`; consider structure or price (`small-business-acquisition-negotiation`).
- Consistent complaints (slow response, quality): an operating fix, and a question about why revenue held.
