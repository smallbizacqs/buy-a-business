# Time in the business before closing

Spending days alongside the owner and staff shows what the financials can't: how work comes in, who really does what, the condition of equipment, the culture. The seller decides whether staff are told why the buyer is there; agree it first.

## Plan (agree with the seller)

| Day | Where | Focus |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Office with the owner | A full day of the owner's actual work: calls, quotes, scheduling, billing, banking, problems |
| 2 | Office with the office manager or bookkeeper | Order to cash: lead, quote, job, invoice, collection; payables; payroll run |
| 3 | Field or shop with the lead crew or technician | Quality, safety, how jobs are run, vehicle and tool condition |
| 4 | Busy day (Monday or peak season day) | Capacity and bottlenecks |
| 5 | Sales or estimating with the owner | How prices are set and jobs won |

Shorter versions work: two days, one office and one field.

## What to watch and write down

- Every task the owner did, how long it took, and whether someone else could do it.
- Where work gets stuck (approvals only the owner gives, information only in the owner's head).
- How staff talk to customers and to each other.
- Equipment that breaks or is worked around.
- Records kept on paper, in texts or in the owner's memory.
- Anything that contradicts what the CIM or the seller said.

## Questions to ask staff (if the seller has agreed they know)

1. What does a normal day look like?
2. What slows you down?
3. Who do you go to when something goes wrong?
4. What would you change if you could?

Don't discuss the sale terms, pay changes or job security; that conversation belongs to the seller and later to the buyer as owner.
