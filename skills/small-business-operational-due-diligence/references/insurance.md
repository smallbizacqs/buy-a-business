# Insurance review

Bring in a commercial insurance broker early in diligence; quotes and binders take time and the lender will want proof of coverage at closing. The lender's credit memo for an SBA acquisition addresses life insurance (on whom and how much, or why none) and business hazard and liability insurance (source `sba-sop-50-10-8-1-insurance-credit-memo`, SOP 50 10 8.1; check its `review_by` date). Ask the lender for its insurance requirements in writing and record them in `lender.md`.

## What to collect

- Every current policy with declarations pages (from the seller).
- Loss runs from each insurer for five years (the seller requests them; insurers send them to the policyholder).
- Open claims and reserves.
- Certificates of insurance the business is required to provide to customers (contracts often require specific limits).

## Coverage checklist (for the broker conversation)

| Coverage | Question |
|---|---|
| General liability | Limits match customer contract requirements? |
| Property / equipment | Replacement cost? Equipment at job sites covered? |
| Commercial auto | All vehicles listed? Employees driving personal vehicles for work? |
| Workers' compensation | Classification codes right for the work? Experience modifier? Open claims? |
| Professional / E&O | Needed for advice, design, healthcare, accounting, IT work? Claims-made? |
| Cyber | Customer data, payments, ransomware? |
| Employment practices | Number of employees, past claims? |
| Umbrella | Above GL and auto? |
| Business interruption | Covers lost income after a covered loss? |
| Inland marine / tools | Tools and equipment in vehicles? |
| Bonds | License or contract bonds the business must carry, and whether they transfer |
| Life and disability on the buyer | Lender requirement? Amount? |
| Key-person | Business depends on one person after closing? |

## Claims-made policies and tail coverage

Some policies (often professional, E&O, cyber, employment practices) cover claims made while the policy is active, not acts that happened while it was active. When the seller's policy ends at closing, claims about the seller's past work may be uncovered. The broker and lawyer decide whether the seller buys tail coverage (an extended reporting period) and who pays; the purchase agreement records it.

## Reading loss runs

- Frequency: many small claims point to operating problems.
- Severity: one large claim may still be open or reserved.
- Types: vehicle accidents, worker injuries, customer property damage: each says something about how the business runs.
- Gaps: years with no loss run or a change of insurer after claims.

Record facts in the tracker (`I` rows) and gaps in `evaluation.md`.
