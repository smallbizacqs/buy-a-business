# Binding and non-binding parts of an LOI

This explains common practice so the buyer knows what to ask their lawyer. Whether a given sentence is binding depends on its wording, the rest of the document, how the parties behave and the governing state's law. That is a lawyer's question; don't answer it for the user.

## The usual split

| Usually non-binding (statement of intent) | Usually binding |
|---|---|
| Price and how it's paid | Exclusivity / no-shop |
| Asset or stock structure | Confidentiality |
| Seller note, earnout, rollover | Expenses (each side pays its own) |
| Working capital and inventory | Governing law |
| Transition, non-compete, employees | How and when the letter ends, and which parts survive |
| Conditions to closing | Access for diligence (sometimes) |
| Target closing date | Any deposit terms (when there is a deposit) |

The non-binding parts still matter: they set expectations, and moving away from them later costs goodwill. A buyer who changes a non-binding price without a factual reason (see the `retrade` leaf and `small-business-acquisition-negotiation`) can lose the deal or the seller's trust even though nothing obliged them to the price.

## Questions to put to counsel

- Does the letter say clearly which sections bind and which don't, and does any other sentence undercut that (for example, "the parties agree", "shall")?
- Could the letter be read as an obligation to negotiate in good faith, and what would that mean here?
- If there is a deposit: when is it refundable, who holds it, and what releases it?
- Does exclusivity bind only the seller, or the business and its owners and brokers too?
- Does the confidentiality section conflict with the NDA already signed?
- Is the buyer signing personally? Should an entity sign, or "buyer or its designee"?

## Signals to flag (not interpret)

- No binding/non-binding statement at all.
- "This letter constitutes the agreement of the parties."
- Economic terms in the same section as exclusivity.
- A clause saying the buyer pays a fee or loses a deposit if it doesn't close.

Record any of these under "Questions for counsel" in `loi-draft.md` with the quoted text and section number.
