# LOI term checklist

Every term a small-business LOI should settle or deliberately leave open. For each: what to decide, a buyer-side starting point to offer the user as the recommended answer, and what to watch. Starting points are common practice, not rules; the deal, the lender and counsel can change them.

The rule of thumb: **settle in the LOI anything that moves money or could kill the deal.** Terms left vague in the LOI ("normal working capital", "reasonable transition") get negotiated later, when the seller knows the buyer has spent money and time and is less likely to walk.

## Economics

| Term | Decide | Recommended starting point | Watch |
|---|---|---|---|
| Purchase price | A number, not a range or formula | A single figure tied to the earnings in `evaluation.md` ("based on SDE of $X for the 12 months ending [date]") | Saying what earnings the price rests on is what makes a later reprice discussion factual rather than personal. |
| What the price buys | Assets included and excluded | All assets used in the business: equipment, vehicles, customer lists, phone numbers, domains, social accounts, trade names, software licenses, goodwill | Named exclusions (owner's truck, real estate, personal accounts). Vehicles titled to the owner personally. |
| Cash-free, debt-free | Who keeps cash and pays debt | Seller keeps cash, pays off all debt and liens at closing | Debt-like items: customer deposits, deferred revenue, accrued payroll and PTO, unpaid taxes, equipment leases. See `working-capital-peg-for-small-business`. |
| Working capital | Whether a normal level is delivered, and how it's measured | "A normal level of net working capital, set as the trailing 12-month average, defined in diligence" | "Normal level" with no method. Inventory priced on top of the price ("plus inventory at cost") without a counting method. |
| Inventory | Included in price or added | If added: physical count within a few days of closing, at the lower of cost or what it's worth, obsolete and damaged stock excluded, a cap | No cap; "at cost" with no definition of cost; who counts. |
| Cash at close | How much the seller gets on the day | Senior loan proceeds plus buyer equity | Share of price at close matters to the seller more than headline; the negotiation skill trades on it. |
| Senior loan | Amount and type | The amount from the coverage scenario in `evaluation.md` | Financing contingency below. |
| Seller note | Amount, rate, term, payments, standby, subordination | Amount the lender's structure allows; rate and term from `seller-note-structuring`; "subordinated to senior lender, on terms the lender requires" | If SBA-financed: seller debt counts toward the buyer's equity only if subordinated and on full standby, with no principal or interest payments for the life of the 7(a) loan (source `sba-sop-50-10-8-1-seller-debt-equity`) (SOP 50 10 8.1, loans numbered on or after 2026-10-01; check the source's `review_by` date). Confirm terms with the lender and `seller-note-structuring` before fixing them in the LOI. |
| Earnout | Metric, period, cap, how measured | Only if seller and buyer disagree about the future; a metric the seller can see (revenue or gross profit) | Vague metrics cause disputes. In an SBA 7(a) change of ownership, seller earnouts are prohibited; buyer rebates tied to business performance are allowed (source `sba-sop-50-10-8-1-earnouts`) (SOP 50 10 8.1, loans numbered on or after 2026-10-01; check the source's `review_by` date). See `small-business-deal-structure`. |
| Rollover equity | Whether the seller keeps a stake | Usually none in a first small deal | Governance and exit rights; counsel. |
| Deposit / earnest money | Whether any is paid, refundable or not | None, or a small fully refundable deposit held by a neutral party | Nonrefundable deposits; deposits released to the seller before closing. |
| Allocation of price | Who decides | "To be agreed in the purchase agreement" | A tax matter for each side's CPA. In an asset purchase both buyer and seller report the sale on IRS Form 8594 (source `irs-form-8594-who-files`). |

## Structure

| Term | Recommended starting point | Watch |
|---|---|---|
| Asset or stock purchase | Asset purchase unless a reason (licenses, contracts, permits that can't transfer) points to stock | Ask the CPA and lawyer; `small-business-deal-structure` lists the trade-offs. |
| Buyer entity | "Buyer or an entity Buyer forms" | Signing personally when an entity is intended. |
| Real estate | Lease from the seller at market rent, with a term and renewals that cover the loan, and an option or first right to buy | Rent set below market in the P&L (owner-landlord). For 7(a) loans the lease term including renewal options should equal or exceed the loan term (source `sba-sop-50-10-8-1-lease-term`). |

## People and transition

| Term | Recommended starting point | Watch |
|---|---|---|
| Seller transition | A set period full-time then part-time, stated in weeks or months; paid or included in price stated | "Reasonable assistance" with no period. In an SBA 7(a) initial acquisition the seller can't stay as an officer, director, owner or employee, but the business may hire the seller as a consultant for up to 24 months in total (source `sba-sop-50-10-8-1-seller-consultant`) (SOP 50 10 8.1, loans numbered on or after 2026-10-01; check the source's `review_by` date). |
| Consulting agreement | If paid, a separate agreement with duties and hours | Moving price into consulting changes tax for both sides; CPA. |
| Non-compete and non-solicit | Seller doesn't compete or solicit customers or employees in a defined area for a defined period | Enforceability varies by state; lawyer. |
| Employees | Buyer intends to offer employment to [all / named] employees | Seller pays accrued wages, bonuses and (if the law requires) PTO at closing. |

## Conditions to closing

List every condition that lets the buyer walk without penalty. Recommended set:

1. Satisfactory completion of due diligence, in the buyer's judgment.
2. Financing on terms acceptable to the buyer.
3. A signed purchase agreement acceptable to both sides.
4. Landlord consent to assign the lease, or a new lease on acceptable terms.
5. Consents for named key contracts, licenses and permits.
6. No material adverse change between signing and closing.
7. Key employees or the seller's transition commitment, if the deal depends on them.

Watch for templates that leave out the financing or diligence condition, or that make diligence satisfaction "reasonable" (an objective test someone else can argue about).

## Process

| Term | Recommended starting point | Watch |
|---|---|---|
| Exclusivity (no-shop) | Long enough for diligence and financing to finish with a buffer: ask the lender how long its process takes, count back from a realistic closing date, and add a couple of weeks. Add an automatic extension while the buyer is actively working and the lender's approval is pending. | Short periods with no extension. Exclusivity that ends before the lender can close. |
| Access | Access to books, records, premises, and (with seller consent, late in diligence) key customers, employees and landlord | "Access at seller's discretion". |
| Target closing date | A date, labeled a target | A hard date that, if missed, ends the deal. |
| Confidentiality | Both sides keep terms confidential; an NDA may already cover it | Conflicts with an existing NDA. |
| Expenses | Each side pays its own | Broker fee shifted to buyer; break-up fees. |
| Offer expiry | A date, for example five business days | An open offer the seller can shop. |
| Binding vs non-binding | Only exclusivity, confidentiality, expenses, governing law and termination binding | See `binding-vs-nonbinding.md`. |

## Common LOI mistakes that cost buyers later

- Price with no stated earnings basis, so a diligence finding becomes "you're retrading".
- Working capital not mentioned, then a fight at closing over whether receivables come with the business.
- Seller note terms the lender later rejects, forcing a renegotiation.
- Exclusivity shorter than the lender's process.
- No financing condition, or a deposit that goes hard before diligence ends.
- Transition left as "reasonable" when the business depends on the owner.
- Signing personally with no entity language.
- Treating the LOI as the place to finally learn the seller's priorities, instead of asking before drafting (see `small-business-acquisition-negotiation`).
