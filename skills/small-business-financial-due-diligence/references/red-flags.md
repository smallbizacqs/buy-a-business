# Misstatement and fraud signals

None of these proves wrongdoing; each is a reason to ask and to dig. Record the fact in the tracker's `finding` and the question in `follow_up`; don't characterize intent.

## Revenue

- P&L revenue above tax return gross receipts.
- Bank deposits below reported revenue, or deposits in accounts not disclosed.
- "Cash sales not deposited" offered as an add-back or as a reason the numbers are better than they look. The buyer can't verify it, and a lender testing coverage on tax returns and IRS transcripts won't see it (source `sba-sop-50-10-8-1-seller-tax-verification` for SBA loans).
- Revenue jump in the last months before sale with no new customer or price change to explain it.
- Large round-number deposits, or deposits from the owner recorded as sales.
- Invoices to related parties or to customers who can't be confirmed.
- Year-end revenue entries reversed in January.

## Expenses and add-backs

- Add-backs that grow every time the buyer asks.
- "One-time" costs that appear every year.
- Expenses that fall sharply just before the sale (maintenance, marketing, wages).
- Family members' wages added back while they actually work in the business.
- Personal expenses claimed as add-backs with no records.

## Balance sheet and cash

- AR that grows faster than revenue; very old receivables still counted.
- Inventory that never gets written down.
- Payables stretching out before closing.
- Merchant cash advances (lump-sum deposits followed by daily debits).
- Payroll taxes or sales taxes past due.
- Liens on public searches not on the debt schedule.

## Behavior

- Slow, partial or edited documents; PDFs that look retyped rather than exported.
- Refusal to allow the buyer to see bank statements directly or to sign a tax transcript authorization.
- Refusal to let the buyer speak to the outside bookkeeper or CPA.
- Story changes when numbers are questioned.

## What to do

1. Record the fact and the source document.
2. Ask the seller for an explanation in writing (draft it; the user sends).
3. If it isn't resolved by the phase-1 gate, take it to `acquisition-go-no-go-review` before spending on phase 2.
4. If it suggests the seller misrepresented the business, the user should talk to their lawyer before saying anything further to the seller.
