# Reading a listing in five minutes

A listing is an advertisement written for the seller. Read it to decide whether to ask for more, not whether to buy.

## Pull out these items (write "not stated" if absent)

| Item | Why |
|---|---|
| Asking price, and what it includes (inventory, equipment, real estate, receivables) | "Plus inventory" or "real estate available separately" changes the real price. |
| Revenue and its year | Old numbers hide trends. |
| Cash flow / SDE / EBITDA figure, its label and year | Decide later which measure applies (`sde-and-add-backs-analysis`). |
| Years established; years under current owner | Owner tenure hints at how much depends on them. |
| Employees (full-time, part-time, contractors) | Team depth, owner dependence. |
| Owner's role and hours | "Semi-absentee" claims need checking. |
| Reason for selling | Retirement, health, burnout, partner dispute, decline. Ask again on the call (`business-owner-outreach`). |
| Facilities and lease | Lease term left and transferability matter to lenders. |
| Seller financing offered? | A signal of the seller's confidence and flexibility; terms come later. |
| Training or transition offered | Length and form. |
| Days on market or listing date | Long-listed deals deserve a question about why (see `how-brokers-work.md`). |

## Quick ratios (on the listing's own numbers, labelled as such)

- Asking price ÷ stated cash flow = the implied multiple the seller is asking. It is not a value judgment; compare only after `small-business-valuation`.
- Cash flow ÷ revenue = the implied margin. If far from what the user has seen in the same industry, ask why.

## Early warning signs worth a question (not automatic passes)

- No year given for financials, or only a "projected" figure.
- Cash flow suspiciously close to revenue minus cost of goods only.
- "Cash business" language or claims of unreported income. A buyer usually can't finance or rely on income that isn't on the tax returns; see `sde-and-add-backs-analysis`.
- One customer, one supplier, or one license mentioned prominently.
- Price far above anything the stated earnings could carry with debt (check with `sba-dscr-and-affordability`).
- Repeated relisting or many price cuts.

## What to write in `listing.md` → First questions

Five or fewer, specific to the listing. Examples:

- "Which year is the $410K cash flow from, and does it come from the tax return or the internal P&L?"
- "How many owner salaries are added back?"
- "What share of revenue does the largest customer represent?"
- "How long is left on the lease, and will the landlord assign it?"
- "How long has it been on the market?"
