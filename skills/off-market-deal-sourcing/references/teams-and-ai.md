# Help: assistants, interns, sourcing services and AI

## Options

| Option | Good for | Watch for |
|---|---|---|
| Doing it yourself | Learning the industry; owners hear the buyer directly | Time; consistency slips |
| Virtual assistant | List building, data cleanup, mailing logistics, logging | Data quality; access to your accounts; they speak for you only from approved scripts |
| Interns (often business-school students) | List research, first calls with scripts, industry research | Training and supervision time; pay and employment rules (ask a lawyer or payroll provider); turnover each term |
| Paid sourcing or calling services | Volume | They speak in your name; ask who calls, from what script, under which consent rules, and who owns the data. A lawyer should review the contract. |
| AI agent (this one) | Building and scoring lists from public sources, summarizing websites, drafting personalized notes, tracking follow-ups | Accuracy (verify facts before using them in a message), source terms of use, privacy, and never sending on its own |

Questions to ask any person or service before hiring:

- What exactly will you do, under whose name, and with which scripts?
- How will you record opt-outs and follow the rules for each channel?
- Who owns the list and replies if we stop?
- How do you report activity (sends, replies, conversations) each week?
- Can I talk to two buyers you've worked with?

## What the AI agent can do in this skill

1. Turn the buy box into search terms, industry codes and geographic filters.
2. Read public pages the user points to (or can reach without logging in) and fill `targets.csv` with sources and dates.
3. Score each target against must-haves and note one public fact for personalization, with its source.
4. Draft letters, emails and call notes, saved as drafts.
5. Prepare the weekly follow-up list from `next_touch` dates.
6. Summarize replies the user pastes, and suggest a response draft.

## What it won't do

- Send, schedule or queue messages, make calls, or log in to the user's accounts without explicit, specific OK.
- Guess personal details or scrape personal social media.
- Present modelled revenue or a "likely to sell" score as fact.
