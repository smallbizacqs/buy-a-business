# Outreach drafts

Templates to adapt in the user's own voice. Every draft saved to `acquisition/outreach/drafts/` carries "DRAFT: not sent". Fill `[brackets]` only with true facts; delete a line rather than invent.

## Principles

- **Short and specific.** Owners get many letters. Say who you are, why this business, what you're asking for (a conversation), and how to say no.
- **True story.** Real background, real reason for the industry and area. Never imply funding, a referral, or experience the user doesn't have.
- **Ask for a conversation, not a sale.** Many owners aren't ready; a relationship can last years (`business-owner-outreach` covers "not ready yet").
- **Respect.** Make declining easy; honor it permanently.
- **Personalize from public facts** (years in business, a project on their site, their community role) and note the source in the draft.
- **Follow up a few times, then stop.** Space touches out; vary the channel only where the user has checked the rules for it.

## Letter (one page)

> [Date]
>
> Dear [Owner name],
>
> My name is [Name]. I live in [town] and I'm looking to buy and run one business in [industry] in [area], for the long term. [One sentence on background: e.g. "I spent eight years managing service technicians for a regional facilities company."]
>
> I came across [Business] because [true public fact: e.g. "you've served the north side since 1994 and your customer reviews mention the same technicians by name for years"]. I'm writing to owners whose businesses I admire, in case a sale is something you've thought about now or for the future.
>
> I'm not a broker and I'm not asking for anything confidential. I'd value a short conversation, whenever suits you, about how you built the business and what you'd want for it someday.
>
> If now isn't the right time, or you'd rather not hear from me again, just tell me and I won't write again.
>
> [Name] · [phone] · [email]

## Cold email (under 120 words)

> Subject: [Business], a question from a local buyer
>
> Hi [Owner first name],
>
> I'm [Name], based in [town]. I'm looking to buy one [industry] business in [area] and run it myself for the long term. [One line of background.]
>
> [Business] stood out because [public fact]. If you've ever thought about what happens to the business when you step back, I'd welcome a 20-minute call, with no obligation and nothing confidential needed.
>
> If you'd rather not hear from me, reply "no thanks" and I won't write again.
>
> [Name]
> [Phone] · [Mailing address if the user's rules require one]

Follow-up 1 (a week or two later): one line referencing the first email, one new true detail, the same easy out.
Follow-up 2 (a few weeks later): "Closing the loop. If the timing is ever right, I'd still like to talk. Either way, thanks for reading."

## Phone

**Opener** (ask permission first):
> "Hi, is this [Owner]? My name's [Name]. I'm a local buyer; this isn't a sales call. Do you have a minute, or is there a better time?"

If yes:
> "I'm looking to buy one [industry] business in [area] and run it myself. I'm calling a few owners I respect to ask whether selling is something you've thought about, now or down the road. Would you be open to a coffee sometime?"

If "not for sale": "Understood, thanks for telling me. Would it be all right if I checked in once a year, or would you rather I didn't?" Log the answer.

If gatekeeper: "Could you tell me the best way to reach [Owner] about a personal business matter? I'm happy to send a letter instead."

**Voicemail** (under 20 seconds): name, "local buyer, not a sales call", one reason for calling, phone number twice.

## In person

Visiting a storefront or counter business: introduce yourself as a customer would, ask for the owner, hand over the letter if they're busy. Never pose as a customer to extract information.

## What never goes in a draft

- Claims about funding ("cash buyer", "pre-approved") unless the user can prove them.
- Invented mutual connections or referrals.
- Valuation numbers or offers before a conversation.
- Pressure ("other buyers are interested", deadlines).
- Anything about the owner's health, family or finances learned from gossip.
