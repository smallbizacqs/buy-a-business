# Operating system toolkit

## A. Meeting rhythm

Start small. A team under 10 may need only the weekly meeting and the monthly review; add the rest as managers appear.

| Meeting | Cadence | Who | Length | Agenda | Output |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Daily huddle (optional) | Daily, start of shift | Crew or team | 10 min | Today's jobs, problems, safety | Everyone knows the day's plan |
| Weekly team or leadership meeting | Weekly, same time | Owner + people who own scorecard numbers | 60–90 min | 1. Scorecard: on or off target (no discussion yet). 2. Quarterly priorities: on or off track. 3. Customer and employee news. 4. Issues: pick the top three, solve them, assign actions. 5. Recap actions. | Action list with owners and dates |
| Monthly financial review | Monthly, after close | Owner, bookkeeper or CPA | 60 min | P&L vs budget and last year, balance sheet, cash forecast, receivables aging, covenant tests, payroll as share of revenue | Variances explained; actions |
| Quarterly priorities | Quarterly | Owner + leaders | Half day | Review last quarter; pick three to five priorities for next quarter, each with one owner and a measure | Priority list |
| Annual plan | Yearly | Owner + leaders (+ advisors) | 1–2 days | Where the business is going in 3 years; this year's goals; budget; org chart; capital spending | Plan and budget |
| One-to-ones | Every 2–4 weeks | Owner with each direct report | 30 min | Their agenda first; progress; obstacles; development | Notes; follow-ups |

Meeting rules that help: start on time; scorecard first, every week; issues get solved or dropped, not discussed forever; every action has an owner and a date.

## C. Named management frameworks

Several packaged systems exist for running small and mid-sized companies; EOS is one many owners mention. Most share the same parts: a vision written down, a scorecard, quarterly priorities, a weekly meeting with an issues list, and an org chart built on roles rather than people.

Views practitioners hold:
- **Adopt one fully:** a shared language and tools help a team move together; outside facilitators can keep the owner honest.
- **Borrow the parts:** a small team gets most of the value from a scorecard, a weekly meeting and quarterly priorities, without the cost or vocabulary of a full system.
- **Wait:** in the first months, installing a framework is one more change on top of a new owner; build trust first.

What most owners of very small businesses do by default is unclear; present the views and let the user choose. Questions to decide: team size, whether there's a leadership team to run it with, budget for a facilitator, and whether the team has bandwidth for new routines right now.

## D. Hiring a key role

1. **Role scorecard:** the mission of the seat in one sentence; three to five outcomes with measures for the first year; the skills and traits needed.
2. **Pay range:** check local market data with sources; set the range before interviewing. Terms are the user's call with their lawyer and CPA.
3. **Sourcing:** referrals from employees, customers, suppliers and peers; industry associations; trade schools for technical roles; job boards. Ask current employees whom they'd want to work with.
4. **Screen:** a 20-minute call on the scorecard outcomes and pay range.
5. **Structured interview:** same questions for every candidate, scored. Examples:
   - "Walk me through your career: for each job, what were you hired to do, what did you accomplish, what went wrong, why did you leave?"
   - "Tell me about a time you inherited a mess. What did you do in the first month?"
   - "What would your last boss say you should work on?"
   - For managers: "Tell me about someone you had to let go. How did you handle it?"
6. **Work sample:** a short paid or realistic task (price a job, build a schedule, handle an angry customer role-play).
7. **References:** ask to speak to former managers the candidate names; ask what they'd rate the person on a scale of 1 to 10 and why not higher.
8. **Offer and first 90 days:** written plan for weeks 1, 4 and 12; who trains them; what they own by day 90.

Warning signs: hiring the first person who seems fine because the owner is exhausted; hiring a manager without defining what they own; skipping references.

## E. AI in operations

Pick up to three. For each, record benefit, monthly cost, data touched, and the human check.

| Use | What it does | Human check | Cautions |
|---|---|---|---|
| Drafting customer messages | First drafts of quotes, follow-ups, review responses | Owner or staff reads before sending | Don't paste sensitive customer data into tools whose terms you haven't read |
| Call and meeting summaries | Summaries and action items from recorded calls | Staff confirm actions | Recording consent laws vary by state; ask a lawyer before recording calls |
| Scheduling and dispatch help | Suggest routes and schedules | Dispatcher approves | Check results against real drive times before trusting |
| Bookkeeping assistance | Categorize transactions, flag anomalies | Bookkeeper or CPA reviews | Never let a tool approve payments |
| Document search | Answer staff questions from manuals and SOPs | Spot-check answers | Keep the source documents current |
| Quoting support | Draft estimates from past jobs | Estimator approves every quote | Errors cost margin; track quote accuracy |
| Marketing content | Drafts for website, social posts, ads | Owner approves | Avoid claims you can't back up |
| Hiring help | Draft job posts, summarize applications | Owner decides | Don't let a tool screen candidates without checking for unfair exclusion; ask a lawyer |

Start with one use, measure time saved for a month, then decide. AI exposure of the business itself is a different question: see `ai-disruption-risk-check`.

## F. Outside support

| Option | What it is | Time and cost | Good for |
|---|---|---|---|
| Peer group | A group of owners of similar-sized, non-competing businesses meeting monthly or quarterly | A few hours a month; membership fees vary | Isolation, accountability, practical answers from people who've done it |
| Searcher or acquirer community | Other people who bought businesses | Varies | Post-acquisition problems specific to buyers |
| Mentor | One experienced owner or executive | An hour a month; often unpaid | Judgment on specific decisions |
| Advisory board | Two to four outside advisors meeting quarterly, without legal authority | Half day a quarter; small fees or equity | Strategy, discipline, accountability |
| Formal board | Legal board of directors, usually when there are investors | Per the company's documents | Governance with investors; set up with a lawyer |

Questions to ask any group: who else is in it (size, industry, stage), how meetings run, how confidentiality is kept, cost, and how to leave.

### Owner workload and well-being

Track the owner's hours for two weeks; list what only the owner can do; hand off the rest over time (`professionalize-small-business`). Warning signs worth taking seriously: sleep problems, irritability with staff or family, avoiding the numbers, dread of the phone. These are signs to talk to someone (a peer, a mentor, a health professional), not something the agent diagnoses.
