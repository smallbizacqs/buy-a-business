# Raise playbook

## Counsel gate (paste at the top of the plan)

Before any of the following, securities counsel must have chosen the exemption and approved the documents and wording, and the plan must record the date:

- contacting a prospective investor about investing, by any channel;
- sending a teaser, memo, model or deck to anyone outside the user's advisors;
- any public statement that mentions raising money (social posts, podcasts, newsletters, websites, conference talks);
- accepting money or a commitment.

If something already happened (a post, a pitch over coffee), write it down and tell counsel first. Whether it matters depends on facts counsel will ask about.

## Rule 506(b) and 506(c) at a high level

Quote from the cited sources (17 CFR 230.501–506 via eCFR). Check each `review_by`. Counsel decides which applies and what else is required (state notice filings, information requirements, documents).

| | Rule 506(b) | Rule 506(c) |
|---|---|---|
| Advertising and general solicitation | Not allowed: "neither the issuer nor any person acting on its behalf shall offer or sell the securities by any form of general solicitation or general advertising" except as 506(c) or a narrow 504 case provides (`ecfr-17-cfr-230-502-general-solicitation`) | Allowed, under the 506(c) conditions |
| Who may buy | "no more than … 35 purchasers" in any 90-calendar-day period, counted as Rule 501(e) provides (`ecfr-17-cfr-230-506-b-purchasers`; counsel applies the counting rules, including how accredited investors are treated); each non-accredited purchaser must be sophisticated alone or with a representative (`ecfr-17-cfr-230-506-b-sophistication`) | "All purchasers of securities sold in any offering under paragraph (c) of this section are accredited investors." (`ecfr-17-cfr-230-506-c-accredited`) |
| Verification | Non-accredited purchasers must receive specified information before sale (`ecfr-17-cfr-230-502-information`) | "The issuer shall take reasonable steps to verify that purchasers … are accredited investors." (`ecfr-17-cfr-230-506-c-verification`) |
| Notice filing | Form D within 15 calendar days after the first sale (`ecfr-17-cfr-230-503-form-d`) | Same |
| Disqualification | Not available if the issuer or covered persons are "bad actors" (`ecfr-17-cfr-230-506-bad-actor`) | Same |

Accredited investor, for individuals (two of the tests): income over $200,000 (or $300,000 joint) in each of the two most recent years with a reasonable expectation of the same this year (`ecfr-17-cfr-230-501-accredited-income`); net worth over $1,000,000 alone or with a spouse or spousal equivalent (`ecfr-17-cfr-230-501-accredited-net-worth`). The rule has other categories and exclusions (for example, how a primary residence is treated in net worth); counsel applies them.

Practical consequence to tell the user: under 506(b), keep the raise private, with people you have a prior relationship with, and say nothing public about raising; under 506(c), you may talk publicly but must verify every investor is accredited. Counsel chooses.

## Terms to decide

| Raise type | Term | Question to settle | Trade-off |
|---|---|---|---|
| Search capital | Amount and use | How many months of salary and search costs? | Bigger raise, more dilution or obligations |
| Search capital | Rights to invest in the deal | Do search investors get first right to fund the deal, and at what step-up on their search capital? | Rewards early risk; complicates later investors |
| Search capital | Searcher equity | How is the searcher's share earned: at close, over time, on performance? | Alignment vs certainty |
| Deal equity (sponsor) | Preferred return | Do investors get a set return before the sponsor shares profits? At what rate? | Higher pref, less sponsor upside early |
| Deal equity (sponsor) | Carry or promote | What share of profits above the pref goes to the sponsor, and in tiers? | Sponsor upside vs investor appeal |
| Deal equity (sponsor) | Fees | Closing fee, ongoing management fee? | Pays the sponsor; investors scrutinize it |
| Deal equity (sponsor) | Sponsor co-investment | How much of the sponsor's own money goes in? | Commitment signal |
| All | Governance | Board seats, approval rights over budget, debt, hiring, sale | Investor protection vs operator freedom |
| All | Liquidity and exit | When and how investors can get money back | Long hold vs investor needs |
| All | Instrument | Common, preferred, notes, LLC units | Counsel and CPA decide structure and tax |
| SBA deal | Ownership levels | Will anyone own 20% or more (and so give an unlimited guaranty under SOP 50 10 8.1)? | Investors may resist guaranties |

Never quote a "standard" preferred return, carry, step-up or searcher share as fact. If the user wants a reference point, list questions for experienced investors and counsel, and record the answers as views.

## Materials (drafts for counsel review only)

- One-page summary (buyer, thesis or deal, amount, use of funds).
- Investor memo (business, earnings, price, structure, risks, plan).
- Model (sources and uses, debt service, coverage, returns scenarios labeled as scenarios).
- Buyer background.
- Term summary from counsel.

## Co-sponsor or partner questions

1. What does each bring (deal, capital, operating, investor relationships)?
2. How are economics split, including fees and carry?
3. Who decides what, and how are deadlocks broken?
4. Who guarantees debt, if anyone?
5. What happens if one wants out, or doesn't perform?

## Questions for securities counsel

1. 506(b) or 506(c) for this raise, and why?
2. What can I say publicly, and what have I already said that matters?
3. What documents do I need, and what state filings?
4. How do I handle investors who aren't accredited?
5. How do the terms interact with the lender's requirements (guaranties, ownership levels)?
6. What does the engagement cost (fixed fee?) and what's the timeline?
