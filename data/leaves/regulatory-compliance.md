**Short answer.** Find out which agencies and licensing boards regulate the business, then check its record with each: inspections, citations, complaints, disciplinary actions and any open corrective orders. Also check whether the business is set up the way its regulators require, including who is allowed to own it. A clean license today doesn't mean a clean history, and regulators' findings can stay with the business after you buy it.

### Map the regulators first

Most small businesses need a mix of federal and state licenses and permits, and activities regulated by a federal agency need a federal license.[^sba-licenses-permits-federal] Which state, county and city licenses apply depends on what the business does and where.[^sba-licenses-permits] Ask the seller for a list of every license, permit, registration and certification, then check it against the state's licensing agency and the industry's board yourself. Regulators to consider include:

- **State licensing boards** for trades and professions: contractors, electricians, plumbers, cosmetology, healthcare, engineering, accounting, real estate.
- **Health departments** for food service and personal care.
- **Workplace safety** regulators (OSHA or the state equivalent) for physical work.
- **Environmental agencies** for businesses with emissions, wastewater or hazardous materials. See [environmental reports](/q/environmental).
- **Transportation** regulators for trucking and vehicle fleets.
- **Payers and program agencies** for businesses that bill government health programs or hold government contracts.
- **Consumer protection** rules for businesses that sell to the public by phone, text, email or subscription.

### What to check with each

- **Status.** Active, in good standing, and held by the right person or entity.
- **History.** Inspection reports, citations, fines and complaints. Some boards publish disciplinary actions and license lookups online, and some health departments post inspection reports.
- **Open items.** Corrective action plans, probation, unpaid fines, or a pending renewal with conditions.
- **Ownership rules.** Some professions restrict who can own the business. SBA treats a business whose services require a professional license as eligible only if its ownership structure meets the state's requirements.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-professional-license-ownership] If you don't hold the license, confirm you're allowed to own the business at all.
- **Required personnel.** Some licenses require a qualified person on staff (a licensed supervisor, a pharmacist in charge, a qualifying agent). If that person is the seller, see [licenses tied to the owner](/q/license-dependence).

### Why lenders care

SBA lenders discuss federal, state or local citations that would stop the business from operating normally in their credit analysis.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-credit-memo-citations] A serious open citation can delay or stop the loan.

### Questions to ask the seller

- Has any regulator inspected, cited or fined the business in the last several years?
- Are there complaints pending with any board?
- Does any license depend on you or another individual personally?
- Have you changed anything about how you operate to address a regulator's concern?

Ask for copies of inspection reports and correspondence, not just answers. Then confirm with the public records where they exist.

### Protecting yourself

The purchase agreement should include a representation that the business complies with laws and holds the licenses it needs, a list of exceptions in the disclosure schedules, and an indemnity for pre-closing violations. See [reps and warranties](/q/reps-warranties). Transferring or re-applying for licenses is covered in [licenses and permits](/q/licenses-permits-transfer).
