**Short answer.** Usually not as an outside buyer. Under SOP 50 10 8.1 (effective October 1, 2026), a newcomer buying with a 7(a) loan is making an "Initial Acquisition," which means buying 100% of the business.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-initial-acquisition-100-percent] SBA's partial-purchase category is built for existing owners and long-time employees: someone who hasn't worked in the business for 24 months may buy less than half and can't become the largest owner.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-owner-buyout-outsider-limit] When a selling owner keeps under 20%, they must guarantee the whole loan for at least two years.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-partial-change-guaranty]

### The two categories that matter

**Initial Acquisition.** A deal that makes someone the new majority or largest owner, when they weren't an owner before or have worked there fewer than 24 months.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-initial-acquisition-definition] If you're an individual buyer who isn't already an owner, you buy 100%, and you and the business are co-borrowers.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-initial-acquisition-100-percent] In this category the seller can't stay as an owner, officer or employee, only as a consultant for up to 24 months.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-seller-consultant]

**Owner Buyout, Partial Change.** A 7(a) loan can fund buying all or part of an owner's interest when at least one original owner stays as an owner and guarantees the loan.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-partial-change-definition] Here the seller may stay on as an owner, officer or employee.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-partial-seller-stays]

### The limit for newcomers

In any owner buyout, a person who hasn't been employed by the business for at least 24 months may acquire less than 50% and can't become the largest shareholder.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-owner-buyout-outsider-limit] A deal that breaks that limit is underwritten to Initial Acquisition standards: the minimum coverage ratio, the minimum equity injection and the quality of earnings requirement.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-owner-buyout-outsider-consequence]

So a plan like "buy 60% now and the rest in three years" doesn't fit the partial-change category for an outsider. Raise it with the lender before you put it in an LOI.

### Guaranties in a partial change

- **New owners are co-borrowers.** Every new owner, whatever the stake, signs as a co-borrower alongside the business.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-partial-co-borrowers]
- **Owners of 20% or more guarantee**, as on any SBA loan.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-personal-guaranty]
- **A selling owner left with under 20%** must give a full guaranty of the whole loan for at least two years after final disbursement.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-partial-change-guaranty] SBA doesn't require that seller to pledge a personal residence for a collateral shortfall.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-partial-guaranty-no-residence]

A seller may balk at guaranteeing a loan that paid them, so raise it early.

### Structures SBA won't finance

A "multi-step" partial change, where existing owners and a new owner form a new entity that buys 100% of the operating company, isn't eligible.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-multi-step-partial] A holding company deal that mixes old and new owners needs a lender's read on how it will be classified.

### If you want the seller to keep a stake

Two paths to discuss with your lender: buy 100% with SBA financing and have the seller hold a note instead of equity (see [seller financing](/q/seller-financing-basics)), or to finance without SBA so a rollover is allowed (see [rollover equity](/q/rollover-equity) and [partial buyouts and recaps](/q/partial-buy-recap)). Which one works depends on your lender, your price and the seller's goals.
