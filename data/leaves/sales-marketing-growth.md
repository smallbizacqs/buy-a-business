**Short answer.** Start with the customers you already have: keep them, sell them more, and ask them for referrals. Then find out where your best customers came from and put more effort there, before trying new channels. If the business grew on the seller's reputation and word of mouth with little deliberate selling, basic steps (answering every lead quickly, following up on quotes, a working website and reviews) can matter more than a big new campaign.

### First, see where sales come from

In the first months, find out:

- Which customers and services produce most of the revenue and margin.
- Where new customers came from (referrals, repeat, search, signs, a partner, a salesperson) and which sources bring the best customers.
- How leads are handled: who answers, how fast, how quotes are followed up, and how many quotes turn into jobs.
- Who did the selling. If it was the seller, someone has to take that over. See [making the business run without you](/q/professionalize-systems).

If the business doesn't track this, start now. A simple customer list or CRM (customer relationship management software) with the lead source, quote date, amount and outcome is enough to begin.

### Existing customers first

- **Retention.** Every customer lost offsets a new one won. See [keeping customers](/q/customer-retention).
- **More from each.** Offer services they don't buy from you yet, maintenance plans or agreements, and better options at a higher price.
- **Reactivation.** Contact past customers who stopped buying.
- **Referrals.** Ask satisfied customers directly, and make it easy.

### Fix the basics

- Answer calls and inquiries quickly, including after hours.
- Follow up on every quote.
- Keep the website, business listings and hours accurate.
- Ask happy customers for reviews.

**Review and endorsement rules.** Under an FTC rule, a business may not pay or give incentives for reviews conditioned on expressing a particular sentiment, positive or negative.[^ecfr-16-cfr-465-4-paid-reviews] Endorsements you use in advertising must reflect the endorser's honest opinions or experience.[^ecfr-16-cfr-255-1-endorsements] Ask every customer for an honest review, not only the happy ones you hope will be positive, and don't reward a good one.

### Then add channels deliberately

Pick one or two new channels that fit how your customers buy: local search ads, commercial outreach to property managers or general contractors, trade partnerships, a salesperson for larger accounts, or industry events. For each, set a budget and a test period, track leads and revenue by source, and keep what pays back.

### Hiring a salesperson

A salesperson can grow a business that relied on the owner's relationships, but give them a defined market, a pipeline to track, realistic time to ramp up and a pay plan tied to results you can measure. See [hiring and strengthening the team](/q/hiring-team).

### Pricing and capacity

Growth that the team can't deliver hurts service. Check capacity (people, equipment, scheduling) before pushing volume, and look at pricing alongside sales; sometimes the better lever is a higher price, not more jobs. See [raising prices](/q/raise-prices).

### Common mistakes

- Spending on new marketing before fixing slow response and missed follow-ups.
- Changing the brand or name in the first months, losing the recognition that brings referrals.
- Measuring leads instead of profitable jobs.
- Growing revenue in low-margin work.

See [increasing the value of the business](/q/value-creation) and [marketing diligence](/q/marketing-dd).
