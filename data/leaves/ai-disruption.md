**Short answer.** Break the business into what customers pay for and who does the work, then ask, for each piece, whether software could do it well enough, cheaply enough, that customers would stop paying or pay less. Work that is physical, local, regulated or relationship-based is harder to replace. Work that is mostly producing documents, answers, content or routine digital tasks is more exposed. Exposure isn't automatically bad; it can also mean lower costs for you.

**Three questions**

1. **Will customers still need this?** If a customer could get the same result from an AI tool themselves, demand may shrink or prices may fall. Think of basic content writing, simple bookkeeping, transcription, routine design and first-line support.
2. **Will competitors use it to undercut you?** Even if customers still buy the service, a competitor using AI may deliver it faster or cheaper. Your margins are at risk if you can't match them.
3. **Can you use it to cut costs?** Scheduling, quoting, billing, customer messages and back-office work may get cheaper. That's upside for an owner who adopts it, and you shouldn't pay the seller for it. See [hidden upside](/q/hidden-upside).

**Less exposed vs. more exposed**

- **Less exposed:** hands-on work at a customer's site, licensed trades, work needing physical equipment, local service with relationships and trust, regulated activities, and anything where the customer pays for accountability, not just output.
- **More exposed:** businesses whose product is information, text, images or standard digital tasks; lead generation that depends on search traffic; staffing for routine office work; and services customers buy mainly on price.

Many businesses are a mix. A plumbing company is barely exposed in the field but may use AI in dispatch and billing. A marketing agency may lose some simple work and gain capacity on the rest.

**How to check a specific target**

- List revenue by service line and mark each line's exposure.
- Look at recent trends in those lines: falling prices or volumes in exposed lines are an early sign. See [TTM and trends](/q/trends-ttm).
- Ask customers how they decide to buy and what would make them switch. See [customer calls](/q/customer-dd-calls).
- Check where new customers come from. If it's search, ask how that traffic has changed.
- Look at the staff mix. Roles doing routine digital work could be done differently.

**In the price**

Where a quality of earnings report is required for an SBA loan, it assesses whether revenue and margins are likely to hold after the sale.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-qoe-revenue-quality] Under SOP 50 10 8.1 (effective October 1, 2026), the lender can't rely on your projections to meet coverage,[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-projections] so a story about future efficiencies won't raise what you can borrow. Pay for today's earnings, discount exposed lines, and treat AI savings as yours to earn.
