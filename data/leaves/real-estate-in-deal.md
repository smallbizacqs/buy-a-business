**Short answer.** Yes. Under SOP 50 10 8.1 (effective October 1, 2026), an SBA acquisition that includes the seller's building is financed either as one 7(a) loan with a blended term or as two separate loans, one of which can be a 504.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-real-estate-separate-or-blended] Only the real estate share can amortize past 10 years, up to 25.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-real-estate-structure] Your business must occupy at least 51% of an existing building.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-occupancy]

### The two structures

- **Blended 7(a).** One loan, with a maturity weighted by use of proceeds. Real estate may go up to 25 years; everything else, including soft costs and working capital, gets 10, and the lender figures the blend before applying equity.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-real-estate-structure] An acquisition loan may include owner-occupied real estate only on this blended basis.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-coo-working-capital-use]
- **Separate loans.** One loan for the business and one for the building, which can be a 504.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-real-estate-separate-or-blended] See [7(a) or 504](/q/7a-vs-504).

The total still has to fit within SBA limits; the largest Standard 7(a) loan is $5 million.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-max-loan] See [larger deals](/q/loan-limits-pari-passu).

### Rules that apply when the building is in the deal

- **Occupancy.** For an existing building, the business must occupy at least 51% and may lease up to 49% to others.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-occupancy] A building that's mostly tenants is an investment property, not something SBA finances with the business.
- **Appraisal.** You'll need a commercial real estate appraisal that meets SBA's requirements.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-real-estate-appraisal] Budget the cost and time.
- **The price gets split.** For SBA's due diligence tests, the "business purchase price" excludes owner-occupied real estate; the lender removes the appraised value of the real estate from the contract price.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-business-purchase-price] That matters for the $3 million QoE threshold.
- **Equity applies to the building too.** Real estate bought with the business is subject to the same equity requirement, allocated pro rata across separate loans, except that a 504 loan follows 504 rules.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-real-estate-equity]
- **Coverage gets a rent add-back.** When the building is part of the deal, the lender may add back the rent the business was paying.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-rent-addback] Your new mortgage payment replaces it in debt service.
- **Collateral.** Real estate is a fixed asset that counts toward SBA's "fully secured" test.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-fully-secured]

### Special purpose properties

When the business owns and operates from a special purpose property (one built for a specific use) that can't be separated from the business, the loan can run up to 25 years when 85% or more of project costs are real estate.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-special-purpose-25] No QoE is required for these acquisitions, whatever the price.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-qoe-special-purpose]

### What to work out before the LOI

1. Whether the seller will sell the building, lease it to you, or give you an option to buy later. See [lease or buy](/q/real-estate-lease-or-buy).
2. A separate price for the building and for the business, so the allocation isn't left to the appraisal.
3. How much of the building your business uses, measured the way SBA measures it.
4. What environmental review the lender will require on the property. See [environmental diligence](/q/environmental).
5. Quotes for both the blended and the split structure from your lender.
