**Short answer.** Build one spreadsheet with five parts: sources and uses (where every dollar comes from and goes), a debt schedule for each loan, a cash flow forecast built up from the business's verified historical earnings, coverage and returns, and scenarios. The lender cares whether cash flow covers all debt payments, the seller cares whether the note gets paid, and you care what's left for your salary and equity after both. If the model works only in the best case, the deal doesn't work.

### 1. Sources and uses

**Uses:** purchase price, working capital you'll add, closing costs, lender and SBA fees, the valuation and QoE, legal and accounting fees, and a cash reserve. An SBA acquisition loan can include working capital and your out-of-pocket costs such as the valuation and QoE.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-coo-working-capital-use] The SBA upfront guaranty fee is figured on the guaranteed portion and can be passed to you.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-upfront-fee]

**Sources:** senior loan, seller note, your cash, investor equity. For an SBA deal, check the equity line: at least 10% for an initial acquisition,[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-equity-minimum] measured against total project cost, not just the price.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-equity-project-cost] Sources must equal uses to the dollar.

### 2. Debt schedule

One block per loan: amount, rate, amortization, payment start date, and annual principal and interest.

- **SBA term loan.** A 10-year amortization with no balloon for the business portion;[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-change-of-ownership-term] any real estate portion can run up to 25 years.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-real-estate-structure] Model the variable rate at a realistic spread and at higher rates. SBA caps the variable rate at the base rate plus a spread set by loan size.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-max-variable-rates]
- **Seller note.** If it counts as SBA equity it pays nothing during the SBA loan term,[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-seller-debt-equity] so model zero payments. If it pays, include it in debt service. See [seller note terms](/q/seller-note-terms).
- **Lines of credit**, if any, with expected draws and interest.

### 3. Cash flow

Start from historical, verified earnings (EBITDA or seller's discretionary earnings, adjusted for add-backs you can defend). See [SDE vs EBITDA](/q/sde-vs-ebitda) and [add-backs](/q/add-backs). Then subtract:

- A market salary for you or the manager who replaces the seller. Your pay must cover your personal obligations.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-owner-compensation]
- Capital expenditures the business needs to stay as it is.
- Taxes at the entity or owner level, as your CPA advises.
- New costs you'll add: insurance, software, accounting, a manager.

### 4. Coverage and returns

- **Debt service coverage** = cash flow ÷ total annual debt payments. SBA's historical test uses EBITDA divided by all debt service after the deal, not just the SBA loan.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-dsc-definition] The minimum for an initial acquisition is 1.25:1.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-dsc-ratio] Interest-only acquisition debt not on full standby is counted as if amortized over no more than 10 years.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-interest-only-debt] See [DSCR](/q/dscr).
- **Total debt check.** In an SBA deal, all debt, including seller debt not on full standby, can't exceed the business valuation.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-total-debt-cap]
- **Global cash flow** including your personal debts and other businesses must cover at least 1:1 for an SBA lender.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-global-cash-flow]
- **Your returns.** Cash left after debt service and your salary each year, debt paid down, and value at a future exit at a conservative multiple. Show cash-on-cash, IRR and multiple on your equity, and the same for investors through the waterfall. See [investor terms](/q/investor-terms).

### 5. Scenarios

Build at least three cases (base, downside and severe) by changing revenue, gross margin, a lost top customer, a higher interest rate, and a slower transition. Find the revenue decline at which coverage drops below 1.0, so you know how much room you have. See [stress-testing a deal](/q/stress-test).

### What each party reads

| Reader | What they look for |
|---|---|
| Lender | Historical coverage, sources and uses, equity. The lender must look at your projections but can't rely on them to meet coverage.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-projections] |
| Seller | That the price and note payments are realistic and the business can carry them |
| Investors | Downside case, waterfall and returns |
| You | Your salary, your cash at risk, and how bad things can get before you miss a payment |

Keep one version, label every assumption, and tie each historical figure to its source document.
