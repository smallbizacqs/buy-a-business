**Short answer.** Treat the cost of keeping equipment and vehicles running and replacing them on schedule as a real, recurring expense, even though SDE and EBITDA leave it out. Inspect what you're buying, build a replacement schedule, and subtract the yearly average from earnings when you judge the price. Equipment that's worn out or overdue for replacement is a cost to negotiate into the price.

**Why earnings figures hide it**

EBITDA (earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation and amortization) excludes depreciation, and SDE adds it back. Depreciation is how the cost of equipment is spread over the years it's used,[^irs-pub-946-depreciation] so those measures look the same whether the trucks are new or about to die. The cash cost of replacing them is still coming.

**Maintenance vs. growth spending**

- **Maintenance capital spending (capex)** keeps the business doing what it does now: replacing vans, compressors, computers.
- **Growth capex** adds capacity: a new truck for a new crew.

Only maintenance capex reduces the earnings you're buying. Growth capex is a choice you make later.

**How to estimate it**

1. Get a full asset list: each vehicle and major piece of equipment, its age, mileage or hours, and condition.
2. Inspect it, or have a mechanic or equipment specialist do so for expensive items.
3. Estimate each item's remaining useful life and replacement cost from current quotes.
4. Add up replacements over the next several years and convert to a yearly average.
5. Compare with what the seller has actually spent per year. Spending well below your estimate can mean deferred maintenance.

**Deferred maintenance**

When an owner preparing to sell stops replacing things, earnings look better and you inherit the bill. Under SOP 50 10 8.1 (effective October 1, 2026), a required quality of earnings report must document deferred maintenance,[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-qoe-revenue-quality] and SBA lenders may adjust cash flow for unfunded capital expenditures.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-adjustment-items] SBA debt coverage itself is calculated on EBITDA,[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-dsc-definition] so check that your own model leaves room for capex after loan payments.

**In the price and structure**

- **Price.** Reduce the price for near-term replacements the seller skipped, or ask the seller to replace items before closing.
- **Allocation.** The SBA business valuation must put separate values on land, buildings, equipment and intangible assets.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-valuation-allocation] Equipment value also matters as collateral: a 7(a) loan is fully secured when liens on fixed assets, including machinery and equipment, reach the loan amount.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-fully-secured]
- **Leases and loans.** Check whether vehicles or equipment are leased or financed, and whether those agreements transfer or must be paid off.
- **Working capital.** Keep cash set aside for breakdowns you didn't plan for.
