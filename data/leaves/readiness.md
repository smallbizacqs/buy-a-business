**Short answer.** You're close to ready when you can fund the equity and still keep a cash cushion, you've accepted the personal guarantee and what it can reach, you're comfortable being the person every problem lands on, and your household has agreed to a period of lower income and long hours. If any of those is a "not yet", fix it before you start sending offers, not after.

This is a self-assessment. The lender's own eligibility test is separate; see [eligibility and credit](/q/eligibility-credit).

### Money

- **Equity.** With an SBA 7(a) loan, under SOP 50 10 8.1 (effective October 1, 2026), an initial acquisition needs at least 10% equity from the buyer, and the lender can't reduce or waive it.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-equity-minimum] See [equity injection](/q/equity-injection).
- **Beyond the equity.** You'll also pay for diligence, legal work and loan fees, and you'll want a personal reserve for surprises after closing. See [search budget](/q/search-budget) and [closing costs](/q/closing-costs).
- **Living costs.** The lender checks that the pay you'll draw from the business covers your obligations and living expenses.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-owner-compensation] Know your household's real monthly number before you look at deals.
- **Full disclosure.** Owners of 20% or more give the lender a personal financial statement that includes a spouse's and minor children's assets.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-owner-financial-statement] If you're uneasy putting your whole balance sheet on paper, that's a sign to slow down.

### Risk

Under the same SOP, every individual owning 20% or more of the borrower gives an unlimited personal guarantee.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-personal-guaranty] Ask yourself whether you could live with the worst case, not just the expected one. See [personal guarantees](/q/personal-guarantee) and [what happens when a deal fails](/q/failure-stories).

### Temperament

Owners of small businesses deal with ambiguity, people problems and cash pressure, often all in the same week. Signs you'll handle it:

- You can make a decision with incomplete information and change course without drama.
- You like working with people outside your profession: frontline staff, tradespeople, customers who complain.
- You can sell, because in a small business the owner is often the main salesperson, at least at first.
- You can sit with a bad month without panicking or hiding.

See [owner-operator skills](/q/operator-skills).

### Timing

- **Household.** Is your spouse or partner on board with the money at risk and the hours? See [talking it through with family](/q/spouse-family).
- **Life events.** A new baby, a move or a health issue in the next year or two changes how much you can take on.
- **Your job.** Can you search while employed, and when would you leave? See [keeping your day job](/q/keep-day-job).
- **The search itself.** A search can take longer than you plan. Could you keep going if it does? See [search timeline](/q/search-timeline).

### Signs to wait

- You'd need every dollar you have for the equity, with nothing left over.
- You're counting on the business to pay you more than your current salary in year one. See [the pay dip after closing](/q/j-curve).
- You're buying to escape a job rather than to run a business.
- Your partner hasn't agreed, or doesn't know the size of the guarantee.
