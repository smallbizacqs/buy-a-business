**Short answer.** Look for things the owner hasn't done because of age, habit or lack of time: prices not raised in years, no marketing, no follow-up with past customers, paper systems, services customers ask for but the business doesn't offer. Note them, estimate them conservatively, and keep them out of the price. Upside is what you're paid for the work and risk of buying; if you pay the seller for it, you've given it away.

**Where to look**

- **Pricing.** When were prices last raised? How do they compare with competitors' published rates? Are there jobs or customers priced below cost? See [raising prices](/q/raise-prices).
- **Marketing and sales.** No website, a dated one, no reviews strategy, no one following up on quotes, no outreach to past customers.
- **Recurring revenue.** Service plans, maintenance contracts or subscriptions that could be added to one-off work.
- **Collections and billing.** Slow invoicing, old receivables, no deposits on large jobs.
- **Operations.** Paper scheduling, manual quoting, no routing, no job costing. Software can save hours.
- **Services and geography.** Requests the business turns away, or nearby areas it doesn't serve.
- **Purchasing.** Supplier prices never renegotiated; no use of buying groups.
- **Capacity.** Equipment or space that sits idle part of the week.
- **Team.** A capable employee who could take on more with training and authority.

**How to find it in diligence**

- Ask the owner, "If you were 20 years younger, what would you do with this business?" Many owners know exactly what they've left undone.
- Read reviews for repeated complaints and requests.
- Mystery-shop the business and its competitors.
- Compare prices, hours, services and online presence with competitors.
- Ask customers what else they'd buy. See [customer calls](/q/customer-dd-calls).

**Why you shouldn't pay for it**

The seller didn't earn it, and you'll carry the cost and risk of capturing it. An SBA lender won't count it either: under SOP 50 10 8.1 (effective October 1, 2026), the lender must review your projections but can't rely on them to meet the coverage requirement.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-projections] If you agree to a price above the independent valuation because of upside, the difference must come from your own equity.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-valuation-gap-equity] When a seller argues for a higher price because of growth potential, a fair reply is: "That's the part I'm buying the right to work for."

**Be realistic about capturing it**

- Each improvement takes time, money or both, and some fail.
- Changing too much too quickly can cost you employees and customers. See [don't change too fast](/q/dont-change-too-fast).
- If the owner hasn't raised prices in years, customers may be loyal partly because of that.
- Rank ideas by size, cost and risk, and plan one or two for the first year. See [first 100 days](/q/first-100-days-plan) and [value creation](/q/value-creation).
