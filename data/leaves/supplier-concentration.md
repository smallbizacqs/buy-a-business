**Short answer.** Find out what share of the business's costs, products or leads come through one supplier, distributor, franchisor or platform, read the agreement that governs it, and ask what happens on the day it ends or changes terms. If losing that relationship would cripple the business, price the risk in, line up alternatives before closing, and confirm the relationship transfers to you.

**Where dependence hides**

- **A sole supplier** of a product the business resells or a part it can't easily substitute.
- **An exclusive dealer or distributor agreement**, including territory rights that make up much of the value.
- **A platform** that supplies customers or sales: a marketplace, an app store, a search or ad channel, a referral network.
- **A franchisor or brand license** that sets terms for the whole business. See [franchise transfer](/q/franchise-transfer).
- **A key subcontractor** who does work the business sells as its own.
- **Software or a payment processor** the business can't operate without.

**What to check**

1. **Spend and revenue share.** Rank suppliers by spend, and by how much revenue depends on each.
2. **The agreement.** Term, renewal, termination rights, exclusivity, minimum purchases, price change clauses, and whether it can be assigned to a new owner or needs consent. See [contract assignment](/q/contract-assignment).
3. **Change-of-control terms.** Some agreements let the supplier end or reprice on a sale, even in a stock purchase.
4. **The relationship.** Is it with the business or with the owner personally? Informal discounts, credit terms or priority allocation may rest on a handshake.
5. **History.** Past price increases, shortages, policy changes, account suspensions.
6. **Alternatives.** Who else could supply it, at what cost, and how long switching would take.

**Why it matters to the price**

A supplier who can raise prices or cut you off can take your margin. Where a quality of earnings report is required for an SBA loan, it assesses whether revenue and margins are likely to hold after the sale,[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-qoe-revenue-quality] and under SOP 50 10 8.1 (effective October 1, 2026) the lender's file includes the purchase agreement with all schedules,[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-purchase-agreement-in-file] which is where key contracts are scheduled.

**Ways to reduce the risk**

- **Talk to the supplier before closing**, with the seller's agreement, and get written consent to assignment where needed.
- **Make consent a closing condition** in the purchase agreement. See [closing conditions](/q/closing-conditions).
- **Ask for a seller representation** that no key supplier has said it will end or change the relationship. See [reps and warranties](/q/reps-warranties).
- **Price it.** Lower the multiple, or keep part of the price in a seller note or holdback.
- **Plan a second source** in your first year.
