**Short answer.** Tell the people owners already trust (accountants, lawyers, bankers, wealth advisers, insurance agents) exactly what you're looking for, in a way that makes it easy and safe for them to mention you to a client. Referrals come slowly and from relationships, so start early, be specific, follow up, and always close the loop on anything they send you.

**Who sees owners thinking about selling**

- **Accountants (CPAs) and bookkeepers.** They see the numbers, hear about retirement plans, and may know early that an owner is tired.
- **Estate planning and business lawyers.** Succession, wills and partner disputes all raise the question of what happens to the business.
- **Bankers and lenders.** Including lenders that make acquisition loans, who meet sellers and buyers and see deals fall through.
- **Wealth advisers and financial planners.** Owners planning retirement talk about turning the business into cash.
- **Insurance agents.** Especially commercial agents in your target industry.
- **Business brokers who don't list your size.** A broker focused on larger deals may pass along smaller owners.
- **People in the industry.** Suppliers, distributors, equipment dealers, trade association staff, and owners who've already sold.
- **Your own network.** Former colleagues, classmates and friends who own businesses or know someone who does.

**How to ask**

1. **Be specific.** A one-page summary: industry, size range, geography, what you'd do with the business, and how you'd pay. "Any business" gives a referrer nothing to match.
2. **Make it safe to refer.** A professional's first duty is to their client. Explain how you'd treat the owner: confidentiality, respect for employees, a fair process. Offer to sign an NDA (nondisclosure agreement) before any details are shared.
3. **Make it worth their while, without strings.** Some referrers will hope to keep the client after the sale; if you'd consider keeping the business's accountant or lawyer, say so honestly, and don't promise what you won't do.
4. **Ask for a meeting, not a list.** A coffee or call where you learn how they work with owners builds more trust than an email.
5. **Follow up regularly.** A short update every month or two on what you've seen and learned keeps you in mind without being a nuisance.
6. **Close the loop.** If they send someone, tell them what happened, quickly, even if it's a no.

**Paying for referrals**

If you're considering a finder's fee, ask the professional first whether they can accept one and whether they'd need to tell their client. Put any fee arrangement in writing and agree when it's earned (for example, only on a closed deal). Some referrers would rather not be paid at all because it could look like a conflict with their client.

**Where referrals fit**

Referrals tend to bring owners who are further along in their thinking, with a trusted adviser already involved, which can make the first conversation easier. They're also slow and unpredictable, so run them alongside direct outreach: see [finding owners whose businesses aren't listed](/q/off-market-sourcing) and [earning an owner's trust](/q/earn-trust).
