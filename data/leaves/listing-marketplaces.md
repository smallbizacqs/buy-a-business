**Short answer.** Several websites list businesses for sale. Some focus on main-street and lower-middle-market businesses posted by brokers and owners (BizBuySell is one); others focus on online businesses such as software, e-commerce and content (for example Acquire.com, Flippa and TrustMRR). They differ in what they show and what, if anything, they verify. None of them is a party to your purchase, and the listing is the seller's description, not checked facts.

This page describes what a few marketplaces show; it isn't a ranking or a recommendation.

**BizBuySell**

Listings of business opportunities posted by the owner or by a licensed broker or agent the owner has engaged under an exclusive listing agreement.[^bizbuysell-terms-who-lists] Its terms say it doesn't broker or sell businesses and isn't a party to the transaction,[^bizbuysell-terms-not-a-party] and that it has no obligation to verify the accuracy of what others post.[^bizbuysell-terms-no-verification]

**Acquire.com**

Online businesses: SaaS, e-commerce, agencies, content, newsletters, mobile apps and crypto businesses.[^acquire-home-categories] Its terms say it isn't a party to the transaction[^acquire-terms-not-a-party] and isn't responsible for the truthfulness or completeness of sellers' listings.[^acquire-terms-listing-accuracy]

**Flippa**

Online assets and businesses, listed either as auctions or at a fixed price.[^flippa-terms-listing-types] A "Data Verified" badge means the seller has connected at least one third-party data source such as analytics or payments;[^flippa-terms-data-verified] Flippa's terms say that verification isn't a substitute for your own due diligence,[^flippa-terms-not-diligence] and that it isn't a party to the sale contract.[^flippa-terms-role]

**TrustMRR**

SaaS and other startups, with revenue shown from connected payment providers such as Stripe and the App Store.[^trustmrr-home-verified-revenue] Connected revenue data is a starting point; you still need to check costs, churn, and who owns the code, accounts and customers.

**Using any marketplace**

- Read the listing as the seller's pitch. Verify earnings against tax returns and bank statements; see [proof of cash](/q/proof-of-cash).
- Check who you're dealing with: an owner, a broker working for the owner, or the platform's own brokerage service.
- Read the platform's terms on fees, exclusivity and where payments must go.
- Many businesses never appear on a marketplace at all; see [finding owners who aren't listed](/q/off-market-sourcing).
