**Short answer.** Rollover equity means the seller keeps a minority stake in the business instead of taking all of the price in cash. It can keep the seller invested in the business doing well and reduce the cash you need at closing. It also means you have a co-owner, with rights you have to agree on, and SBA rules limit it in the most common kind of SBA-financed purchase. An SBA 7(a) loan is a bank loan partly guaranteed by the U.S. Small Business Administration (SBA).

**Why buyers consider it:**

- Less cash and debt needed at closing.
- The seller has a reason to help after closing.
- It can show the seller believes in the business.

**What to work out:**

- **Rights**: voting, board seats, information rights, and what decisions need the seller's approval.
- **Distributions**: how and when profits are paid out, including tax distributions.
- **Exit**: when and how you can buy the rest (a call option), when the seller can make you buy it (a put option), and how the price is set then.
- **Transfers**: what happens if the seller dies, divorces, or wants to sell to someone else.
- **Non-compete**: what the seller can and can't do while still an owner.

**With an SBA 7(a) loan.** SBA groups change-of-ownership deals into four types: initial acquisition, business expansion, owner buyout (including partial changes) and ESOP (employee stock ownership plan) or cooperative.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-change-types] Under SBA SOP 50 10 8.1 (effective October 1, 2026), in an initial acquisition or business expansion the seller may not remain as an officer, director, stockholder or employee, though the business may hire them as a consultant for up to 24 months.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-seller-consultant] In a partial change of ownership, a selling owner who keeps less than 20% must guarantee the full loan for at least two years after final disbursement.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-partial-change-guaranty] Which category your deal falls in is the lender's call; see also [buying a majority now and the rest later](/q/partial-buy-recap).
