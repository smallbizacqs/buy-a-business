**Short answer.** The costly ones are terms left vague that later become fights (working capital, what's included, the seller's role), terms that don't fit your financing (structures your lender won't accept), and an offer made before you understand the business well enough to stand behind it. A letter of intent (LOI) is mostly non-binding, but it sets expectations; anything you have to change later reads to the seller as a price cut.

**Mistakes on price and structure**

- **No working capital terms.** If the LOI doesn't say what working capital comes with the business, you may get it stripped of receivables and cash before closing. State a target or a method. See [the peg](/q/peg) and [working capital](/q/what-is-nwc).
- **Not saying what's included.** Equipment, vehicles, inventory, real estate, intellectual property, cash, debt. "Cash-free, debt-free" should be stated if that's the deal. See [cash-free, debt-free](/q/cash-free-debt-free).
- **Structures your lender won't accept.** Under SOP 50 10 8.1 (effective October 1, 2026), seller earnouts are prohibited in an SBA 7(a) acquisition (buyer rebates are allowed),[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-earnouts] a seller note counts toward equity only if it's on full standby,[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-seller-debt-equity] and interest-only seller debt is counted as if it amortized over no more than 10 years.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-interest-only-debt] Show your lender the term sheet before you sign.
- **A price you can't finance.** If the price exceeds the lender's independent valuation, the gap must come from your equity.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-valuation-gap-equity] Build in a financing condition.
- **Asset vs. stock left open.** It changes taxes, liabilities and contracts. See [asset vs. stock](/q/asset-vs-stock).

**Mistakes on the seller's role**

- **No transition terms.** How long, how many hours, paid or unpaid. With an SBA loan in an initial acquisition, the seller can stay only as a consultant, for up to 24 months in total.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-seller-consultant] Promising a longer role or ongoing ownership can break the financing. See [seller transition](/q/seller-transition-period).
- **No non-compete outline.** Raise it now so it isn't a surprise later. See [non-compete](/q/non-compete).

**Mistakes on process**

- **Exclusivity that's too short**, or no exclusivity at all. See [exclusivity](/q/exclusivity).
- **No diligence scope or access terms.** Say what you'll need: records, site visits, customer and employee conversations at the right time.
- **No timeline.** Target dates for diligence, financing and closing keep things moving. See [keeping a deal moving](/q/deal-momentum).
- **Binding terms by accident.** Make clear which sections bind (for example confidentiality, exclusivity and costs) and which don't.

**Mistakes in approach**

- **Offering high to win, planning to cut later.** Sellers remember the first number, and retrading damages trust. See [retrades](/q/retrade) and [what to settle before diligence](/q/settle-before-diligence).
- **Offering before seeing enough.** Get the basics first. See [diligence before the LOI](/q/pre-loi-diligence).
- **Burying bad news.** If you already know something lowers the price, say so in the LOI.

**Getting help**

Some buyers handle small deals with templates, for example openagreements.org. Buyers who hire counsel can look for AI-using law firms with fixed fees that are lean and run by an attorney with big-law experience. See [what goes in an LOI](/q/loi-contents).
