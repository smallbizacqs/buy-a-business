**Short answer.** Brokers are paid by sellers and have limited time, so they prioritize buyers who look likely to close: people who can show how they'll pay, who know what they're looking for, who respond quickly and who explain why this particular business suits them.

**Why credibility matters to a broker**

A business broker usually works for the seller. On BizBuySell, for example, a listing may be posted only by the owner or by a licensed broker or agent the owner has engaged under an exclusive listing agreement.[^bizbuysell-terms-who-lists] A broker's job is to get the seller's deal closed, so they sort inquiries by who seems serious and able.

**What brokers commonly look for**

- **Proof you can pay.** Evidence of the cash you'd put in, and a realistic financing plan (for example, a conversation already started with a lender).
- **A clear buy box.** The size, industry and location you're after, so the broker can tell whether this listing fits and send you future ones. See [defining a buy box](/q/define-buy-box).
- **Relevant background.** Why your experience fits this business, or how you'd cover the gaps.
- **Speed and follow-through.** Signing the nondisclosure agreement (NDA) promptly, reading what they send, and asking specific questions.
- **A short, specific first note.** Who you are, how you'd pay, and why this business.

**What tends to cost credibility**

Requesting every listing with a form letter, asking for documents before reading the summary, opening with a lowball price, or going quiet after receiving confidential information.

## What practitioners say
- SMBash suggests that a searcher's note to a broker should explain the buyer's connection to the business.[^c406151]
