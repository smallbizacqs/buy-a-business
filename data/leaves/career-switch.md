**Short answer.** People move from banking, tech, consulting and the military into ownership by saving the equity while still employed, learning how small-company deals work, searching (for some, part-time at first), and leaving once a deal is real. What carries over is analysis, leadership and discipline; what you may have to learn is selling, managing frontline staff and making decisions without a large organization behind you.

### What carries over, and what doesn't

**Banking and finance.** You'll read statements, model a deal and speak a lender's language. The gap is the operating side: hiring, scheduling, handling a customer complaint on a Saturday. Don't expect clean data or formal reporting in a small business; you may have to build it.

**Tech.** Systems thinking, software and comfort with data transfer well. The gap is pace and workforce: a small business may run on paper, phone calls and long-tenured staff who don't welcome sudden change. See [don't change too fast](/q/dont-change-too-fast).

**Consulting.** Diagnosing problems and presenting a plan carry over. The gap is execution: as owner you implement the recommendation yourself, then live with it. You'll also do work no consultant would bill for.

**Military.** Leading people, running operations and staying calm under pressure carry over strongly. The gap tends to be commercial: pricing, selling, negotiating with suppliers and reading a profit and loss statement. SBA's Boots to Business training is part of the Defense Department's transition program, and Boots to Business Reboot extends it to veterans of all eras.[^sba-veterans-boots-to-business]

### The steps

1. **Save while employed.** Your salary funds the equity and the search budget. See [readiness](/q/readiness) and [search budget](/q/search-budget).
2. **Learn the basics.** How small deals are valued and financed. See [what entrepreneurship through acquisition is](/q/what-is-eta).
3. **Pick a path.** Self-funded, investor-backed or with a partner. See [choosing your path](/q/choose-path).
4. **Search part-time or full-time.** See [keeping your day job](/q/keep-day-job).
5. **Leave when the deal is real.** Points buyers use include a signed letter of intent, a lender's commitment, or closing itself.

### How lenders see your background

For an SBA 7(a) loan, under SOP 50 10 8.1 (effective October 1, 2026), the lender's credit memo describes your industry experience and management team and who will run daily operations.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-management-experience] A corporate résumé helps when it shows you've managed people, budgets and results. Translate titles into what you actually ran. See [buying outside your industry](/q/no-industry-experience).

### Common mistakes

- Expecting the business to behave like your old employer, with staff functions to lean on.
- Changing too much in the first months to prove yourself.
- Underestimating how much selling the owner does. See [owner-operator skills](/q/operator-skills).
- Using the same professional vocabulary with employees and sellers that you used at work; plain talk earns trust faster. See [earning an owner's trust](/q/earn-trust).
