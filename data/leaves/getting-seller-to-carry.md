**Short answer.** Start by finding out why the seller is reluctant: fear of not being paid, wanting a clean break, or needing the cash now. Each has a different answer, from security and reporting for the note, to a shorter or smaller note, to a different way of keeping the seller involved, such as a paid consulting agreement.

**Common reasons and the responses buyers use**

- **"What if you don't pay me?"** Explain your plan, your experience and your own money at risk. Offer regular financial reporting, a personal guaranty on the note, or a security interest behind the bank. Show the coverage math.
- **"I want to be done."** A smaller note, a shorter term, or a note that starts paying after the bank loan has seasoned.
- **"I need the money at closing."** Show what the bank will and won't lend. Sometimes the gap is a choice between a note and a lower price.
- **"My adviser told me not to."** Ask what specifically worries the adviser; it's often a term that can be fixed.

**Limits when there's an SBA loan** (SOP 50 10 8.1, effective October 1, 2026)

- A note counts as equity only if it's on full standby, with no principal or interest paid for the life of the 7(a) loan.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-seller-debt-equity] Many sellers dislike that, so be clear early about whether you need the note as equity or as ordinary subordinated debt.
- Seller earnouts (extra price paid later if the business hits targets) are prohibited.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-earnouts]
- In a full purchase the seller can't stay as an owner or employee, but the business may hire the seller as a consultant for up to 24 months in total.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-seller-consultant] So a longer consulting term doesn't fit an SBA-financed full purchase. How a consulting agreement is written (pay for real work, not a hidden part of the price) is a question for your lawyer and the lender.

## What practitioners say
- Barlow & Williams describe a deal where the seller refused a seller note and the buyer proposed a three-year consulting agreement instead.[^c453de6]
