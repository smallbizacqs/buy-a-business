**Short answer.** Both measure earnings before financing and taxes, but they treat the owner differently. Seller's discretionary earnings (SDE) adds the owner's pay back, so it shows what one full-time owner-operator would have to cover their salary, debt payments and return. EBITDA (earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation and amortization) treats the owner's role as a paid job, so it shows what's left after paying someone to run the business. SDE is common in listings for smaller owner-operated businesses; EBITDA is common for larger ones with managers in place.

**How each is built**

- **EBITDA:** net income, plus interest, taxes, depreciation and amortization, plus agreed adjustments (non-recurring or personal items). The owner's job is paid at a market salary.
- **SDE:** EBITDA plus one owner's total compensation (salary, payroll taxes and benefits), plus discretionary expenses the owner ran through the business.

SDE has no official definition, and listings calculate it differently. Ask for the calculation line by line.

**Which one the lender uses**

Under SOP 50 10 8.1 (effective October 1, 2026), the SBA defines historical debt service coverage as EBITDA divided by combined debt service after the transaction.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-dsc-definition] The lender may justify adjustments, including seller discretionary expenses and owner compensation,[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-adjustment-items] and your own pay from the business must cover your obligations and living expenses.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-owner-compensation] So an SBA lender starts from EBITDA and decides which owner-related items to add back, rather than accepting a listing's SDE figure.

**Why the difference matters**

- An SDE figure isn't free cash: you still have to pay yourself (or a manager) out of it. See [owner replacement cost](/q/owner-replacement-cost).
- Multiples quoted on SDE and on EBITDA aren't comparable; a business priced at the same multiple of each would carry very different prices. See [what multiple](/q/what-multiple).
- If you plan to hire a general manager instead of working in the business, EBITDA after that manager's salary is the more honest measure. See [run it yourself or hire a manager](/q/operator-vs-absentee).
