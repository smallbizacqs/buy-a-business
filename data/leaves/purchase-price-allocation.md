**Short answer.** In an asset purchase, the price is split among the assets you buy (cash, receivables, inventory, equipment, intangibles and goodwill) using a method the IRS sets, and both buyer and seller report that split on IRS Form 8594, attached to their tax returns for the year of the sale. The split matters because it sets your tax basis in each asset and the seller's gain or loss on each, so buyer and seller can want different allocations. Your CPA prepares the allocation; agree it, or how you'll agree it, in the purchase agreement.

### Who files Form 8594

Both the seller and the buyer of a group of assets that makes up a trade or business must file Form 8594 when goodwill or going concern value attaches, or could attach, to the assets.[^irs-form-8594-who-files] It's generally attached to each party's income tax return for the year the sale took place.[^irs-form-8594-when-to-file] Failing to file a correct form on time without reasonable cause can bring penalties.[^irs-form-8594-penalties]

In a straightforward purchase of shares or membership units, you're buying the owner's interest rather than the assets, so ask your CPA whether Form 8594 applies to your deal and how the structure affects your taxes. See [asset or stock purchase](/q/asset-vs-stock).

### How the allocation works

The price must be allocated to determine your basis in each acquired asset and the seller's gain or loss on each, using the residual method.[^irs-form-8594-residual-method] The assets are grouped into seven classes, and no asset outside the last class (goodwill and going concern value) can be allocated more than its fair market value on the purchase date,[^irs-form-8594-fmv-cap] so whatever price is left over after the other assets ends up in goodwill. Examples from the IRS instructions:

- **Class I** is cash and general deposit accounts.[^irs-form-8594-asset-classes]
- **Class III** includes debt instruments such as accounts receivable, and **Class IV** is inventory.[^irs-form-8594-asset-classes]
- **Class V** generally includes furniture and fixtures, buildings, land, vehicles and equipment.[^irs-form-8594-asset-classes]
- **Class VI** is section 197 intangibles other than goodwill and going concern value,[^irs-form-8594-asset-classes] such as a non-compete signed in connection with buying the business, and franchises, trademarks and trade names.[^irs-form-8594-section-197]
- **Class VII** is goodwill and going concern value,[^irs-form-8594-class-vii] which takes the residual.

Your CPA places each asset in its class.

### Why buyer and seller care

- **For you,** the allocation sets the basis you recover through depreciation and amortization, and how quickly you can deduct that basis depends on the type of asset. Your CPA can show you how different allocations change your taxes in the early years.
- **For the seller,** the allocation changes how the gain is taxed, since different assets can produce different kinds of income.
- **Non-competes and consulting payments.** A non-compete signed in connection with the purchase is a Class VI intangible,[^irs-form-8594-section-197] separate from goodwill, while consulting fees are paid for the seller's services. How much you pay under each agreement has tax consequences on both sides. See [seller consulting agreements](/q/seller-consulting-agreement).

This is a negotiation, but it isn't unlimited: values need to be supportable. Under SOP 50 10 8.1 (effective October 1, 2026), an SBA business valuation must allocate separate values to the transaction's components, including land, building, equipment and intangible assets,[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-valuation-allocation] so in an SBA deal there's already an independent reference point.

### Getting it into the deal

- Put the allocation, or a schedule and process for agreeing it after closing, in the purchase agreement, with a promise that both parties will file Form 8594 consistently.
- Decide what happens if the price later changes. If the amount allocated to any asset increases or decreases after the year of sale, the affected party files a supplemental Form 8594 with the return for that later year.[^irs-form-8594-allocation-change] Working capital adjustments, escrow releases and seller note offsets can trigger this.
- Get your CPA's view before you sign the LOI if the seller is asking for a particular allocation, because it can change the after-tax value of the deal to you.

The broader tax trade-offs between structures are covered in [tax structuring](/q/tax-structuring).
