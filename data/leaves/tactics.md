**Short answer.** Owner-sellers are selling something personal: years of work, their employees, their reputation. What helps is honesty, patience, understanding what the seller needs beyond the price, explaining your numbers instead of just stating them, and keeping your word. Hardball tactics borrowed from corporate deals can end a negotiation with an owner who doesn't have to sell.

**Understand what the seller wants**

Before you negotiate, find out:

- Why they're selling and what they plan to do next. See [why owners sell](/q/why-owner-selling).
- What they need in cash at closing, and why.
- What they care about besides money: employees keeping their jobs, the business keeping its name, customers being looked after, their own role after the sale.
- Who else influences the decision: a spouse, a partner, their accountant, a broker.

Then shape your offer around those needs. A seller who mainly wants their long-time staff protected may value a written plan for employees more than a slightly higher price. See [seller psychology](/q/seller-psychology).

**Approaches that help with owners**

- **Show your work.** Walk the seller through how you arrived at your price: earnings, adjustments, what the bank will lend. An independent number helps: SBA describes its required business valuation as helping the buyer decide whether the asking price is supported,[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-valuation-purpose] and under SOP 50 10 8.1 (effective October 1, 2026), any amount above that valuation must come from the buyer's equity.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-valuation-gap-equity]
- **Trade, don't concede.** When you give on one point, ask for something on another. See [price vs. terms](/q/price-vs-terms).
- **Bring bad news early and with evidence.** Problems found in diligence are easier to discuss when you raise them promptly and show the document. Surprises late in the deal feel like tricks. See [retrades](/q/retrade).
- **Ask questions instead of making demands.** "What would make this work for you?" can surface a solution.
- **Let the seller save face.** Frame changes as responses to facts, not as judgments about the seller.
- **Be reliable.** Do what you say, when you say it. Owners judge buyers on small promises kept. See [earning trust](/q/earn-trust).
- **Know your walk-away point** before you start, and be willing to use it politely.

**Approaches that can backfire**

- Lowball offers meant to anchor the conversation.
- Artificial deadlines or pressure.
- Criticizing the business to justify a lower price.
- Using a lawyer or adviser as the "bad cop" to push positions you won't own.
- Going silent for weeks.

**Working with a broker**

When there's a broker, they work for the seller. They can be a useful channel for explaining your reasoning and learning what the seller really needs. See [how brokers work](/q/how-brokers-work).

**Remember the relationship continues**

If the seller will help with the transition or hold a note you're paying, how you negotiate sets the tone for that relationship. See [buyer of choice](/q/buyer-of-choice) and [keeping a deal moving](/q/deal-momentum).
