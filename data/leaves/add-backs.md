**Short answer.** An add-back is an expense the seller ran through the business that a new owner wouldn't have, added back to earnings to show "true" profit. Legitimate add-backs are documented, clearly personal or one-time, and won't recur under you. Inflated ones are expenses the business actually needs, recurring costs labeled one-time, or items with no paper trail.

**Usually easier to support**

- The owner's personal expenses paid by the business (a personal vehicle, family phone plans), with receipts.
- One-time costs with a clear end: a lawsuit that's settled, a one-off move, a rebuilt website.
- Owner pay above a market salary for the role, if you'll hire a replacement at market pay or do the job yourself at that pay.
- Related-party costs at above-market rates (for example, rent paid to the owner's own building) adjusted to market.

**Often inflated**

- "One-time" repairs and equipment that recur every year.
- Family members on payroll who do real work you'd have to replace.
- Marketing or staff cuts the seller "could" make: those are projections, not history.
- Owner pay added back in full with no cost for the work the owner does. See [owner replacement cost](/q/owner-replacement-cost).
- Cash income the owner says isn't on the books. See [unreported cash](/q/cash-business).

**What SBA lenders and QoE reports look for**

Under SOP 50 10 8.1 (effective October 1, 2026):

- A required quality of earnings (QoE) report must document every add-back and adjustment, including non-recurring items, owner compensation above or below market, related-party transactions, deferred maintenance, and cash-versus-accrual differences.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-qoe-revenue-quality]
- The lender may make adjustments such as unfunded capital expenditures, non-recurring income, distributions, seller discretionary expenses and owner compensation,[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-adjustment-items] but must explain why each is prudent, necessary and supported by ongoing operations.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-adjustment-justification] Prudent adjustments for savings the deal will realize are allowed.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-adjustments]
- The seller's numbers are verified against IRS records.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-seller-tax-verification]

**A practical test**

For each add-back, ask for the document behind it and ask, "Would I still pay this after closing?" If yes, it isn't an add-back.
