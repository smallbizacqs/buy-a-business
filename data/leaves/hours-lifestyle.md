**Short answer.** Expect the first year to be the most demanding: long weeks while you learn the business, keep employees and customers through the change, and handle problems that used to go to the seller. The hours and stress depend heavily on the kind of business (seasonal peaks, emergency calls, weekend trade), on how much the seller ran personally, and on whether there's a capable team under you. Ask the seller to walk you through their real week; that's your starting job description.

### What the first year tends to involve

**The first weeks.** Meeting every employee, key customers and suppliers. Learning how money moves: payroll, billing, collections, loan payments. Listening more than changing. See [the first 100 days](/q/first-100-days-plan).

**The first months.** Taking over the decisions the seller made. Fixing urgent problems you found in diligence. Possibly hiring to fill a role the seller filled. The seller's help tapers; in an SBA-financed initial acquisition, they can stay only as a consultant, for up to 24 months.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-seller-consultant] See [the seller's transition period](/q/seller-transition-period).

**Later in the year.** Setting up routines: weekly numbers, regular meetings, a plan for the year ahead. Starting the improvements you held back on. See [don't change too fast](/q/dont-change-too-fast).

### What drives the hours

- **How much the seller did personally.** If they were the main salesperson, estimator and problem solver, you inherit all three. See [owner dependence](/q/owner-dependence).
- **The team.** A strong office manager or operations lead can take a lot off your plate.
- **The business's rhythm.** Seasonal businesses have intense peaks. Service businesses may have after-hours calls. Retail and hospitality run on weekends.
- **Systems.** Paper records and informal processes take more owner time than documented ones.
- **Surprises.** An employee quits, equipment fails, a big customer wavers. Something will.

### Where the stress comes from

- **Cash.** Making payroll and loan payments, especially in a slow month. A reserve helps. See [working capital](/q/what-is-nwc).
- **People.** Managing a team that worked for someone else, sometimes for years.
- **Being the last stop.** Every unresolved problem ends up with you.
- **Isolation.** There's often no one inside the business to talk to as a peer. See [peer groups and boards](/q/peer-groups-board).

### How owners protect their time and health

- Agree on hours and expectations at home before closing. See [talking it through with family](/q/spouse-family).
- Block regular time off, even short, and keep it.
- Write down what only you can do, and train or hire for the rest.
- Watch for burnout signs early. See [owner wellbeing](/q/owner-wellbeing).

### Questions to ask the seller

- What does a typical week look like for you, hour by hour?
- What calls or problems only come to you?
- When did you last take a week off, and what happened?
- What's the busiest time of year, and what does it demand?
