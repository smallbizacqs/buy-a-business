**Short answer.** Combine a few kinds of sources: government statistics to size the market, public registries and licensing records to name the businesses and owners, and industry directories and business listings to fill in details. No single source is complete or current, so expect to clean, verify and enrich the list before you contact anyone.

**Sizing the market (counts, not names)**

The Census Bureau's County Business Patterns is a yearly series with the number of establishments with paid employees by industry and employment size, by state and county.[^census-cbp-establishments] Use it to check whether your industry and area have enough candidates before you build a list by hand.

**Naming businesses and owners**

- **State business entity registries.** Each state's business filing office (in many states, the secretary of state) keeps records of corporations and LLCs, which can include formation date, status, registered agent and sometimes officers or managers. Formation date is a rough proxy for how long the business has existed. What's shown varies by state.
- **Licensing boards.** Businesses in licensed trades and professions appear in public license lookups. Which state, county and city licenses a business needs depends on its activities and location,[^sba-licenses-permits] and federally regulated activities need a federal license,[^sba-licenses-permits-federal] so licensing records exist for many industries. They can show the license holder, which may be the owner or the employee the business depends on.
- **Local records.** Business license and tax registrations, building permits (for trades), property records (who owns the building) and, in some places, fictitious business name ("doing business as") filings.
- **Lien filings.** Uniform Commercial Code (UCC) filings at the state filing office show which lenders have liens on a business's assets, a clue to financing and age.
- **Industry associations.** Member directories, award lists and conference exhibitor lists.
- **Online business listings and maps.** Name, address, phone, website, hours, reviews and photos, which hint at size and activity.
- **The business's own website.** "About us" pages may name the founder and the year started.
- **Commercial business databases.** Paid data providers compile company and contact records with estimated revenue and headcount. Treat those estimates as rough; check a sample against what you can verify.

**Building a usable list**

1. Start with the narrowest filter in your [buy box](/q/define-buy-box): industry and geography.
2. Pull candidates from two or three sources and merge them, removing duplicates and closed businesses.
3. Add signals: years in business, size indicators, owner name, whether the owner appears to be near retirement or running the business alone.
4. Verify the owner's name and a business contact before outreach.
5. Track source and date for each record so you know what's stale.

**Responsible use**

- Follow each source's terms of use; some lookups forbid bulk downloading or scraping.
- Prefer business addresses and phone numbers. A number you find may be the owner's personal cell phone, and the National Do Not Call Registry covers wireless as well as landline numbers.[^fcc-dnc-registry] See [outreach rules](/q/outreach-rules).
- Keep the data secure and collect only what you need.
