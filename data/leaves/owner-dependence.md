**Short answer.** A business depends too much on its owner when the customers, the know-how, the licenses or the key decisions live with that one person and would leave with them. Find out exactly what the owner does each week and who would do it after the sale; then decide whether you can take that role, hire for it, or price the risk.

**How to test it**

- **The owner's week.** Ask the owner to walk through a typical week, hour by hour. Which tasks only they can do?
- **Customers.** Do the largest customers deal with the owner directly? See [customer concentration](/q/customer-concentration).
- **Licenses and certifications.** Does the business operate under the owner's personal license? See [license dependence](/q/license-dependence).
- **Team depth.** Is there a manager or senior employee who already runs parts of the business?
- **Records.** Are pricing, processes and supplier terms written down, or in the owner's head?

**Why lenders care**

Under SOP 50 10 8.1 (effective October 1, 2026), an SBA lender's credit memo must describe the buyer's industry experience and management team, including who will manage daily operations.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-management-experience] In a full purchase financed by a 7(a) loan, the seller can't stay as an owner or employee but may consult for up to 24 months in total,[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-seller-consultant] so the handover has to fit inside that window.

**Ways buyers reduce the risk**

- A written transition plan with specific introductions and training.
- A consulting agreement with the seller for the transition period.
- Hiring or promoting a manager for the owner's tasks before or soon after closing.
- A lower price or more of the price paid later, if the risk can't be reduced.
