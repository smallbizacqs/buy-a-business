**Short answer.** An owner-operator spends most days on four things: leading people, bringing in and keeping customers, managing cash, and keeping the work flowing. Industry knowledge helps, but these general management skills apply whatever the business does. You don't need to be expert at all of them on day one; you need to be willing to do all of them until you can hire for some.

### People

- **Hiring and firing.** Finding people who fit, and acting when someone doesn't.
- **Setting expectations.** Clear roles, what good work looks like, and regular feedback.
- **Earning trust.** A new owner inherits a team that worked for someone else. See [culture](/q/culture) and [keeping employees](/q/employee-retention).
- **Getting worker status right.** Whether someone is an employee or a contractor isn't your choice alone; the IRS looks at behavioral control, financial control and the relationship.[^irs-worker-classification]

### Sales and customers

Expect to sell, especially early: that means calling on key accounts, quoting work, handling complaints and deciding prices. If you've never sold, practise before you buy. See [keeping customers](/q/customer-retention) and [raising prices](/q/raise-prices).

### Cash and numbers

- **Reading the statements.** Profit and loss, balance sheet and cash flow, and how they connect. See [reading financials](/q/reading-financials).
- **Watching cash weekly.** Receivables, payables, payroll and loan payments. Profitable businesses still run out of cash.
- **Paying yourself correctly.** How an owner is paid depends on the entity; a corporate officer is generally an employee and should be paid wages in line with the work.[^irs-paying-yourself-officer] Your CPA sets this up.
- **Knowing a few numbers cold.** The handful of measures that tell you the business is healthy. See [financial controls and KPIs](/q/financial-controls-kpis).

### Operations

Scheduling, quality, purchasing, equipment and suppliers. The skill is less about doing each task than spotting where work gets stuck and fixing the process. See [operating system](/q/operating-system-eos).

### Judgment under uncertainty

You'll make decisions with partial information and live with the results. Owners who do well tend to decide, watch what happens and adjust, rather than waiting for certainty.

### How to test yourself

- List the last time you hired someone, sold something, built a budget and fixed a broken process. Gaps show you where to learn or hire.
- Talk to owners of similar businesses about how their week actually goes.
- Ask a seller to walk you through a typical week; their calendar is a rough draft of your job. See [hours and lifestyle](/q/hours-lifestyle).
- If industry knowledge is your gap rather than management, see [buying outside your industry](/q/no-industry-experience).
