**Short answer.** A first meeting is for understanding the business and the owner well enough to decide whether to keep going, and for the owner to decide whether they'd sell to you. Most buyers walk through the business's past, how it runs today, and where it could go, and leave numbers and terms for later.

**Before the meeting**

- Read whatever you have (the listing, a teaser or CIM, the website, reviews) and write down the questions it leaves open.
- If a broker is involved, ask what the seller wants to cover and whether you can visit the site.
- Decide what you most need to learn to make a go/no-go call.

**A structure many buyers use**

1. **The past.** How the business started, how it grew, hard years and how the owner handled them.
2. **The present.** Who the customers are and how they find the business; who does what (including the owner's own week); key employees, suppliers, the lease and equipment; what keeps the owner up at night.
3. **The future.** What the owner would do with more time or money; what a buyer would need to learn; how long the owner would help after a sale.
4. **The owner.** Why they're selling now, and what matters to them besides price.

**What usually waits**

Detailed financial requests, a price, and deal terms. You can say you'd like to see financial statements and tax returns as a next step if things look promising.

**After the meeting**

Write up what you learned the same day, list what you'd need to verify, and send a short thank-you. If you want to continue, see [how much checking to do before an offer](/q/pre-loi-diligence).

## What practitioners say
- A buyer on The Operating System Show runs a three-hour first meeting with the owner covering the business's past, present and future, before any LOI.[^c3707cf]
