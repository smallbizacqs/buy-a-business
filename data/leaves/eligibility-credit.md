**Short answer.** SBA's rulebook doesn't set a minimum credit score or a required number of years of experience for an acquisition loan; it makes the lender judge both. Under SOP 50 10 8.1 (effective October 1, 2026), the lender must review your personal credit report,[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-credit-reports] describe your industry experience and who will run the business day to day,[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-management-experience] and confirm your pay from the business will cover your living costs.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-owner-compensation] A few things disqualify you outright, such as a past default that cost the government money.

### What the lender looks at

- **Credit.** The lender reviews the personal credit reports of owners and guarantors and must discuss any credit issues.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-credit-reports] Lenders set their own score floors. Ask each one what it uses, and pull your own reports first so a surprise collection or error doesn't come up at underwriting.
- **Experience.** The credit memo describes your industry experience and how day-to-day operations will be managed.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-management-experience] Direct industry experience is the easiest case. Without it, show transferable management experience, a plan for the seller transition, and key employees who stay.
- **Personal cash flow.** Your salary from the business must cover your personal obligations,[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-owner-compensation] and a global cash flow analysis that includes your other businesses must meet 1:1.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-global-cash-flow]
- **Liquidity, both ways.** You need at least 10% equity for an initial acquisition, which the lender can't waive.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-equity-minimum] Ask each lender how much cash it wants you to keep in reserve after closing. At the same time, SBA lending is for borrowers who can't get credit elsewhere on reasonable terms,[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-credit-elsewhere] and that test looks at whether 20% owners could fund the need themselves, allowing reasonable reserves for medical, education and retirement needs.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-personal-liquidity]
- **Personal financial statement and guaranty.** Owners of 20% or more give a personal financial statement dated within 90 days[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-owner-financial-statement] and an unlimited personal guaranty.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-personal-guaranty] See [personal guarantees](/q/personal-guarantee).

### What disqualifies you outright

- **A prior loss to the government**, such as a defaulted federal loan that was written off.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-prior-loss]
- **Delinquent non-tax federal debt** owed by you or a guarantor.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-delinquent-federal-debt] Paying it off can clear the way; ask the lender how to document it.
- **Incarceration or a pending felony or financial-misconduct indictment** of an associate of the business. An owner on parole or probation may still be eligible.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-incarceration]
- **Citizenship.** Every owner and required guarantor must be a U.S. citizen or national living in the U.S.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-citizenship-owners] See [the citizenship rule](/q/citizenship-rule).

### Before you talk to lenders

1. Pull all three credit reports and fix errors.
2. Write a one-page summary of your experience that maps it to the business you're targeting.
3. Prepare a personal financial statement and a household budget showing the salary you need.
4. Know your cash: what's liquid, what's in retirement accounts, and what you'd keep in reserve.
5. List anything in your history a lender will find: bankruptcies, liens, judgments, federal debt, litigation.

SBA guarantees these loans but lenders make them,[^sba-7a-loans-page] and lenders differ. A decline from one lender isn't a decline from all. See [how lenders underwrite](/q/underwriting) and [being ready to buy](/q/readiness).
