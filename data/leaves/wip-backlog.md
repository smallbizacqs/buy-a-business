**Short answer.** In a contractor or project business, the jobs in progress at closing must be split between seller and buyer: who gets paid for work already done, who owes work already paid for, and who bears losses on jobs that are underwater. Get a work-in-progress (WIP) schedule for every open job, check whether the business is overbilled or underbilled, and put the treatment into the working capital terms. Backlog (signed work not yet started) is part of what you're buying, but only profitable, likely-to-proceed jobs count.

**Terms**

- **Work in progress (WIP):** jobs started but not finished.
- **WIP schedule:** for each job, the contract price, estimated total cost, costs to date, percent complete, amount billed and estimated profit.
- **Underbilling:** the business has done more work than it has billed. That's an asset: money earned but not yet invoiced.
- **Overbilling:** the business has billed more than the work done. That's a liability: you'll have to finish work the seller has already been paid for.
- **Backlog:** signed contracts not yet started or not yet billed.

**What to check**

1. **The WIP schedule's accuracy.** Compare estimated costs to complete with what project managers say. Optimistic estimates make jobs look more profitable and more complete than they are.
2. **Job profit fade.** Compare estimated profit on finished jobs with what they actually earned. A pattern of profit shrinking toward the end means estimates are too rosy.
3. **Billing position.** Large net overbillings mean cash was collected early and the work is still owed. Ask where that cash is now.
4. **Loss jobs.** Any job expected to lose money, and how much.
5. **Retainage:** amounts customers hold back until completion, and whether they'll be paid.
6. **Backlog quality.** Signed contracts vs. verbal awards, margin, start dates, cancellation rights and whether the customer will consent to a new owner.
7. **Bonding and warranties.** Who stands behind open jobs and completed work. Ask the surety whether bonds stay in place after a sale.

**How the earnings are affected**

Contractor revenue can look very different on a cash basis than when recognized by percent complete. Where a quality of earnings report is required for an SBA loan, it must document cash-versus-accrual differences and assess contract continuity.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-qoe-revenue-quality] Ask whoever reviews the numbers to restate prior years on a consistent percent-complete basis.

**Handling it in the deal**

- **Working capital.** Include underbillings and retainage as assets and overbillings and customer deposits as liabilities in the working capital definition, and set the target with that in mind. See [working capital](/q/what-is-nwc) and [the peg](/q/peg). Under SBA rules, cash paid to the buyer under a working capital true-up can stay in the business.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-working-capital-true-up]
- **Cut-off.** Agree a WIP schedule as of the closing date and a process to true it up after closing.
- **Loss jobs.** Price them in, or have the seller keep responsibility for them.
- **Backlog in the price.** Don't pay separately for backlog that's already reflected in earnings, and don't pay for backlog at margins the business hasn't achieved.
