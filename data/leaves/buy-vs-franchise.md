**Short answer.** An independent business gives you full control and no ongoing fees to a franchisor, but no outside playbook. A new franchise unit gives you a system and brand but no operating history at your location, plus ongoing fees. A franchise resale sits in between: an operating unit with its own track record, inside a system whose rules, fees and transfer approval you take on. The choice turns on how much guidance you want, how much control you'll give up, and whether you want to pay for a proven location or build one.

### The core trade

The SBA puts it simply: the main difference between franchising and buying an existing business is the level of control you'll have.[^sba-buy-business-control] A franchisee runs under the franchisor's brand, rules and fees in return for training, systems and a known name. An independent owner decides everything and keeps the margin that would otherwise go to royalties, but depends on what the seller built and documented.

### A new franchise unit

- **Disclosure first.** Under the FTC Franchise Rule, the franchisor must give you its current franchise disclosure document (FDD) at least 14 calendar days before you sign a binding agreement or pay anything.[^ecfr-16-cfr-436-2-disclosure-14-days]
- **Earnings claims.** A franchisor may share financial performance figures only if they have a reasonable basis and appear in Item 19 of the FDD.[^ecfr-16-cfr-436-5-item-19] If Item 19 is thin or missing, you're estimating your own revenue.
- **Talk to franchisees.** Item 20 lists current franchisees with contact details,[^ecfr-16-cfr-436-5-item-20] and franchisees who left the system in the last fiscal year.[^ecfr-16-cfr-436-5-item-20-former] Call both kinds.
- **The risk.** You still have to build the customer base at a new site, so the cash flow risk resembles [starting a business](/q/buy-vs-start), with more structure and higher ongoing costs.

### A franchise resale

You buy an operating unit, so you can review its real financials, as with any acquisition. You also step into the franchise relationship. Item 17 of the FDD covers renewal, termination and transfer, including the franchisor's approval of a transfer.[^ecfr-16-cfr-436-5-item-17-transfer] A resale by an existing franchisee isn't a "sale of a franchise" under the FTC rule when the franchisor has no significant involvement with the buyer, and approving the transfer alone doesn't count as significant involvement.[^ecfr-16-cfr-436-1-transfer-not-sale] So you may not be handed a disclosure document automatically; ask for the current one anyway. Ask the franchisor early:

- Will you approve me, and what training, fees or remodeling do you require on transfer?
- Do I take over the seller's agreement or sign your current form, and how many years remain?
- Are there open defaults, required upgrades or territory limits at this unit?

See [franchise transfers](/q/franchise-transfer).

### An independent business

You get the most control and no royalties. The main risk is how much of the business lives in the seller's head; see [owner dependence](/q/owner-dependence). You'll build your own systems where a franchise would hand you one.

### Financing

For SBA lending, under SOP 50 10 8.1 (effective October 1, 2026), a brand that meets the FTC definition of a franchise must be on the SBA Franchise Directory for the business to get SBA financing.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-franchise-directory] Check the brand before you spend money on diligence.

### Questions that decide it

- Do you want a manual and a support line, or freedom to change prices, suppliers and services?
- After royalties and required spending, does the unit leave enough for debt payments and your pay? See [take-home pay](/q/owner-take-home).
- What do current and former franchisees say about the franchisor's support and fees?
