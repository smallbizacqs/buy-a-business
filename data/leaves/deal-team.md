**Short answer.** A lender (if you're borrowing), a deal lawyer, an accountant, and an insurance broker, plus a quality of earnings provider when the deal's size or complexity calls for one. Line up the lender and accountant before you make offers, bring in the lawyer by the LOI, and add the QoE provider and insurance broker once you have a signed LOI. Specialists (appraisers, environmental, industry experts) join only when a specific issue needs them.

**The core team and when each joins**

| Role | What they do | When to bring them in |
|---|---|---|
| **Lender** | Says what they'll finance and on what terms; runs underwriting | Before offers, to know your budget |
| **Accountant (CPA)** | Reviews financials, tax structure, price allocation, your entity | Before offers, for a first look at numbers |
| **Deal lawyer** | LOI review, legal diligence, purchase agreement, closing | At or before the LOI |
| **QoE provider** | Independent review of earnings and cash | After the LOI, when needed |
| **Insurance broker** | Coverage for the business you're buying | During diligence, well before closing |

**What to know about each**

- **Lender.** SBA doesn't lend directly; it guarantees 7(a) loans that lenders make.[^sba-7a-loans-page] Talk to lenders who do acquisition loans regularly; ask how many they closed last year and what they need from you. See [choosing a lender](/q/lender-selection).
- **Accountant.** Your own CPA works for you, separate from the lender's QoE provider. Both buyer and seller report an asset purchase to the IRS on Form 8594,[^irs-form-8594-who-files] so the price allocation needs your CPA's input. See [what your accountant does](/q/cpa-role).
- **Lawyer.** Look for someone who does small business acquisitions, not just general business law. See [choosing counsel](/q/choosing-counsel).
- **QoE provider.** Under SOP 50 10 8.1 (effective October 1, 2026), a QoE required for an SBA loan must be independent, done for the lender, and not prepared by or for the seller.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-qoe-not-for-seller] If you commission your own first, the lender may have one of its vendors review it instead of ordering a new one.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-qoe-buyer-commissioned] Ask your lender before you hire anyone. See [do I need a QoE](/q/qoe-need).
- **Business valuation.** For an SBA loan the valuation is ordered by and prepared for the lender, not you or the seller,[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-valuation-for-lender] so it's on the lender's side of the team.
- **Insurance broker.** The lender's credit memo addresses life insurance and business hazard and liability insurance,[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-insurance-credit-memo] and you need coverage in place at closing. See [insurance review](/q/insurance-review).

**Others you may need**

- **Equipment or real estate appraiser** for asset-heavy deals or property.
- **Environmental consultant** where the site or industry raises the question. See [environmental diligence](/q/environmental).
- **Industry expert or operator** to check operations, pricing or equipment.
- **IT or cybersecurity review** for businesses that run on data or software.
- **Buy-side adviser or broker** if you want help finding or running deals. See [buy-side advisers](/q/buy-side-advisor).
- **A mentor or peer group** who has bought a business before.

**How to put it together**

- Ask each adviser how many small acquisitions they've worked on, and for references.
- Get fee quotes in writing. See [what advisers cost](/q/advisor-costs).
- Introduce the team to each other early and agree who sends what to whom.
- Make clear that you make the decisions; advisers advise.

Some buyers handle small deals with templates and a lean team. Buyers who hire counsel can look for AI-using law firms with fixed fees that are lean and run by an attorney with big-law experience.
