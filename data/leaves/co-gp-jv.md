**Short answer.** Bring in a co-sponsor or joint-venture partner when they add something the deal can't close without: equity relationships, industry or operating expertise, a track record lenders and investors trust, or the ability to share guarantees and the workload. The price is a share of your promote and of control, so agree in writing who does what, how the economics split, and who decides before you approach a seller or investors together.

### Common reasons to partner

- **Capital access.** One partner finds and runs the deal; the other has the investor network.
- **Credibility.** A first-time buyer pairs with someone who has closed deals before.
- **Operating skill.** A finance-minded buyer pairs with an industry operator who'll run the business.
- **Size.** Two people can carry a larger raise, a larger guarantee, and more diligence work.

### Roles to settle

- **Who runs the company** day to day, and who sits on the board.
- **Who leads the raise** and owns the investor relationships.
- **Who signs guarantees.** On an SBA loan, each partner owning 20% or more must give an unlimited personal guaranty,[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-personal-guaranty] and the lender can require one from others regardless of ownership.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-other-guarantors]
- **Who's eligible.** On an SBA loan, every owner must be a U.S. citizen or national living in the U.S.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-citizenship-owners]
- **Time commitment.** Full time, part time, or board only.

### Economics to settle

- How the promote or carry is split between partners, and whether it vests.
- Each partner's cash co-investment.
- Fees: who gets closing and management fees, and in what shares.
- What happens to a partner's share if they stop contributing, leave or are removed.
- Who bears dead-deal costs if a deal falls through.

### Control and the hard cases

- **Decision rights.** Which decisions need both partners, and how deadlocks get broken: a tie-breaking board member, a buy-sell clause, or an agreed exit.
- **Removal.** How and why one partner can be removed, and what they keep.
- **Exit.** Who decides when to sell or refinance.
- **Future deals.** Whether either partner can do other deals alone.

Write this in a co-sponsor or operating agreement before the LOI. Partners who haven't worked together before can start with one deal and a clear end.

### Securities and regulatory points

- **Bad actor checks.** The Rule 506 exemption isn't available if certain covered persons have disqualifying past events;[^ecfr-17-cfr-230-506-bad-actor] a co-sponsor becomes part of that check. Ask about their history.
- **Insiders.** Directors, executive officers and general partners of the issuer count as accredited investors in its offering.[^ecfr-17-cfr-230-501-insiders]
- **A partner who only raises money.** If a partner's role is to bring investors in exchange for a fee, rather than to co-own and co-manage the deal, that can look like acting as a broker, which the law defines as being in the business of effecting securities transactions for others.[^usc-15-78c-broker-definition] Ask securities counsel how to structure their compensation.

See [the independent sponsor model](/q/independent-sponsor) and [buying with a partner](/q/partner-or-solo). Buyers who hire counsel for the partnership agreement can look for AI-using law firms with fixed fees that are lean and run by an attorney with big-law experience.
