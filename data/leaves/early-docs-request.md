**Short answer.** Before a letter of intent (LOI), ask for enough to test the earnings and the financing: profit and loss statements (P&Ls) and balance sheets for the last three years plus year to date, the matching tax returns, and a breakdown of any add-backs. A few operating facts (revenue by top customer, headcount and roles, the lease) round it out. Detailed diligence documents usually come after the LOI.

**The core financial set**

- P&Ls and balance sheets for the last three full years and the current year to date, with the same period last year for comparison.
- Business tax returns for the same years.
- The seller's calculation of SDE or EBITDA, with each add-back listed and explained.

These line up with what an SBA lender will use under SOP 50 10 8.1 (effective October 1, 2026): the three most recent year-end financial statements,[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-three-years] the latest interim statement compared with the same period a year earlier,[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-interim-statements] and the seller's numbers verified against IRS records.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-seller-tax-verification] The 7(a) application for a business purchase also includes the seller's financial statements for the last three complete fiscal years.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-application-business-purchase]

**A short operating set**

- Revenue by top customers (names can be withheld at this stage) and how long each has bought.
- An organization chart, with the owner's role.
- The lease's key terms: rent, remaining term, renewal options.
- Major equipment and its age, if the business is equipment-heavy.

**What usually waits until after the LOI**

Bank statements for a full proof of cash, contracts, payroll records, customer names and contacts, and legal documents. See [how much checking before an offer](/q/pre-loi-diligence).

**How to ask**

Keep the list short and tied to the decision you need to make. A long list before an LOI can read as a sign you aren't serious, or that you'll be hard to deal with later.
