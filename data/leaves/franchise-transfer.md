**Short answer.** The seller's franchise agreement decides, including whether the franchisor must approve you before the seller can transfer. Conditions a franchisor can attach include checking your finances and background, requiring you to complete its training, charging a transfer fee, and requiring a new franchise agreement on current terms, a remodel or equipment upgrades, and a release from the seller. The franchisor may also have a right of first refusal to buy the business itself on your terms. Read the current Franchise Disclosure Document's Item 17 and the seller's actual agreement to see the specific conditions.

### Where the rules are written

The Federal Trade Commission's Franchise Rule requires the Franchise Disclosure Document (FDD) to include, in Item 17, a table covering renewal, termination, transfer and dispute resolution,[^ecfr-16-cfr-436-5-item-17-transfer] including the franchisor's approval of a transfer by a franchisee, the conditions for that approval, and any right of first refusal to acquire the franchisee's business. Those rows point to the sections of the franchise agreement that control.

Two cautions:

- **The seller's agreement may be older** than the current FDD, with different transfer terms. Get the signed agreement and all amendments, not just the FDD.
- **You may not receive an FDD at all.** Under the FTC rule, a transfer by an existing franchisee isn't treated as a franchise sale when the franchisor has no significant involvement with the buyer, and approving or rejecting the transfer alone doesn't count as significant involvement.[^ecfr-16-cfr-436-1-transfer-not-sale] If the franchisor is involved, for example by signing a new agreement with you, it must give you its current disclosure document at least 14 calendar days before you sign a binding agreement with it or pay it.[^ecfr-16-cfr-436-2-disclosure-14-days] Either way, ask for the current FDD.

### Conditions to look for

Check each of these in the agreement and ask the franchisor directly:

- **Application and approval**: your financial statements, credit, background and experience, and sometimes an interview.
- **Training**: completion of the franchisor's training program before closing, sometimes at your expense.
- **Transfer fee**: a fixed amount or a formula; find out who pays it.
- **New agreement**: whether you take over the seller's remaining term or sign the current form, which may carry different royalties, fees or territory.
- **Remodel or upgrades**: bringing the location up to current standards, with a deadline.
- **Seller conditions**: the seller must be current on all fees, sign a general release and agree to a non-compete.
- **Right of first refusal**: the franchisor's right to buy the business on the same terms. Find out how long it has to decide.

### Diligence specific to franchises

- **Royalty and fee history.** Confirm the seller is current; unpaid fees can block the transfer.
- **Compliance record.** Ask the franchisor for any default notices, audit findings or inspection scores for this location.
- **Other franchisees.** Item 20 of the FDD lists franchisees who left the system in the last fiscal year, with contact details,[^ecfr-16-cfr-436-5-item-20-former] which gives you people to call about the system.
- **Territory.** Whether the territory transfers as is, and whether the franchisor has opened or plans nearby units.

### If you're using an SBA loan

Under SOP 50 10 8.1 (effective October 1, 2026), a business operating under a brand that meets the FTC definition of a franchise can get SBA financing only if the brand is on the SBA Franchise Directory.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-franchise-directory] Check the directory before you spend money on diligence.

### Timing

Build the franchisor's approval into the purchase agreement as a closing condition, and start the application early: training dates and approval committees run on the franchisor's schedule.
