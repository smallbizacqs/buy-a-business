**Short answer.** In an asset purchase, your company buys the business's assets (equipment, customer lists, the name, goodwill) and takes only the liabilities it agrees to. In a stock purchase, you buy the owner's shares (or membership units in an LLC), so the company, with its contracts and its history, including its liabilities, changes hands intact. The right choice depends on taxes, contracts, licenses and risk, and your lawyer and CPA work it out with you.

**Asset purchase**

- You choose which assets and liabilities you take. Others generally stay with the seller, though some liabilities can follow the business by law.
- Contracts, leases and some licenses have to be assigned to you, which can require consent from the other party.
- Both buyer and seller report the sale to the IRS on Form 8594 when goodwill could attach to the assets.[^irs-form-8594-who-files] The price is allocated across the assets using the IRS residual method, which sets your basis in each asset and the seller's gain or loss on each.[^irs-form-8594-residual-method] See [taxes and price allocation](/q/tax-structuring).

**Stock purchase**

- The company stays the same legal entity, so contracts, permits and accounts often stay in place. Check each one for a change-of-control clause that requires consent or lets the other side end it.
- Past liabilities stay with the company you now own, known or not. That puts more weight on diligence, the seller's representations and warranties, and indemnification. See [reps and warranties](/q/reps-warranties).

**With an SBA 7(a) loan,** either structure can be financed.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-asset-or-stock] The borrower differs: in an asset purchase, your acquiring company is the borrower;[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-asset-purchase-borrower] when a new owner buys 100% of the shares, the business and the buyer are co-borrowers.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-borrower-structure] (SBA SOP 50 10 8.1, effective October 1, 2026.) An SBA 7(a) loan is a bank loan partly guaranteed by the U.S. Small Business Administration (SBA).

**Questions to settle with your advisors:** Which liabilities worry you most? Which contracts, leases and licenses can't be assigned? How do the tax results compare for you and for the seller? Sellers often have their own tax reasons to prefer one structure, so this is a negotiation point to raise in the LOI.
