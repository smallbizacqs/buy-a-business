**Short answer.** Yes, people buy businesses outside their industry. The gaps to close are the lender's confidence that you can run it, licenses or certifications that only a credentialed person can hold, and your own learning curve. Buyers close them with general management experience, an experienced manager or key employee who stays, a planned handover from the seller, and by choosing businesses where running the company matters more than doing the trade.

### What the lender looks at

For an SBA 7(a) loan, under SOP 50 10 8.1 (effective October 1, 2026), the lender's credit memo describes the applicant's industry experience and management team, including who will manage daily operations.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-management-experience] Lenders weigh this in their own way. Some accept strong general management experience; others want industry experience on the team. Ask early how a lender will view your background. See [underwriting](/q/underwriting).

### How buyers make up for the gap

- **Show transferable experience.** Managing people, budgets, sales or operations carries across industries. Put it in your lender package in concrete terms. See [lender package](/q/lender-package).
- **Keep the people who know the work.** A general manager, lead technician or office manager who stays can carry the know-how while you learn. Talk to them before closing and plan how you'll keep them. See [key employee risk](/q/key-employee-risk) and [keeping employees](/q/employee-retention).
- **Use the seller.** In an SBA-financed initial acquisition, the seller can stay as a consultant for up to 24 months.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-seller-consultant] Write down what they need to teach you. See [the seller's transition period](/q/seller-transition-period).
- **Spend time in the business first.** Some buyers work alongside the owner during diligence. See [working in the business before closing](/q/work-in-business-first).
- **Find an advisor from the industry.** A retired operator or consultant can help with diligence and the first year.
- **Pick the right kind of business.** Where the owner's job is sales, scheduling and managing staff, an outsider can learn it. Where the owner is the main producer, the gap is harder to close.

### Licenses and credentials

Some businesses need a licensed person to operate: a contractor's license, a professional license or a certification. Check whether the license belongs to the business or to a person, and whether you can hold it, hire someone who does, or keep the seller's credential in place lawfully. In an SBA change of ownership, transferable licenses (for example, a liquor license) are among the assets the lender takes as collateral.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-transferable-licenses] See [license dependence](/q/license-dependence) and [transferring licenses and permits](/q/licenses-permits-transfer).

### Questions to ask yourself

- Can I describe how this business wins and keeps customers, in plain terms?
- Who will answer technical questions on day one if I can't?
- What would I do if the key employee left in month two?
- Have I talked to people who own similar businesses about what they wish they'd known?
