**Short answer.** The 7(a) loan is the one that buys the business itself: goodwill, equipment, working capital, and the building too if you blend it in. The 504 program finances only long-term fixed assets such as real estate, so in an acquisition it can't pay for goodwill; that part needs a 7(a) or other financing.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-504-change-of-ownership] When a deal includes the building, you can use one blended 7(a) loan, or split it into a 7(a) for the business and a separate real estate loan, which can be a 504.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-real-estate-separate-or-blended]

### What a 504 is

The 504 program gives long-term, fixed-rate financing for major fixed assets.[^sba-504-page-purpose] It's made only through Certified Development Companies (CDCs), working alongside a bank.[^sba-504-page-cdcs][^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-504-structure] A 504 project generally has three pieces: a bank or other third-party lender provides 50% or more, the CDC provides up to 40% through an SBA-guaranteed debenture, and the borrower puts in at least 10%.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-504-structure] A new business contributes at least 15%.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-504-contribution] Terms run 10, 20 or 25 years, up to 25 for real estate,[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-504-maturity] with the rate pegged above the 10-year Treasury.[^sba-504-page-rates] A 504 can't fund working capital or inventory.[^sba-504-page-not-for]

### What a 7(a) does in an acquisition

A 7(a) loan can finance a purchase that includes goodwill and other intangibles, as long as SBA's financial due diligence rules are met.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-intangibles] If it also buys real estate on a blended basis, only the real estate share may amortize past 10 years, up to 25; everything else gets 10.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-real-estate-structure]

### Using a 504 in a business purchase

Under SOP 50 10 8.1 (effective October 1, 2026), a 504 can be part of a change of ownership when:

- It finances only eligible long-term fixed assets; goodwill and receivables must be financed another way, which may include a 7(a) loan.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-504-change-of-ownership]
- The application shows jobs will be created or retained because of the purchase.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-504-change-of-ownership]
- You end up owning 100% of the business, and the seller doesn't stay as an officer, director, owner or key employee.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-504-coo-conditions]
- The business occupies the building: for an existing building, at least 51% of it.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-occupancy]

A 504 can't be blended into the 7(a); it's always a separate loan.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-real-estate-separate-or-blended]

### Equity when you split the deal

Real estate bought with an SBA acquisition is subject to the same equity requirement. With two loans, the lender allocates the equity pro rata, but a 504 loan follows the 504 program's own contribution rules.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-real-estate-equity] The 10% minimum on the business purchase can't be waived for an Initial Acquisition.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-equity-minimum]

### How to choose

| Question | Points toward |
|---|---|
| Want a fixed rate on the building for a long term? | A separate 504 for the real estate |
| Want one loan, one closing, one lender? | A blended 7(a) |
| Is the building a large share of the price? | Get quotes both ways; the structure changes the payment |
| Is your lender also comfortable working with a CDC? | Needed for the 504 route |

Ask your lender to run the monthly payment, total fees and equity required under both structures. The right answer depends on rates at the time, the real estate's share of the deal and how long you plan to own the building.
