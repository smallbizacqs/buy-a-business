**Short answer.** Pass early when the numbers can't be verified, when the deal can't be financed as asked, or when the business's value walks out the door with the owner or one customer. Most other problems can be priced or structured; these usually can't be fixed with terms.

**Numbers you can't verify**

- The seller won't share tax returns, or the returns are far below the profit and loss statement (P&L) and the explanation is unreported cash. If you're using an SBA loan, the lender must verify the seller's financial data against IRS records,[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-seller-tax-verification] so earnings that aren't on the returns won't count. See [tax returns vs. books](/q/tax-returns-vs-books).
- Add-backs make up much of the claimed earnings and lack documents. See [add-backs](/q/add-backs).
- The price is justified by projections rather than history. An SBA lender can't rely on projections to meet the coverage requirement (special purpose property aside).[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-projections]

**A deal that can't be financed as asked** (SBA 7(a), SOP 50 10 8.1, effective October 1, 2026)

- Historical cash flow doesn't cover all the debt at 1.25:1 for an initial acquisition.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-dsc-ratio]
- The asking price is far above what a valuation is likely to support; the gap would have to be paid with equity.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-valuation-gap-equity]
- The seller insists on an earnout, which is prohibited,[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-earnouts] or on staying as an owner or employee after a full sale, which isn't allowed beyond a consulting role of up to 24 months.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-seller-consultant]
- The business is a type that's ineligible for SBA loans.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-ineligible-businesses]

**Value that might walk out the door**

- One or a few customers account for a large share of revenue, and they're tied to the owner. See [customer concentration](/q/customer-concentration).
- The business runs on the owner's personal license or relationships with no one ready to take over. See [owner dependence](/q/owner-dependence).
- The lease is ending or can't be assigned, and the location is essential.

**Behavior**

A seller who won't explain basic numbers, changes figures between conversations, pushes for a fast close without diligence, or won't let you meet key staff before closing. None of these proves a problem, but each raises the cost of finding out.

Some red flags are dealbreakers for one buyer and acceptable to another; decide in advance which ones are yours and write them into your [buy box](/q/define-buy-box).
