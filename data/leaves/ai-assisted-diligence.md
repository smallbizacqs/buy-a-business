**Short answer.** Yes, for the reading, sorting and first-pass analysis: summarizing contracts and leases, pulling key terms into a table, reconciling exports, flagging gaps in a document request list and drafting follow-up questions. AI tools don't replace verification against outside records, a quality of earnings report a lender requires, or a lawyer's or CPA's judgment, and you have to check their output. Before you upload anything, make sure your NDA and the tool's data terms allow it.

### Where AI tools help

- **Contract and lease review.** Extract the term, renewal, assignment and change-of-control clauses, exclusivity, termination rights and notice addresses from a stack of agreements into one table. Then read the clauses that matter yourself. See [whether contracts transfer](/q/contract-assignment).
- **Organizing the data room.** Index what the seller has sent against your request list and flag what's missing, outdated or duplicated. See [the financial checklist](/q/financial-dd-checklist).
- **Spreadsheet work.** Clean a customer-level sales export, group customers into cohorts, calculate retention, and tie monthly totals to bank deposits. See [revenue quality and churn](/q/revenue-quality-churn) and [proof of cash](/q/proof-of-cash).
- **Finding inconsistencies.** Compare what the CIM, the seller's answers and the documents say about the same facts (revenue, headcount, customer count, lease terms).
- **Drafting.** First drafts of follow-up questions, a diligence summary, a list of disclosure schedule items for your lawyer, or a 100-day plan.
- **Learning the industry.** Explaining terms, regulations and typical operating metrics so you ask better questions, checked against primary sources.

### Where they don't

- **They can be wrong with confidence.** Models can misread a clause, invent a number or miss a page. Check every figure against the source document and every legal conclusion with your lawyer.
- **They only see what you give them.** AI can't tell you a document is forged or that a customer is invented. Verification comes from records the seller doesn't control: bank statements, tax transcripts, public filings and calls. See [fraud and misstatements](/q/fraud-misstatement).
- **They don't replace required reports.** Under SOP 50 10 8.1 (effective October 1, 2026), an SBA-required quality of earnings report must be independent, done for the lender, and not prepared by or for the seller,[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-qoe-not-for-seller] and the SBA business valuation must be ordered by and prepared for the lender.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-valuation-for-lender] Your own AI analysis is useful preparation, not a substitute.
- **They don't make judgment calls.** Whether a finding is a reason to reprice or walk away is your decision. See [findings that kill or reprice a deal](/q/deal-killers).

### Protect the seller's information

- **Read your NDA.** It may limit who can see the seller's documents and how they're stored. Uploading them to a third-party service may or may not be allowed.
- **Check the tool's terms.** Know whether the provider keeps your uploads, uses them for training, and where they're stored. Use business or enterprise settings that exclude training where available.
- **Minimize.** Remove employee personal data, customer contact details and account numbers when the task doesn't need them.
- **Keep the originals.** Work on copies, and keep a record of which documents you analyzed and when.

### A practical workflow

1. Index everything received against your request list.
2. Extract key terms and numbers into tables, with a page reference for each.
3. Check a sample of extractions by hand; if errors appear, check them all.
4. Run the reconciliations and calculations, and keep the formulas visible.
5. Turn gaps and inconsistencies into written questions for the seller.
6. Hand the findings to your lawyer, CPA and lender with the source documents attached.

For AI in finding deals rather than checking them, see [AI for sourcing](/q/ai-sourcing).
