**Short answer.** It's a more formal, more demanding process than selling to an individual. Expect deeper due diligence (which may include a quality of earnings report), a purchase agreement with detailed representations and indemnities, and a price that may be split between cash at closing and other pieces such as rollover equity, a seller note or an earnout. A private equity buyer may want you to stay for a period and keep a stake; a strategic buyer (a company in your industry) may integrate the business into its own and need you for less time.

### Two kinds of buyer

**Private equity (PE).** A fund that buys companies with investors' money and debt, aiming to grow them and sell within a set period. A PE firm may buy your company as a platform (the base for a roll-up) or as an add-on to one it already owns. See [PE consolidation](/q/pe-consolidation).

**Strategic buyer.** A company in your industry or a related one, buying to add customers, territory, services or capabilities. It may combine your business with its own operations, name and systems.

### What the process looks like

1. **Preparation.** Clean, timely financial statements, ideally reviewed or audited, and an understanding of your adjustments to earnings. Some sellers commission their own quality of earnings report first to find problems before buyers do.
2. **Marketing.** An investment banker or M&A advisor may prepare a confidential memorandum and approach buyers, sometimes running a process with several bidders.
3. **Indications of interest and a letter of intent.** Non-binding offers, then an LOI (letter of intent) with price, structure and an exclusivity period.
4. **Due diligence.** Financial, legal, tax, HR, IT and commercial review, which can take weeks or longer, with long document request lists.
5. **Purchase agreement and closing.** Negotiated with lawyers on both sides; signing and closing may be separate.

### How the price is paid

- **Cash at closing.** Reduced by debt, transaction costs and any working-capital adjustment.
- **Rollover equity.** You keep or reinvest part of your proceeds as a stake in the buyer's company, sharing in a later sale. See [rollover equity](/q/rollover-equity).
- **Seller note or earnout.** Part of the price paid later, fixed or tied to results. See [earnouts](/q/earnouts).
- **Escrow or holdback** for indemnity claims or a working-capital true-up.
- **Employment or consulting agreement** for your role after closing, which may include non-compete terms.

Compare offers on what you'll actually receive and when, after taxes, and the risk on each deferred piece, not just the headline price.

### Life after the sale

With PE, you may stay as CEO or in another role, now reporting to a board, with budgets, reporting requirements and growth targets. With a strategic buyer, your business may be absorbed, and your team will work under new management. Think about what you want for your employees and customers, and negotiate for it.

### Your advisors

You'll want an M&A advisor or banker (depending on size), a CPA who knows transaction tax, and an experienced deal lawyer. Sellers who hire counsel can look for this category: AI-using law firms with fixed fees that are lean and run by an attorney with big-law experience. Ask any advisor about fees, how they're paid and the size of deals they handle.
