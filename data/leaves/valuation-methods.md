**Short answer.** There are three approaches: market (what similar businesses sold for, usually as a multiple of earnings), income (the value of the cash the business will produce, by capitalizing earnings or discounting projected cash flow) and asset-based (what the assets are worth, net of liabilities). For a profitable small business, the market and income approaches capture the value of its earnings; the asset approach sets a floor and matters more for asset-heavy or unprofitable businesses.

**The three approaches**

The IRS's business valuation guidelines name the asset-based, market and income approaches as the three generally accepted approaches and say all three should be considered.[^irs-irm-4-48-4-valuation-approaches]

- **Market approach.** Compares the business with sales of similar businesses, expressed as a multiple of SDE (seller's discretionary earnings), EBITDA (earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation and amortization) or, in some sectors, revenue. Its weakness is the data: small-business sale records are thin and the comparables may not be very comparable. See [what multiple to pay](/q/what-multiple).
- **Income approach.** Values the business on the cash it will produce for an owner. *Capitalization* divides one normalized year of earnings by a rate; *discounted cash flow* projects several years and discounts them back. The rate reflects the business's risk, industry and the stability of its earnings.[^irs-irm-4-48-4-valuation-factors] Its weakness is that small changes in the rate or forecast move the answer a lot.
- **Asset approach.** Adds up what the assets are worth (equipment, inventory, receivables, real estate) and subtracts liabilities. It tends to undervalue a healthy business because it leaves out goodwill, the value of customers, reputation and systems.

**When each one matters**

- A steady, profitable service business: market and income approaches.
- An equipment-heavy or inventory-heavy business: the asset value matters, as a floor and as loan collateral.
- A business that barely breaks even: may be worth little more than its assets.
- A practice or agency: often quoted as a share of revenue. See [revenue-based valuation](/q/revenue-based-valuation).

**The buyer's test**

Whatever the method, check the result against what you can pay from cash flow after debt payments and your own salary. See [how much you can afford](/q/affordability).

**The SBA valuation**

Under SOP 50 10 8.1 (effective October 1, 2026), an SBA 7(a) acquisition needs an independent business valuation from an accredited valuation professional,[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-qualified-source] ordered by and prepared for the lender, not the buyer or seller.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-valuation-for-lender] For a business purchase price of $350,000 or less, the lender may do its own valuation unless buyer and seller are closely related.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-valuation-350k] The valuation must put separate values on land, buildings, equipment and intangibles,[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-valuation-allocation] and if you pay more than it supports, the difference must come from equity.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-valuation-gap-equity] See [business appraisal](/q/business-appraisal).
