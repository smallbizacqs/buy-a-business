**Short answer.** Build a search budget with three separate buckets: your living costs for the whole search, your search and deal expenses (including money spent on deals that don't close), and the cash you'll need to put into the deal. Fund the first two from savings, a job, a partner's income or search investors, and never from the third. Know in advance how many months you can afford, and plan for the search to take longer than you hope.

**Bucket 1: living costs**

- **Monthly household spending,** honestly measured, multiplied by a realistic search length plus the months after closing before the business pays you reliably.
- **Health insurance.** If you leave a job, COBRA lets you keep your employer's group health insurance for a limited time after a qualifying event such as losing your job.[^healthcare-gov-cobra] Price it against individual coverage before you resign.
- **Other benefits you'll lose:** retirement contributions, disability and life insurance.

**Bucket 2: search and deal expenses**

- **Search costs:** data and lists, software, mail and postage, travel, an entity and a website if you want them.
- **Deal costs:** accountants and a quality of earnings (QoE) review, legal fees, lender fees, valuations, and travel for diligence. Some are paid before closing and lost if the deal dies, so budget for at least one deal that doesn't close.

Get quotes for the big items rather than guessing.

**Bucket 3: the equity you'll need**

For an SBA 7(a) initial acquisition, under SOP 50 10 8.1 (effective October 1, 2026), you must inject at least 10% equity.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-equity-minimum] Two SOP rules affect how search spending interacts with that:

- What you spend on financial due diligence reports (such as the valuation or a QoE) can count toward the equity injection.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-diligence-costs]
- Spending on education, advisory services, or fees paid to an agent doesn't count as equity.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-prepaid-not-equity]

Ask your lender early how it will treat what you've spent.

**Ways to fund the search**

- **Savings.** The simplest; set a firm limit so the search can't eat your equity.
- **Keep your job.** Slower but lower risk. See [keeping your day job](/q/keep-day-job).
- **A partner's or spouse's income.** Agree openly on the budget and the deadline. See [spouse and family](/q/spouse-family).
- **Part-time consulting or contract work.** Keeps income coming, at the cost of search time.
- **Search investors.** In a traditional search fund, investors pay a salary and expenses during the search in exchange for rights in the deal. Raising money from investors brings securities rules. See [traditional search funds](/q/traditional-search-fund) and [Rule 506(b) and 506(c)](/q/506b-506c-securities).

**After closing**

Your budget doesn't end at the closing table. An SBA lender checks that your pay from the business will cover your current obligations and living expenses,[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-owner-compensation] so know your minimum salary and build it into the deal model. See [what the owner takes home](/q/owner-take-home).

**Keep it honest**

Track spending monthly against the budget, and set a review point (a date or a remaining-cash level) when you'll decide whether to continue, change course or stop. See [when a search runs out of time or money](/q/search-ran-out) and [how long a search takes](/q/search-timeline).
