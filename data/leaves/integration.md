**Short answer.** Decide before closing how far to integrate: fully merge the add-on into your business, share only back-office functions, or keep it separate. Then integrate in stages: money and controls first, people and payroll next, systems and processes after, and the name and brand last, once customers and employees are secure. Keep a known brand if customers value it; rebrand when the benefits are clear and you can explain the change.

### Choose the level of integration

- **Full merger.** One company, one name, one team and set of systems. Saves the most overhead and makes cross-selling easier, but disrupts the most.
- **Shared back office.** The acquired company keeps its name and front-line team but uses your accounting, payroll, purchasing and HR.
- **Stand-alone.** Separate operations under common ownership, perhaps in a holding company. Least disruption, least savings. See [running several companies](/q/holdco-operations).

The right level depends on how similar the businesses are, whether customers overlap, how strong the acquired brand is, and how much management capacity you have.

### A staged plan

**Before closing.** Name one person to lead the integration. List what changes on day one, in the first 90 days, and later. Plan communication to employees and customers. Get any required consents for contracts, leases and licenses.

**Day one: money and controls.** Bank access, payment approvals, cards, insurance and access to systems. See [reporting and financial controls](/q/financial-controls-kpis).

**First months: people.** Meet every employee. Compare pay, benefits, titles and policies between the companies; differences become visible quickly once teams work together. Decide how and when to align them, and avoid cutting anyone's pay or benefits abruptly. If acquired employees move to your company, you choose whether to complete new Forms I-9 for them or keep the seller's forms.[^uscis-m274-acquisitions] A buyer that takes over another employer's business doesn't use that employer's EIN (employer identification number) for payroll.[^irs-pub-15-successor-ein] Have your payroll provider handle the switch.

**Next: systems and processes.** Move accounting, scheduling, CRM and other software onto common platforms one at a time, with data cleaned up first. Adopt the better process from either company, not automatically yours.

**Last: name and brand.** See below.

### Rebranding

Reasons to rebrand: one name is easier to market, customers already see the businesses as one, or the acquired name has problems. Reasons to wait or keep the name: customers know and trust it, it carries reviews and search rankings, or the team identifies with it.

If you rebrand:

- Introduce the change in steps, for example "Smith Plumbing, now part of Acme," before dropping the old name.
- Tell customers personally first, then publicly.
- Update everything together: signs, vehicles, uniforms, website, listings, phone greetings, invoices and bank details.
- Check name registrations. You may need to register a DBA (a "doing business as" or trade name) with the state, county or city.[^sba-dba-registration] Consider trademark clearance for a new name.
- Keep the old phone numbers and redirect the old website.

### Common mistakes

- Integrating everything at once.
- Assuming your processes are better without looking.
- Ignoring culture differences between the teams.
- Changing invoices and payment details without warning customers, which can look like fraud.
- No single person accountable for integration.
