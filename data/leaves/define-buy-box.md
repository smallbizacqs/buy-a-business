**Short answer.** A buy box is your written list of criteria for the business you'll buy: size, industry, location, the kind of earnings, your role, and the deal-breakers. It lets you say no quickly, tells brokers what to send you, and keeps you from falling for a business that doesn't fit your money or your life. Build it from what you can afford and what you're willing to do, then test it against real listings.

**What usually goes in a buy box**

- **Size.** A range of earnings (SDE or EBITDA) and price. See [deal size](/q/deal-size).
- **Industry.** Sectors you understand or want to learn, and ones you'll avoid. See [industry selection](/q/industry-selection).
- **Location.** Where you'll live, or whether you'll relocate. See [buying locally](/q/geography-relocation).
- **Earnings quality.** Recurring revenue, customer concentration limits, history of stable or growing results. See [recurring revenue](/q/recurring-revenue).
- **Your role.** Owner-operator or absentee with a manager. See [run it yourself](/q/operator-vs-absentee).
- **Deal terms.** Willingness to use a seller note, real estate included or leased.
- **Deal-breakers.** For example, a business that runs under a license you can't obtain.

**Let the money set the size**

Start from your cash and the financing you plan to use. With an SBA 7(a) loan, under SOP 50 10 8.1 (effective October 1, 2026), an initial acquisition needs at least 10% equity[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-equity-minimum] and historical cash flow covering all debt payments at 1.25:1 or better.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-dsc-ratio] Work back from those to a realistic price and earnings range. Some types of business are ineligible for SBA loans altogether, and the lender must check;[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-ineligible-businesses] if you need SBA financing, rule those out of your box.

**Keep it usable**

Write it on one page. Make the must-haves few and specific, and the nice-to-haves clearly separate. Revisit it after your first few dozen listings, and adjust it once you see what's actually for sale.
