**Short answer.** The "boring business" case is that unglamorous, essential services (the companies that fix, clean, inspect, haul, maintain and supply) have steady demand from customers who need them regardless of fashion, and attract less attention from buyers chasing growth stories. Kinds often named include home and commercial services, the trades, maintenance and inspection work, route-based businesses and B2B distribution. Boring isn't the same as safe, though: labor shortages, competition from other buyers using the same thesis, and dependence on the owner can all apply.

### The case for boring

- **Need, not want.** A broken furnace, a required inspection or a contract cleaning job gets paid for in good times and bad. See [businesses that hold up through recessions and AI](/q/resilient-businesses).
- **Repeat customers.** Maintenance contracts, service agreements and regular routes create revenue that recurs. See [recurring revenue](/q/recurring-revenue).
- **Simple models.** You can understand how the business makes money and what could go wrong.
- **Room to improve.** A business that has run the same way for years may benefit from basic changes (scheduling software, pricing discipline, follow-up on quotes). See [hidden upside](/q/hidden-upside).
- **Physical work.** Tasks done on site by people are harder to replace with software. See [AI disruption](/q/ai-disruption).

### Kinds buyers look at

- **Home services.** HVAC, plumbing, electrical, roofing, pest control, landscaping, pool service.
- **Commercial services.** Janitorial, facilities maintenance, fire and life safety inspection, elevator service, security systems.
- **Trades and specialty contractors.** Sheet metal, concrete, paving, sign installation.
- **Route-based businesses.** Waste collection, uniform service, vending, portable toilets, water delivery.
- **B2B distribution and supply.** Industrial parts, packaging, janitorial supplies.
- **Compliance-driven services.** Backflow testing, environmental testing, document destruction, other services required by regulation.

This is a list of kinds, not recommendations. Each one has its own risks; see [industry selection](/q/industry-selection).

### Where the thesis breaks down

- **Labor.** Skilled trades can be hard to staff. See [labor availability](/q/labor-availability).
- **Crowding.** The thesis is no secret, so a good boring business can draw several bidders and a higher price. See [competition](/q/competition-crowded) and [private equity roll-ups](/q/pe-consolidation).
- **Owner dependence.** In a small trade business, the owner may be the lead technician, estimator and salesperson. See [owner dependence](/q/owner-dependence).
- **Licenses.** Some trades require a licensed person to operate. See [license dependence](/q/license-dependence).
- **Capital needs.** Trucks and equipment wear out. See [capex and equipment](/q/capex-equipment).
- **You.** If the work bores you, you'll struggle to lead it well for years.

### Questions to test a boring target

- Why do customers come back, and what would make them leave?
- Who does the skilled work, and could you hire another if they left?
- What did revenue do in past downturns?
- How many other buyers are chasing businesses like this one?
