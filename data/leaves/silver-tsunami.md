**Short answer.** The aging of business owners is real: the Census Bureau's 2019 Annual Business Survey (data year 2018) found that over half of US business owners were 55 or older.[^census-abs-owner-age-2018] But an older owner isn't the same as a business that's ready, sellable and fairly priced. Treat the "silver tsunami" as a reason owners will be willing to talk, not as a promise of more deals at lower prices.

### What the evidence shows, and what it doesn't

The Census figure describes owners' ages. It doesn't say how many of those businesses will be sold, when, or at what price. Be skeptical of headline figures in marketing material about how much wealth or how many businesses will "change hands": ask where the figure comes from and what it measures. Note too that the Census figure is from 2018; check the Census Bureau's newer Annual Business Survey tables for current data.

### Why an older owner may not mean a deal

- **Not ready to sell.** An owner may put off the decision for years. See [when the owner isn't ready](/q/when-owner-not-ready).
- **Too dependent on the owner.** If the business is the owner's personal relationships or skills, there may be little to transfer. See [owner dependence](/q/owner-dependence).
- **Too small to finance.** A business that pays only the owner's wage may not support a loan plus a salary for you. See [affordability](/q/affordability).
- **Closing instead.** Some owners wind down, sell assets, or pass the business to family or employees rather than selling to an outsider.
- **Other buyers.** Individual searchers, private equity firms and competitors are all looking at the same owners. See [competition](/q/competition-crowded) and [private equity roll-ups](/q/pe-consolidation).

### How the trend can still help you

- **Owners thinking about retirement may welcome a call.** Outreach to owners who haven't listed their business can land at the right moment. See [off-market sourcing](/q/off-market-sourcing).
- **Succession may matter to them.** A retiring owner may care what happens to employees and customers. A credible plan for both can matter alongside price. See [why the owner is selling](/q/why-owner-selling) and [being the buyer of choice](/q/buyer-of-choice).
- **Seller financing.** An owner focused on retirement income may be open to carrying part of the price. See [getting the seller to carry a note](/q/getting-seller-to-carry).
- **Transition help.** A retiring owner may be glad to stay on for a handover. See [the seller's transition period](/q/seller-transition-period).

### Questions to ask in each conversation

- Why are you selling now, and what have you done to prepare?
- Who runs the business when you're away?
- What would you do if no buyer came along?
