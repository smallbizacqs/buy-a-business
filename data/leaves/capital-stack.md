**Short answer.** The capital stack is the list of who puts money into the purchase and in what order they get repaid: senior bank debt first, then any seller note, then equity (your money and any investors'). How you split it decides your monthly payments, your coverage ratio, how much cash you need, and how much risk the seller keeps.

**The usual layers in a small acquisition**

- **Senior debt.** Usually a bank loan, often an SBA 7(a) loan. It's repaid first and secured first.
- **Seller note.** Part of the price the seller agrees to receive later, with interest. It sits behind the bank. See [seller financing](/q/seller-financing-basics).
- **Equity.** Cash from you and any investors. It's paid last and absorbs losses first.

**SBA rules that shape the stack** (SOP 50 10 8.1, effective October 1, 2026)

- **Minimum equity.** At least 10% for an initial acquisition, which can't be reduced or waived,[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-equity-minimum] measured against total project cost, not just the price.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-equity-project-cost]
- **Seller notes as equity.** A seller note counts as equity only if it's subordinated and on full standby, with no principal or interest paid for the life of the 7(a) loan,[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-seller-debt-equity] and such sources can supply no more than half of the required equity.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-equity-limited-sources]
- **Debt cap.** All acquisition debt, including a seller note that isn't on full standby, is capped at the business valuation and must be covered by cash flow.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-total-debt-cap] Any part of the price above the valuation must be paid with equity.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-valuation-gap-equity]
- **Coverage.** All post-closing debt service counts toward the 1.25:1 minimum for an initial acquisition,[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-dsc-ratio] and an interest-only seller note not on standby is counted as if it amortized over no more than 10 years.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-interest-only-debt]

**Trade-offs to model**

More bank debt means less cash from you but higher payments and thinner coverage. A larger seller note keeps the seller invested in the outcome, but if it's paid during the bank loan it counts against coverage. More equity lowers risk and payments but dilutes your return and may need investors. Build each version in a [deal model](/q/deal-model) and compare coverage and cash left over.
