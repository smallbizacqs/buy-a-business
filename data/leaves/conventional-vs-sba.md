**Short answer.** A conventional loan (one with no government guaranty) fits when the deal is strong enough that a lender will make it without SBA's help, when SBA rules block the structure you want, or when the deal is too big for SBA alone. In fact, an SBA loan is supposed to be a last resort: if your deal meets the lender's conventional standards, it isn't eligible for an SBA guaranty.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-conventional-ineligible] Expect a conventional lender to ask for more equity, a shorter term or more collateral in exchange for fewer program rules.

### Why SBA exists for acquisitions

Under SOP 50 10 8.1 (effective October 1, 2026), the lender must certify you can't get the credit on reasonable terms elsewhere without SBA help.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-credit-elsewhere] Needing a longer maturity than the lender's own policy allows is one accepted reason,[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-credit-elsewhere-factors] and that's the core of SBA's appeal: a 10-year amortization on a purchase that's mostly goodwill,[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-change-of-ownership-term] with as little as 10% equity.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-equity-minimum]

### When a conventional loan can be the better fit

- **The SBA rules don't fit your structure.**
  - You want the seller to keep a stake, stay as an employee, or be paid partly through an earnout. In an SBA initial acquisition the seller can stay only as a consultant for up to 24 months,[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-seller-consultant] and seller earnouts are prohibited.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-earnouts]
  - An owner or investor doesn't meet SBA's citizenship test, which covers 100% of owners.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-citizenship-owners]
  - Investors won't sign the personal guaranties SBA requires of 20% owners.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-personal-guaranty]
- **The deal is larger than SBA alone can fund.** The largest Standard 7(a) loan is $5 million.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-max-loan] A conventional loan can sit alongside it on a shared (pari passu) lien if its maturity isn't shorter,[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-pari-passu] but SBA forbids "piggyback" structures where the SBA loan is junior.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-piggyback] See [larger deals](/q/loan-limits-pari-passu).
- **Cost.** An SBA loan carries an upfront guaranty fee that the lender can pass to you.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-upfront-fee] A strong borrower may get a conventional loan with lower total fees.
- **Speed and simplicity.** No SBA eligibility review, forms or program rules. A bank's own process can still be slow.

### Types of non-SBA acquisition debt

- **Conventional bank term loans**, each on the bank's own terms, which can mean a shorter term, more equity and covenants (financial tests you must keep meeting, such as a minimum coverage ratio).
- **Cash-flow loans** sized on the business's earnings rather than its assets.
- **Asset-based loans** sized against receivables, inventory or equipment, useful when the business has substantial hard assets.
- **Seller financing**, alone or alongside a bank. See [seller financing](/q/seller-financing-basics).
- **Mezzanine and private credit** for deals that need more debt than a bank will lend. See [mezzanine and private credit](/q/mezz-private-credit).

### Questions to compare

1. Total equity required, and what counts toward it.
2. Amortization, maturity and any balloon.
3. Covenants and what happens if you trip one.
4. Personal guaranty: required, limited or none.
5. All fees, and prepayment terms.
6. Whether the lender allows the seller note, rollover or earnout you've negotiated.

Run the same deal through both in your [deal model](/q/deal-model) before you decide.
