**Short answer.** Three sets of rules matter: federal email law (CAN-SPAM) for email, federal telemarketing and do-not-call rules for calls and texts, and state laws that can add more. Whether each one applies to an approach about buying someone's business depends on the facts, so the safe baseline is to follow them anyway: identify yourself honestly, include your postal address and an opt-out in emails, don't use autodialers, prerecorded or AI voices, or mass texts without consent, respect do-not-call lists, and stop when asked. This is general information, not legal advice; rules vary by state and change.

**Email: CAN-SPAM**

CAN-SPAM covers email whose primary purpose is the commercial advertisement or promotion of a commercial product or service,[^ftc-can-spam-commercial-message] and it has no exception for business-to-business email.[^ftc-can-spam-b2b] Among its requirements: include a valid physical postal address[^ftc-can-spam-postal-address] and honor opt-out requests within 10 business days.[^ftc-can-spam-opt-out] The FTC puts the penalty at up to $53,088 for each violating email.[^ftc-can-spam-penalty] Details on writing compliant emails are in [cold email](/q/cold-email).

**Calls: do-not-call rules and the TCPA**

- **What a "telephone solicitation" is.** The FCC's rule under the Telephone Consumer Protection Act (TCPA) defines it as a call or message to encourage the purchase or rental of, or investment in, property, goods or services.[^ecfr-47-cfr-64-1200-telephone-solicitation] Whether a call offering to buy an owner's business fits is a question for a lawyer.
- **The national registry.** The FCC's rule bars telephone solicitations to residential subscribers who have registered on the national do-not-call registry,[^ecfr-47-cfr-64-1200-dnc] and its do-not-call rules extend to calls and texts to wireless numbers.[^ecfr-47-cfr-64-1200-wireless] The registry includes landline and wireless numbers.[^fcc-dnc-registry] That matters because the number listed for a small business may be the owner's personal cell phone.
- **The FTC's Telemarketing Sales Rule.** The FTC says most calls between a telemarketer and a business are exempt from it.[^ftc-tsr-b2b-exempt] That exemption is in the FTC's rule; the FCC's rules and state laws are separate.
- **State rules.** Many states have their own do-not-call lists[^fcc-state-dnc-lists] and telemarketing laws.[^ftc-tsr-state-laws] Check the rules, including any limits on calling hours, in each state you'll call.

**Autodialers, prerecorded messages, texts and AI voices**

- FCC rules require consent before an autodialed or prerecorded call or text to a wireless number.[^fcc-robocalls-wireless-consent]
- Commercial texts require written consent; informational texts may rest on oral consent.[^fcc-robotexts-commercial-consent]
- AI-generated voice calls are illegal unless the person has agreed to receive them or the caller is exempt.[^fcc-ai-voice-calls]

If you use calling or texting software, or hire a service, ask what equipment it uses and how it gets and records consent.

**A practical baseline for buyers**

1. Say who you are and why you're contacting them, truthfully, in every message.
2. Call by hand from your own phone; don't use autodialers, prerecorded or AI voices, or bulk texting for cold outreach.
3. Don't text an owner until they've given you permission, for example on a call.
4. Scrub call lists against the national registry and any state list where you call, or confine calls to clear business lines.
5. Call during business hours in the owner's time zone.
6. Keep one do-not-contact list across every channel and honor it immediately.
7. Include your name, postal address and an opt-out line in every email.
8. Keep records of what you sent, when, and any opt-outs.

**When to get advice**

Before running a large calling or texting campaign, using any automated dialing or AI voice tool, or hiring a calling service, have a lawyer review the plan. If you hire counsel, one category to look for is AI-using law firms with fixed fees that are lean and run by an attorney with big-law experience.
