**Short answer.** Make yourself the easiest buyer to work with, ask clearly for what you need and why, and follow up on a steady schedule. If that doesn't work, escalate politely within the brokerage or ask for a call that includes the seller. Don't go around the broker to the seller: a listing broker has an agreement with the seller, and your NDA may forbid direct contact. If a broker pushes you to skip steps, keep your own process.

**Why brokers go quiet**

- **Volume.** A broker may get many inquiries per listing and answer the buyers who look most likely to close.
- **The seller is slow.** Documents and answers have to come from the owner, not the broker.
- **They doubt you.** Missing proof of funds, a vague buy box or a lowball first message moves you down the list. See [looking credible to brokers](/q/broker-credibility).
- **The deal is already moving** with another buyer.

**Getting a response**

1. **Send a complete package once.** A short buyer profile, proof of funds or a lender letter (see [proof of funds](/q/proof-of-funds)), a signed NDA (nondisclosure agreement), and a specific reason this business fits you.
2. **Ask specific questions.** A short numbered list is easier to answer than "send everything."
3. **Ask for a call.** Ten minutes on the phone can move faster than a dozen emails.
4. **Follow up on a schedule.** A brief, polite note every few business days, each with a clear question.
5. **Ask what it takes to move forward.** "What do you need from me to get the financials?" or "What does the seller need to see before a meeting?"

**When information is withheld**

- **Explain why you need it.** Tax returns to match the reported earnings, a lease to confirm the business can stay, a customer breakdown to check concentration. A reason the broker can pass to the seller works better than a demand.
- **Stage the request.** Offer to receive the most sensitive items later, after a meeting or under a letter of intent (LOI), if the summaries are enough to price the business now. See [early documents to request](/q/early-docs-request).
- **Make it a condition.** If something essential is refused, write your LOI so that receiving and verifying it is a condition of closing, or decline to offer.

**When the broker is difficult**

- **Escalate within the firm.** If the brokerage has a managing broker, a polite note asking for help is reasonable.
- **Ask for the seller on a call.** Request a meeting with the owner and broker together.
- **Don't go around them.** On BizBuySell, for example, a broker listing comes from an owner who engaged that broker under an exclusive listing agreement.[^bizbuysell-terms-who-lists] Contacting the seller, employees, customers or suppliers directly can breach your NDA, damage trust and end the deal.

**When the broker pushes**

Artificial deadlines, "best and final" rounds and pressure to waive diligence or sign quickly are pressure tactics. Keep to your process: decide your price from the numbers, make diligence and financing conditions of the deal, and be willing to walk away. Remember whom the broker represents: see [how brokers work](/q/how-brokers-work).

**Know when to move on**

If a broker won't share basic information, won't arrange a meeting, or keeps changing terms, the deal may not be real or the seller may not be ready. Your time is limited; spend it where the other side is working too.
