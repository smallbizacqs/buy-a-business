**Short answer.** With an SBA 7(a) loan, possibly. Under SOP 50 10 8.1 (effective October 1, 2026), if the business's assets don't fully secure the loan, the lender must take available equity in personal real estate owned by each 20% owner and guarantor, including a home.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-personal-real-estate] Real estate with less than 25% equity need not be taken,[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-real-estate-equity-threshold] and the lien can be limited to the shortfall or to 150% of the equity.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-lien-limit]

**How the lender decides**

1. **Is the loan fully secured?** The loan is fully secured when liens on the business's fixed assets (real estate, machinery and equipment), at adjusted values, reach the loan amount.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-fully-secured] A business with few hard assets has little to count toward that test, for example when most of the price is goodwill (value above the hard assets).
2. **If not, what personal real estate is available?** Residential and investment property solely owned by co-borrowers, 20% owners and guarantors.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-personal-real-estate]
3. **Is there enough equity?** Under 25% equity, the lender need not take it.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-real-estate-equity-threshold]
4. **How big a lien?** Up to the collateral shortfall, or capped at 150% of the equity.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-lien-limit]

**Collateral and the guaranty are different**

A lien on your house secures the loan with that property. Your personal guaranty is a separate, unlimited promise to repay if you own 20% or more,[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-personal-guaranty] and it can reach other assets even if no lien is recorded. See [personal guarantees](/q/personal-guarantee).

**Outside SBA lending**

Conventional lenders set their own collateral requirements; ask each lender what it will take.
