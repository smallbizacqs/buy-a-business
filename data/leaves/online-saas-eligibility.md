**Short answer.** Yes. SBA rules don't exclude a business for being online. Under SOP 50 10 8.1 (effective October 1, 2026), a 7(a) loan can finance an acquisition made up mostly of goodwill and other intangibles, as long as SBA's financial due diligence rules are met,[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-intangibles] and the lender can verify an e-commerce business without a physical site visit.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-ecommerce-site-visit] What makes these deals harder is collateral, verifiable history and finding a lender that does them.

### What the rules say

- **Intangibles are fundable.** Goodwill, customer lists, intellectual property and non-competes can be financed in an acquisition.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-intangibles] Financing for intangibles is limited to 10 years.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-maturity-intangibles]
- **No storefront needed.** For a virtual or non-customer-facing business, the lender may skip the site visit if it documents why and confirms the business through other means, such as virtual meetings or business records.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-ecommerce-site-visit]
- **The usual eligibility checks still apply.** The lender must check whether the business type is ineligible,[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-ineligible-businesses] and every owner must meet the citizenship rule, which matters for marketplace deals with sellers or partners abroad.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-citizenship-owners]

### Where online deals get stuck

**Collateral.** SBA's "fully secured" test counts fixed assets: real estate, machinery and equipment.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-fully-secured] Receivables and inventory count for no more than 10% of book value.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-ar-inventory-collateral] A software or content business usually has little of either. When the business can't fully secure the loan, the lender must take available equity in personal real estate of 20% owners and guarantors,[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-personal-real-estate] unless the equity is under 25% of the property's value.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-real-estate-equity-threshold] Expect your home to be part of the conversation. See [your home as collateral](/q/collateral-home).

**History and verification.** The lender analyzes the three most recent fiscal years[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-three-years] and must check the seller's financials against IRS records.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-seller-tax-verification] Coverage is measured on historical results, at 1.25:1 for an initial acquisition.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-dsc-ratio] A fast-growing business with a short history, or one whose books are kept in a payment platform and never matched to tax returns, will struggle on both points.

**Revenue quality.** When a QoE is required, it must assess revenue quality, including customer concentration and contract continuity.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-qoe-revenue-quality] For online businesses, also expect questions about dependence on one platform (an app store, marketplace or ad network), churn and refunds.

### What helps

- Tax returns that match the profit-and-loss statement and the payment processor's payouts.
- Monthly revenue and churn by cohort, or by product, going back three years.
- Platform account transfer confirmed in writing (see [buying an online business](/q/buying-online-business)).
- A lender that has closed SBA loans for online or software businesses before. Ask each lender how many it has done and what it declined and why.

If SBA collateral rules or history requirements don't fit, compare seller financing and conventional or private lenders. See [conventional versus SBA](/q/conventional-vs-sba).
