**Short answer.** Keep pay, benefits and schedules steady, get the first payrolls exactly right, and meet every employee one-on-one in the first weeks. For the few people the business can't easily lose, consider a written retention bonus paid after they stay a set period. What keeps people beyond that is how you lead: listening first, keeping your word, and making changes with them rather than to them.

### First, don't break what they rely on

Pay problems right after a sale can cost you trust quickly. Before closing, confirm with your payroll provider and the seller how the first payday under you will work, including direct deposit, benefits, paid time off balances and any commissions or bonuses earned before closing. Agree in the purchase agreement who pays what was earned before closing.

Paperwork also changes hands. You decide whether to complete new Forms I-9 for all acquired employees or keep the seller's forms,[^uscis-m274-acquisitions] and you collect new Forms W-4 unless an IRS alternative procedure applies.[^irs-pub-15-successor-w4] Explain to employees why you're asking for forms so it doesn't read as a sign of layoffs.

### Find out who matters most, and why they might leave

In the first weeks, meet each person and ask what they do, what gets in their way and what they'd change. Separately, list the people whose departure would hurt most: those with customer relationships, licenses, specialized skills or knowledge no one else has. For each, ask what would make them leave (pay, a competing offer, uncertainty, a bad relationship with you) and address that directly. See [key employee risk](/q/key-employee-risk) for how this looks in diligence.

### Retention bonuses

A retention (or "stay") bonus pays a key employee a set amount if they're still employed on a set date or dates. Points to decide with your lawyer and payroll provider:

- **Who gets one.** A small number of people whose departure would cost real money.
- **Amount and timing.** Paying in installments across the first year keeps the incentive longer than one payment.
- **Conditions.** What happens if you end their employment without cause, or they leave for good reason.
- **Who pays.** Some buyers ask the seller to fund part of it as part of the deal.

Two rules to know. Bonuses are supplemental wages for federal tax withholding.[^irs-pub-15-supplemental-wages] And for employees entitled to overtime, a bonus announced in advance to keep people with the firm is part of the "regular rate" used to calculate overtime pay,[^ecfr-29-cfr-778-211-promised-bonuses] so your payroll provider needs to know about it.

### Things that keep people besides money

- **Clarity.** Tell people early what isn't changing, and say when you'll decide on what might.
- **Follow-through.** Fix one or two things they raised, quickly. It shows you listened.
- **Respect for how things are done.** Ask why before changing a process. See [what not to change right away](/q/dont-change-too-fast).
- **A path.** Some employees want more responsibility, which a new owner can offer.

### Common mistakes

- Promising "nothing will change" when you know some things will.
- Cutting benefits or changing pay plans in the first months to improve margins.
- Spending all your time with customers and the seller, and little with staff.
- Letting rumors fill the gap when you haven't explained a decision.
