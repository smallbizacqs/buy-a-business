**Short answer.** It varies widely, and there's no official figure. A search has two very different parts: finding a business and getting a signed letter of intent (LOI), which is the long and unpredictable part, and taking that deal through diligence, financing and closing, which follows a more predictable sequence. Plan for the search to take longer than you hope, and budget money and patience for a signed deal that still doesn't close.

**The phases**

1. **Setup.** Writing your [buy box](/q/define-buy-box), sorting out your money and financing, and lining up an accountant, lawyer and lender. Doing this first makes everything later faster.
2. **Sourcing and screening.** Reading listings, contacting owners, first calls, signing NDAs (nondisclosure agreements), and reviewing financials. This lasts until you find a business worth an offer and the owner accepts. It's where most of the variation lies.
3. **LOI to close.** Diligence, the loan, the purchase agreement and the closing. An LOI can set an exclusivity period for this work; see [exclusivity](/q/exclusivity).

**What happens between LOI and closing**

In an SBA 7(a) deal, several steps take calendar time no matter how fast you work. Under SOP 50 10 8.1 (effective October 1, 2026):

- The lender verifies the seller's financial data against IRS records,[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-seller-tax-verification] requesting tax transcripts and following up if the IRS hasn't responded in 10 business days.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-transcript-follow-up]
- The lender makes and documents a site visit to the business.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-site-visit]
- For a business purchase price above $350,000, a business valuation comes from an outside qualified source[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-valuation-350k] and at $3 million or more the lender must also get a quality of earnings report (QoE).[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-qoe-threshold]

Add your own diligence, lease and license transfers, and drafting and negotiating the purchase agreement. See [SBA approval timeline](/q/approval-timeline).

**What makes a search longer or shorter**

- **How narrow the buy box is.** A tight industry and a small area mean fewer candidates.
- **How much outreach you do.** Volume and consistency of contacts drive how many real conversations you have. See [deal funnel math](/q/deal-funnel-math).
- **Whether you're full time.** Searching around a job is possible but slower. See [keeping your day job](/q/keep-day-job).
- **Readiness of your money.** Financing lined up before you look lets you move when a deal appears.
- **The sellers.** Owners decide on their own schedule; an owner who isn't ready can take months or years.
- **Deal problems.** Diligence findings, a lender decline, or a lease that won't transfer can send you back to sourcing.

**Planning around the uncertainty**

- Track your funnel weekly so you see progress before you have a deal.
- Set check-in points (for example, every quarter) to review what's working and whether to change the buy box or channels.
- Budget for at least one deal that fails after you've spent money on it. See [paying living costs during the search](/q/search-budget) and [when a search runs out of time or money](/q/search-ran-out).
