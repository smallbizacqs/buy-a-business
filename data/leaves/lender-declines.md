**Short answer.** Lenders decline acquisition loans for a handful of reasons: the business's verified cash flow doesn't cover the payments, the price is higher than the lender's valuation supports, the buyer doesn't have enough equity or relevant experience, the buyer's credit or personal finances raise concerns, the business or deal structure isn't eligible, or the records can't be verified. Ask the lender exactly which reason applied, because each one has a different fix, and a decline from one lender doesn't mean every lender will say no.

### Reasons lenders decline, and the rules behind them

Under SOP 50 10 8.1 (effective October 1, 2026), for SBA 7(a) loans:

- **Cash flow coverage.** For an initial acquisition, the lender must see historical debt service coverage of at least 1.25:1, on the last fiscal year or the average of the last two.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-dsc-ratio] Adjustments the lender hasn't analyzed and justified don't count,[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-unsupported-adjustments] so a deal that works on the seller's add-backs may not work on the lender's. See [DSCR](/q/dscr).
- **Price above value.** If the price is higher than the independent business valuation, the difference must come from equity, not more debt,[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-valuation-gap-equity] and all debt in the deal, including seller debt not on full standby, is capped at the valuation.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-total-debt-cap]
- **Not enough equity.** An initial acquisition requires at least 10% equity, which the lender can't reduce or waive.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-equity-minimum] See [equity injection](/q/equity-injection).
- **Experience and management.** The lender's credit memo describes your industry experience and who will run day-to-day operations.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-management-experience] A buyer new to the industry, with no plan for who runs the work, can raise concerns in underwriting.
- **Personal finances.** Your pay from the business must cover your personal obligations and living expenses.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-owner-compensation] Credit history, existing debts and other liabilities also matter.
- **Eligibility.** Some types of business are ineligible for SBA loans,[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-ineligible-businesses] and a franchise brand must be on the SBA Franchise Directory.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-franchise-directory]
- **Liens, judgments and litigation** against the business or the buyer, which lenders discuss in their credit analysis.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-credit-memo-liens-litigation]
- **Records that don't verify.** Tax returns that don't match the financials, or cash revenue that never reaches the bank. See [proof of cash](/q/proof-of-cash).

Lenders also decline for reasons of their own: the industry, the loan size, the region, or how much of that kind of loan they already hold. And the SBA program isn't for borrowers who can get credit elsewhere: the lender must certify you can't get the loan on reasonable terms without SBA help.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-credit-elsewhere]

### Who said no

A lender with delegated authority makes the decision itself. For loans sent to SBA's processing center, SBA decides eligibility and creditworthiness and approves or declines the loan.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-lgpc-decision] Ask which happened; it changes what you can do next.

### What buyers do next

- **Get the reason in detail.** Ask what would have to change for a yes.
- **Restructure.** A lower price, more equity, a seller note on full standby, a longer transition, or a smaller loan can fix coverage or valuation problems. See [the capital stack](/q/capital-stack) and [seller notes on standby](/q/seller-note-standby). Renegotiating with the seller after a decline is a retrade; see [retrading](/q/retrade).
- **Fix the file.** Missing documents, unclear add-backs or an unexplained gap can sometimes be resolved and resubmitted.
- **Try another lender.** Lenders differ in industries, loan sizes and appetite. Tell the next lender about the earlier decline and why.
- **Strengthen the team.** Bring in an experienced manager or partner if experience was the issue.
- **Listen to it.** Sometimes the lender is seeing what you didn't want to see. See [when to walk away](/q/walk-away).

Ask your lender early what it looks for, so problems surface before you've spent on diligence. See [how lenders underwrite](/q/underwriting) and [eligibility and credit](/q/eligibility-credit).
