**Short answer.** There's no correct multiple to look up. A multiple is just the price divided by an earnings figure (usually SDE or EBITDA), and what you can sensibly pay depends on how reliable and durable those earnings are, how much risk you're taking on, and what a lender will finance. Work out the price the cash flow can support first, then see what multiple that implies, rather than starting from a "market" number.

**How a multiple works**

- **Price ÷ earnings.** A price of 3 times SDE means the price equals three years of SDE. Seller's discretionary earnings (SDE) includes the owner's pay; EBITDA (earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation and amortization) assumes the owner's job is paid. A multiple of one isn't comparable with a multiple of the other. See [SDE vs. EBITDA](/q/sde-vs-ebitda).
- **The earnings matter as much as the multiple.** A fair-looking multiple of inflated earnings is an inflated price. See [add-backs](/q/add-backs).
- **Which year?** A multiple of last year, of a two-year average, or of the trailing 12 months can give very different prices. See [trends](/q/trends-ttm).

**What tends to move a multiple up or down**

- Size and stability of earnings.
- How much revenue recurs, and how concentrated it is among a few customers.
- How much the business depends on the owner.
- Growth or decline, and the industry's outlook.
- How much capital spending it needs to keep running.
- How many buyers (and lenders) want this kind of business.

See [value drivers](/q/value-drivers) for each.

**What a lender lets you pay** (SBA 7(a), SOP 50 10 8.1, effective October 1, 2026)

- The lender orders an independent valuation prepared for the lender,[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-valuation-for-lender] or may do its own at a business purchase price of $350,000 or less unless buyer and seller are closely related.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-valuation-350k]
- Any amount of the price above that valuation must be paid with equity.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-valuation-gap-equity]
- All acquisition debt is capped at the valuation and must be covered by cash flow,[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-total-debt-cap] at 1.25:1 or better for an initial acquisition.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-dsc-ratio]
- When a quality of earnings report is required, it assesses customer concentration, contract continuity and whether revenue will hold after the sale.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-qoe-revenue-quality]

**A way to work it**

Take verified earnings, subtract a market salary for the owner's role and expected capital spending, test the loan payments at the lender's coverage ratio, add the equity you're willing to put in, and see what price results. Then compare that price with the asking price. If they're far apart, see [asking price gap](/q/asking-price-gap).

Valuation multiples quoted in articles, broker reports and marketplaces vary by source and method; this guide doesn't publish a multiple because it can't source one that would apply to your business.
