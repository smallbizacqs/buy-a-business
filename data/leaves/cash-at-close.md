**Short answer.** It's a trade-off between what the seller wants (certainty, cash now) and what protects you (paying part of the price later, so the seller shares the risk that the business underperforms or that something surfaces after closing). There's no standard split. Work it out from what your lender will finance, how much cash you can put in, and what the seller will accept.

**Ways to pay part of the price later:**

- **Seller note**: the seller lends you part of the price and you repay it over time, with interest.
- **Holdback or escrow**: part of the price is held back for a set period to cover claims. See [escrow and holdbacks](/q/escrow-holdback).
- **Earnout**: payment depends on future results. See [earnouts](/q/earnouts).
- **Consulting or transition payments**: paid for the seller's work after closing, which is separate from the price. See [seller consulting agreements](/q/seller-consulting-agreement).

**SBA 7(a) limits.** An SBA 7(a) loan is a bank loan partly guaranteed by the U.S. Small Business Administration (SBA). Under SBA SOP 50 10 8.1 (effective October 1, 2026):

- Seller earnouts are prohibited.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-earnouts]
- Seller debt counts toward your equity only if it is subordinated and on full standby: no principal or interest payments for the life of the SBA loan.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-seller-debt-equity]
- All debt in the deal, including seller debt that isn't on full standby, is capped at the business valuation and must be covered by the business's cash flow.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-total-debt-cap]
- If the price exceeds the valuation, the difference must be equity.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-valuation-gap-equity]

So with SBA financing, the question is less "how much later?" and more "what structure will the lender approve?". Ask your lender early.

**Questions to ask yourself:** What would I lose if the main customer left in month three? Could I recover anything from the seller after paying all cash? How much cash do I need left over for working capital and surprises?
