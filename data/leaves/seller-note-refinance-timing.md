**Short answer.** It depends on the note's terms and on whether there's an SBA loan. Under SOP 50 10 8.1 (effective October 1, 2026), seller debt from an SBA-financed acquisition can be refinanced once it has been in place and current for 36 months.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-seller-note-refinance] A note on full standby can't receive any principal or interest for the life of the 7(a) loan,[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-seller-debt-equity] so paying it off early isn't an option while that loan is outstanding.

**Notes on full standby**

A note that counts toward your equity injection is on full standby: no payments of principal or interest for the term of the 7(a) loan.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-seller-debt-equity] Interest may accrue and be paid after the 7(a) loan is repaid in full, and the seller can't take action without the lender's consent.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-standby-terms]

**Notes that are being paid**

A seller note that isn't on standby is paid on its own schedule. Whether you can prepay it, and whether the SBA lender must consent, depends on the note, the subordination agreement and the loan documents. Read them together before planning an early payoff.

**Without an SBA loan**

Refinancing or prepaying is governed by the note itself and by any senior lender's agreement. Check for prepayment penalties and the senior lender's consent rights.

**Planning ahead**

If you expect to refinance, write the timing and prepayment terms into the note when you negotiate it, and model the payments both ways. Your lawyer and the lender settle the final wording.
