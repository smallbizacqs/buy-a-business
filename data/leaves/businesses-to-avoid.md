**Short answer.** First-time buyers do well to steer clear of businesses that can't be financed, that ride a fad, that need heavy ongoing capital, that depend on one customer or on the seller personally, that need a license you can't hold, or that are already in trouble. Treat these as features to screen for rather than a list of banned industries: the same industry can have a sound business and a trap in it.

### Businesses that can't get the financing you plan to use

If you're counting on an SBA 7(a) loan, check eligibility before you spend money on diligence. Under SOP 50 10 8.1 (effective October 1, 2026):

- Some types of business are ineligible, and the lender must check the list in 13 CFR 120.110.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-ineligible-businesses]
- Speculative businesses, including risky businesses run for the chance of an unusually large profit, are not eligible.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-speculative-businesses]
- A business that hands a third-party management company sole discretion over its operations is an ineligible passive business.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-passive-management-agreement]
- A franchise brand must be on the SBA Franchise Directory.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-franchise-directory]

Ask a lender early. See [SBA 7(a) basics](/q/sba-7a-basics) and [online business eligibility](/q/online-saas-eligibility).

### Features that make a first deal harder

- **Fads and trends.** Revenue from a product or format that's popular now may not last long enough to repay an acquisition loan. Look at several years of results, not the last one. See [trends and trailing results](/q/trends-ttm).
- **Heavy capital needs.** Fleets, machinery or facilities that need constant replacement eat the cash meant for debt and you. See [capex and equipment](/q/capex-equipment).
- **One big customer.** Losing it can sink the deal. See [customer concentration](/q/customer-concentration).
- **The owner is the product.** If customers buy the seller's personal skill or relationships, little transfers to you. See [owner dependence](/q/owner-dependence).
- **Licenses you can't hold.** Some businesses need a credentialed person to operate. Transferable licenses are assets the lender takes as collateral in an SBA change of ownership;[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-transferable-licenses] personal ones don't transfer at all. See [license dependence](/q/license-dependence).
- **Turnarounds.** A declining or distressed business needs fixing skills and capital a first-time buyer may not have. See [distressed turnarounds](/q/distressed-turnaround).
- **Regulatory or environmental exposure.** Contamination, heavy permitting, or rules that could change the economics. See [environmental](/q/environmental) and [regulatory compliance](/q/regulatory-compliance).
- **Unverifiable revenue.** Cash sales that never reached the bank, or books that don't match tax returns. See [cash businesses](/q/cash-business) and [proof of cash](/q/proof-of-cash).
- **Too small to pay a manager.** If the business only pays one person's wage, a bad stretch leaves nothing for debt. See [affordability](/q/affordability).
- **A shrinking market.** Steady today, but in an industry customers are leaving. See [macro exposure](/q/macro-exposure) and [AI disruption](/q/ai-disruption).

### Questions to screen with

- Can this business get the financing I plan to use?
- What happens if the biggest customer, the seller or the key employee leaves?
- How much capital spending does it need each year just to stand still?
- Would the business have earned enough to cover the debt in its worst recent year?

See also [early red flags](/q/early-red-flags) for warning signs in a specific deal.
