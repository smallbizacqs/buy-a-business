**Short answer.** Acquisitions that go wrong after closing can fail for operating reasons that were visible early: customers or key employees leaving with the seller, earnings that were weaker than they looked, too much debt for the cash the business actually produces, a new owner who changes too much too fast or not enough, and losing track of cash. Each has early warning signs you can watch from the first month, and catching them early is what gives you room to act.

### Causes and their warning signs

**Customers leave.** Customers who were loyal to the seller drift away, or a large one leaves.
*Watch:* monthly revenue by top customer, order frequency, days to pay, unreturned calls. See [keeping customers](/q/customer-retention) and [customer concentration](/q/customer-concentration).

**Key people leave.** The person who knew the estimating, held the license or managed the crew goes, sometimes to a competitor.
*Watch:* morale, overtime, recruiters calling, people asking about their future. See [keeping employees](/q/employee-retention).

**Earnings were overstated.** Add-backs that weren't really one-time, costs the seller deferred, or revenue that was unusually high in the last year.
*Watch:* gross margin and overhead against what diligence showed, in the first monthly closes. See [add-backs](/q/add-backs) and [whether you need a quality of earnings report](/q/qoe-need).

**Too much debt.** The deal works only if everything goes to plan, leaving no cushion for a slow quarter.
*Watch:* the weekly cash low point, and coverage of loan payments by cash flow each month. See [debt service coverage](/q/dscr).

**The owner depended on the seller more than expected.** Relationships, know-how or licenses didn't transfer.
*Watch:* how often you or staff still need the seller, and work that stalls without them. See [owner dependence](/q/owner-dependence).

**Changing too much, too fast.** New prices, systems, people and branding at once unsettle customers and staff.
*Watch:* complaints, resignations, reversals of your own decisions. See [what not to change right away](/q/dont-change-too-fast).

**Not changing what needed changing.** The opposite problem: avoiding hard decisions on poor performers, unprofitable customers or pricing.
*Watch:* the same problems on your list month after month.

**Losing control of cash.** No monthly close, unreviewed payments, receivables growing.
*Watch:* late financial statements, rising days sales outstanding, surprises in the bank balance. See [reporting and financial controls](/q/financial-controls-kpis).

**The owner burns out.** Long hours with no support erode judgment.
*Watch:* yourself. See [isolation and burnout](/q/owner-wellbeing).

**Outside shocks.** A lost license, a new competitor, a change in regulation or a downturn in the industry.
*Watch:* industry news, regulatory renewals, competitor activity.

### One warning sign to treat as urgent

Falling behind on payroll taxes. Taxes withheld from employees' pay must be paid over, and the IRS can assess the Trust Fund Recovery Penalty personally against a responsible person who willfully fails to pay them.[^irs-tfrp-who] If cash is tight, talk to your CPA and lender before choosing which bills to pay.

### What to do when you see the signs

- **Act early.** Problems are cheaper to fix in month three than month twelve.
- **Talk to your lender before you miss a payment.** Raising a problem early leaves more room to work something out.
- **Use what the purchase agreement gives you.** If a problem traces to a seller representation, talk to your lawyer about notice deadlines. See [tracking post-closing claims](/q/post-closing-true-up).
- **Get help.** A CPA, an experienced operator in your industry, or a peer group can see what you can't.

For what happens to the buyer when a deal fails (personal guarantees, losing equity), see [failure stories](/q/failure-stories). For problems specific to buying several companies, see [buying too much too fast](/q/too-many-too-fast).
