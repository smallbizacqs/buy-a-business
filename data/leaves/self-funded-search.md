**Short answer.** In a self-funded search you pay your own living and search costs, find a business, and buy it with your own equity plus debt, most often an SBA 7(a) loan and frequently a seller note. You keep most or all of the ownership, and you carry the risk, including a personal guarantee on the loan.

**How it typically runs**

1. **Set your criteria.** Size, industry, location and the role you'll play. See [buy box](/q/define-buy-box).
2. **Line up the money.** Know your cash available for equity and talk to lenders early about what they'll finance.
3. **Search.** Brokers and marketplaces, plus direct outreach to owners. See [brokers and marketplaces](/q/listing-marketplaces) and [off-market sourcing](/q/off-market-sourcing).
4. **Screen, offer, verify, close.** Letter of intent, diligence, loan approval and purchase agreement.
5. **Operate.** You usually become the full-time owner-operator.

**How it's financed with an SBA loan** (SOP 50 10 8.1, effective October 1, 2026)

- **Equity.** At least 10% for an initial acquisition, not reducible.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-equity-minimum] Unborrowed cash, including gifts, can supply all of it.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-equity-unlimited-sources]
- **Seller note.** A note on full standby (no payments during the loan) can count as equity,[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-seller-debt-equity] for no more than half of the requirement.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-equity-limited-sources]
- **Guaranty.** If you own 20% or more, you give an unlimited personal guaranty.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-personal-guaranty]

**Trade-offs compared with an investor-backed search fund**

- **More ownership, more risk.** You keep the upside and the downside.
- **No salary during the search** unless you keep a job or have savings. See [keeping your day job](/q/keep-day-job) and [search budget](/q/search-budget).
- **Smaller deals,** since the size is limited by your equity and what a lender will finance.
- **No investor board,** which means more freedom and less built-in support.

Some self-funded buyers bring in a few outside investors for part of the equity; that's a securities offering. See [Rule 506(b) or 506(c)](/q/506b-506c-securities).
