**Short answer.** Possibly. Trades and professions such as contracting, electrical, plumbing, HVAC, healthcare, accounting, insurance, real estate and alcohol sales are commonly licensed, the rules vary by state, and some rules require the owner or a named person in the company to hold the license. Find out exactly which licenses the business operates under, who holds each, and whether the rules let an unlicensed owner employ a licensed person. Then decide how you'll cover it on day one: get licensed yourself, hire or keep a licensed employee, or don't buy.

**Find out what's required**

Which state, county and city licenses a business needs depends on its activities and its location,[^sba-licenses-permits] and federally regulated activities need a federal license as well.[^sba-licenses-permits-federal] A buyer has to get the licenses and permits from the current owner where they transfer, or apply for them.[^sba-buy-business-licenses]

For each license, find out:

1. **Who holds it**: the business, the owner personally, or an employee.
2. **What the rules require of the owner**: whether the license holder must own part of the company, be an officer, or just be employed full time. This varies by state and by trade.
3. **Whether it transfers.** A license issued to an individual may not move with the business at all. Ask whether each business license or permit can be transferred or must be reissued.
4. **How long a new application takes**, including exams, experience requirements and background checks.

Check with the state licensing board for the trade, not just the seller. Ask in writing if the answer is unclear.

**Options when you don't hold the license**

- **Get licensed yourself** before closing, if the experience and exam requirements are within reach on your timeline.
- **Rely on a licensed employee** where the rules allow a company's license to rest on an employee (sometimes called a qualifier or responsible managing employee). That person becomes very important: see [key employee risk](/q/key-employee-risk). Put their role, pay and notice terms in writing, and plan for a backup.
- **Bring in a licensed partner** with an ownership stake, where the rules require ownership.
- **Delay closing** until a license is in place, as a closing condition.

**SBA loan considerations**

Under SOP 50 10 8.1 (effective October 1, 2026), SBA's definition of a key employee includes someone with the required license to run the business.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-key-employee-definition] In an initial acquisition the seller can't stay on as an employee; they can only consult, for up to 24 months in total.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-seller-consultant] So the seller's own license generally can't be the long-term plan. Transferable licenses conveyed in the purchase, such as a liquor license, must be taken as collateral by the lender.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-transferable-licenses]

**Questions to settle before the LOI**

- Can the business legally operate on the day after closing, and under whose license?
- What happens to open jobs, permits and warranties if the license holder changes?
- If your licensed employee quits, how long until you're shut down?

For permits that move with the business, see [licenses and permits that transfer](/q/licenses-permits-transfer).
