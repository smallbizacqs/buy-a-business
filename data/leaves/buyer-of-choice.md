**Short answer.** Make your offer the one most likely to close, on terms that fit what this seller cares about. Show the money and financing up front, move quickly and predictably, keep your conditions few and clear, and offer the things the seller values beyond price: care for employees, the transition they want, timing, and respect. Price still matters; you're trying to make your offer worth more to the seller than a higher number that might not close.

**What sellers weigh against a higher price**

- **Certainty of closing.** A seller who has watched a buyer fail after months of diligence knows a higher offer is worth nothing if it doesn't close.
- **Cash at closing versus later.** Two offers with the same price can be very different if one pays more at closing and the other relies on a large seller note. See [price versus terms](/q/price-vs-terms) and [cash at close](/q/cash-at-close).
- **Speed and hassle.** A clear timeline and fewer surprises.
- **Fit.** Who will run the business, and how employees and customers will be treated.
- **The relationship.** Whether they trust and like the buyer.

**How to make your offer the most certain**

1. **Show you can pay.** Know your equity, have it documented, and have a lender engaged. For an SBA 7(a) initial acquisition, under SOP 50 10 8.1 (effective October 1, 2026), you must inject at least 10% equity.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-equity-minimum] See [proof of funds](/q/proof-of-funds).
2. **Line up your team.** Name your lender, accountant and lawyer, and have them ready to start.
3. **Give a realistic timeline** for diligence, financing and closing, and then keep to it. See [keeping deal momentum](/q/deal-momentum).
4. **Make the LOI clear.** A letter of intent (LOI) that states price, structure, financing, the transition, and the main conditions avoids later fights. See [what goes in an LOI](/q/loi-contents).
5. **Ask for what you need early.** Requesting key documents before the LOI reduces the chance of a [retrade](/q/retrade) (renegotiating the price after finding problems), which sellers rightly dislike.

**Terms that can be worth more than price**

- **The transition role the seller wants.** Some want to leave quickly; others want time. In an SBA initial acquisition the seller can stay only as a consultant, for up to 24 months in total,[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-seller-consultant] so be clear about what's possible. See [the seller's transition period](/q/seller-transition-period).
- **Timing.** A closing date that suits their tax year, retirement plans or family.
- **Employees.** Commitments you'll keep about staff, written down where appropriate.
- **The name and the building.** Keeping the business name; leasing the seller's building on fair terms if they own it. See [lease or buy the real estate](/q/real-estate-lease-or-buy).
- **Seller financing structure.** If you're asking the seller to carry a note, be clear about how and when it gets paid. Under the SOP, seller debt counts toward an SBA buyer's equity only if it's on full standby, with no payments for the life of the loan,[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-seller-debt-equity] which matters to a seller who's counting on payments. See [getting the seller to carry a note](/q/getting-seller-to-carry).

**Be the buyer they want to deal with**

Respond quickly, do what you say, explain your price from the numbers instead of attacking the business, and treat the seller as a person deciding about their life's work. See [what sellers care about besides price](/q/seller-psychology) and [earning an owner's trust](/q/earn-trust).

**Know your limit**

Decide your walk-away price and terms before you negotiate. If another buyer's offer is higher and just as certain, losing the deal can be better than overpaying. See [how to stand out when it's crowded](/q/competition-crowded).
