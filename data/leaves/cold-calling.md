**Short answer.** Calling owners directly is one of the standard ways buyers reach businesses that aren't for sale. There's no official data on how often it works. What you say matters less than whether you sound like a real person with a specific reason to call: introduce yourself, ask whether they have a moment, explain briefly why their business, and ask about them.

**How a call is usually built**

1. **Permission.** Ask whether it's a good time before saying anything else. If it isn't, ask when to call back.
2. **Who you are, in one or two sentences.** That you're an individual buyer (or a small team) looking to own and operate one business, if that's true.
3. **Why them.** Something specific to their business.
4. **A question, not a pitch.** For example, whether they've ever thought about what happens to the business when they step back.
5. **A small next step.** A follow-up call, a coffee, or permission to send a short note.

**Things to settle before you start**

- **A list.** Which businesses fit your criteria (your [buy box](/q/define-buy-box)), and who the owner is.
- **A record.** A simple log of who you called, when, what they said and when to follow up. Most conversations with owners take more than one contact.
- **The rules on calls and texts.** Check which telemarketing and texting rules apply to how and when you contact businesses in your state before running a calling campaign.

**Calls compared with email and letters**

Calls give you a live reaction and a chance to be remembered; they also take more time per contact and more nerve. Many buyers combine calls with [email](/q/cold-email) and [letters](/q/direct-mail) rather than relying on one channel.
