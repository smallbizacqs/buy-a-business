**Short answer.** Put a dated timeline in writing right after the LOI, assign an owner to every task, start the long-lead items (lender, valuation, quality of earnings, landlord, licenses) in the first week, and send everyone a short status update on a fixed day each week. Deals stall when no one owns the next step, when bad news sits unspoken, or when the seller goes quiet. Much of that can be prevented.

**Build the timeline backward from closing**

List every task that has to be done before closing, who does it, what it depends on, and when it's due. Include:

- **Financing:** lender application, business valuation, quality of earnings if needed, loan approval, loan documents.
- **Diligence:** financial, legal and operational reviews, customer and employee conversations.
- **Third parties:** landlord consent or new lease, license and permit transfers, key supplier and customer consents, franchisor approval, insurance.
- **Documents:** purchase agreement, disclosure schedules, seller consulting agreement, non-compete, closing statement.

Share it with the seller, the broker, your lender, lawyer and CPA, and update it weekly.

**Start the slow items first**

Under SOP 50 10 8.1 (effective October 1, 2026):

- A 7(a) application to buy a business needs the purchase agreement, the business valuation, a pro forma balance sheet and three years of the seller's financial statements,[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-application-business-purchase] so request the seller's documents early. See [early document requests](/q/early-docs-request).
- A lender with delegated (PLP) authority may finish the valuation and QoE after the SBA loan number is issued, as long as they're under way and done before closing.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-plp-valuation-timing]
- Lenders request tax transcripts from the IRS and must follow up if there's no response within 10 business days,[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-transcript-follow-up] so get the seller's signed transcript authorization forms in early.
- Every condition in the lender's approval (equity injection, standby agreements, appraisals) must be met before or at disbursement.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-conditions-before-disbursement] Get the list from your lender and track it.

Landlord consents and license transfers move on someone else's schedule. Start them as soon as the seller agrees to tell those parties. See [lease assignment](/q/lease-assignment) and [approval timeline](/q/approval-timeline).

**Keep communication regular**

- A weekly written update to all parties: what's done, what's next, what's blocked, and who owns it.
- A standing short call with the seller, separate from the advisers.
- Respond to the seller quickly, even if only to say when you'll have an answer.

**Common causes of stalls, and what to do**

- **Missing documents.** Send one consolidated, prioritized request list, not a stream of emails. Offer to help gather records.
- **Bad news found in diligence.** Raise it promptly with the evidence and a proposed fix. Delay makes it worse. See [retrades](/q/retrade).
- **Advisers fighting over small points.** Decide which points matter and tell your lawyer which ones to concede.
- **Seller fatigue or second thoughts.** Remind them of what they wanted from the sale, keep the process visible, and ask directly what's worrying them. See [seller psychology](/q/seller-psychology).
- **Lender delays.** Ask for a written list of what's outstanding and a target approval date.
- **Exclusivity running out.** Track the date and extend in writing before it lapses. See [exclusivity](/q/exclusivity).
