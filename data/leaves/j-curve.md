**Short answer.** There's no standard timeline. Owner pay can dip after closing because loan payments are heaviest early, the transition costs money, and some customers or staff may leave when ownership changes. It recovers as the debt is paid down, a seller note ends or is refinanced, and earnings grow. The shape is called a J-curve: down first, then up. Plan your household finances for the dip rather than hoping to avoid it.

### Why pay dips first

- **Debt service.** Under SOP 50 10 8.1 (effective October 1, 2026), an SBA 7(a) acquisition loan amortizes over no more than 10 years with no balloon,[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-change-of-ownership-term] so principal payments start right away.
- **Replacing the seller.** If you hire someone to do part of what the seller did, that salary comes out of the cash flow. See [owner replacement cost](/q/owner-replacement-cost).
- **Transition costs.** New systems, insurance, professional fees and catch-up on deferred maintenance.
- **Change-of-ownership losses.** A customer or key employee who was loyal to the seller may leave. See [keeping customers](/q/customer-retention).
- **Learning-curve mistakes.** Pricing, hiring or purchasing errors while you learn.
- **Reinvestment.** Profit you choose to put back into the business instead of paying yourself.

### What the lender requires

For an SBA loan, the lender checks that your compensation covers your obligations and living expenses,[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-owner-compensation] and analyzes whether working capital is adequate for at least the next 12 months.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-working-capital-adequacy] So the plan on paper must already include a livable salary. The dip comes when results fall short of that plan or you choose to reinvest.

### What makes pay recover

- **Debt paid down.** As earnings grow, loan payments take a smaller share of cash flow, and once the loan is repaid they stop.
- **The seller note.** Seller debt on full standby (no payments for the life of the 7(a) loan) can count as equity for SBA purposes,[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-seller-debt-equity] which keeps it from taking cash early. Seller debt that is paying can be refinanced once it has been in place and current for 36 months.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-seller-note-refinance] See [seller note standby](/q/seller-note-standby) and [refinancing the seller note](/q/seller-note-refinance-timing).
- **Growth.** Price increases, new customers and better margins. See [raising prices](/q/raise-prices) and [value creation](/q/value-creation).
- **Refinancing.** Once the business has a record under you, other loan terms may become available. See [refinance or recap](/q/refinance-recap).

### How to plan for it

- Build a monthly household budget and a personal reserve that covers a stretch of lower pay.
- Model the deal with a downside case: lower revenue, a lost customer, a slower handover. See [stress testing](/q/stress-test).
- Decide in advance how much profit you'll reinvest versus pay yourself.
- Talk it through at home. See [talking it through with family](/q/spouse-family).
