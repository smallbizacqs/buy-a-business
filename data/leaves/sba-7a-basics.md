**Short answer.** An SBA 7(a) loan is a bank loan that the U.S. Small Business Administration partly guarantees, which lets lenders finance purchases they might not otherwise fund. You apply to a lender, not to the SBA.[^sba-7a-loans-page] For a business purchase the loan is capped at $5 million, amortizes over no more than 10 years, needs at least 10% equity from you and a personal guaranty from each 20% owner, and must be covered by the business's historical cash flow.

The rules here are from SOP 50 10 8.1 (effective October 1, 2026), the SBA's lender rulebook, which applies to loans given an SBA loan number on or after that date.[^sba-information-notice-5000-880695] SBA rules change often; check the current SOP.

**The basics**

- **Use.** A change of ownership, complete or partial, is a listed use of 7(a) proceeds,[^sba-7a-loans-page-uses] by stock purchase or asset purchase.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-asset-or-stock]
- **Size and guaranty.** Up to $5 million for a Standard 7(a) loan.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-max-loan] The SBA guarantees up to 75% of a loan over $150,000 (85% at or below), with a maximum guaranty of $3.75 million.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-guaranty-percentage] The guaranty protects the lender, not you.
- **Term.** No more than 10 years of amortization for the business purchase (special purpose property aside), and no balloon payment.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-change-of-ownership-term]
- **Equity.** At least 10% for an initial acquisition, which can't be reduced or waived.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-equity-minimum] See [equity injection](/q/equity-injection).
- **Coverage.** Historical cash flow must cover all debt payments at least 1.25:1 for an initial acquisition.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-dsc-ratio] See [DSCR](/q/dscr).
- **Guaranty.** Every individual owning 20% or more gives an unlimited personal guaranty.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-personal-guaranty] See [personal guarantees](/q/personal-guarantee).
- **Valuation.** The lender orders an independent business valuation prepared for the lender.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-valuation-for-lender]
- **Need.** The lender certifies you can't get the credit on reasonable terms without SBA help.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-credit-elsewhere]

**Deal terms the rules affect**

- Seller earnouts are prohibited.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-earnouts]
- In a full purchase the seller can't stay as an owner or employee but may consult for up to 24 months in total.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-seller-consultant]
- A seller note counts as equity only on full standby; see [seller note standby](/q/seller-note-standby).

Whether a particular deal qualifies is the lender's decision.
