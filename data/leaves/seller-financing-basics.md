**Short answer.** Seller financing is when the seller takes part of the price as a loan to the buyer (a seller note), repaid over time with interest. Sellers agree to it to make a sale possible, to get a higher price, or because a bank won't lend the full amount; buyers want it because it reduces the cash and bank debt needed and keeps the seller invested in the business doing well. How much a seller carries is negotiated deal by deal.

**Why sellers agree**

- A bank may lend less than the price, and the note fills the gap.
- It can support a higher headline price.
- It signals the seller believes the business will keep performing.
- It can spread the seller's proceeds over time (the tax effect is a question for the seller's own adviser).

**Why it matters to the buyer**

- Less cash at closing and a smaller bank loan.
- A seller who is owed money has a reason to help the transition go well.
- If the business underperforms, the note's terms decide what happens; see [what if you can't pay the seller note](/q/set-off-default).

**How SBA rules treat a seller note** (SOP 50 10 8.1, effective October 1, 2026)

- **As equity only on full standby.** A note counts toward your equity injection only if it's subordinated and receives no principal or interest for the life of the 7(a) loan,[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-seller-debt-equity] and it can supply no more than half of the required equity.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-equity-limited-sources]
- **As debt otherwise.** A note that is paid during the loan counts toward the total debt cap (the business valuation) and must be covered by cash flow.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-total-debt-cap] If it's interest-only, the lender counts it as if it amortized over no more than 10 years.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-interest-only-debt]
- **Refinancing.** Seller debt from the acquisition can be refinanced once it has been in place and current for 36 months.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-seller-note-refinance]
