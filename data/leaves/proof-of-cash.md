**Short answer.** A proof of cash compares the money that actually went in and out of the business's bank accounts with the revenue and expenses on the profit and loss statement (P&L) and the tax returns, period by period. If the deposits don't support the reported revenue, or there are withdrawals the books don't explain, you've found something to resolve before you rely on the earnings.

**How it works**

1. Collect bank statements for every business account for the periods you are testing (for example, each full year you rely on plus the year to date), plus the P&L and tax returns for the same periods.
2. Total the deposits each month. Remove transfers between the business's own accounts, loan proceeds, owner contributions and refunds.
3. Compare adjusted deposits with reported revenue, allowing for timing (sales on credit, card processor delays).
4. Do the same for disbursements against reported expenses.
5. List every difference and ask the seller to explain it with documents.

**What it catches**

Revenue that's overstated, revenue booked in the wrong period, personal spending through the business, undisclosed expenses or debts, and gaps between the books and the tax returns.

**When the SBA requires one**

Under SOP 50 10 8.1 (effective October 1, 2026), a quality of earnings (QoE) report is required for an initial acquisition or business expansion priced at $3 million or more,[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-qoe-threshold] and that report must include a cash proof that reconciles bank statement data to the income statement and tax return, for the trailing 12 months and the last two fiscal years.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-qoe-cash-proof] Separately, the lender verifies the seller's financial data against IRS records,[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-seller-tax-verification] including the numbers behind the business valuation.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-valuation-verified]

**Below the QoE threshold**

Smaller deals often have no required QoE, which makes the buyer's own reconciliation more important, not less. An accountant can do it, or you can do a first pass yourself and have an accountant review the differences.
