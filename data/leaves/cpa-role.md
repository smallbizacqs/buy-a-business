**Short answer.** Your accountant works for you: checking whether the seller's numbers hold together, advising on tax structure and the price allocation, setting up your entity and books, and preparing your personal financial information for the lender. That's different from a quality of earnings (QoE) provider, which does a deeper independent review and, when an SBA loan requires one, works for the lender. In a smaller deal without a QoE, your CPA may be the main check on the seller's numbers.

**Before the LOI**

- **A first read of the financials.** Do the tax returns, P&Ls and balance sheets tie together? Are the add-backs believable? Does anything look off? See [add-backs](/q/add-backs) and [why tax returns don't match the P&L](/q/tax-returns-vs-books).
- **Your take-home after debt and taxes**, so you know what price works for you. See [what owners earn](/q/owner-take-home).
- **Structure.** Asset vs. stock purchase changes taxes for both sides. See [asset vs. stock](/q/asset-vs-stock) and [tax structuring](/q/tax-structuring).

**During diligence**

- **Financial review.** In a deal without a QoE, your CPA may do a lighter version: tying revenue to bank deposits, reviewing payroll, checking sales tax and payroll tax filings, looking at receivables and payables. See [proof of cash](/q/proof-of-cash) and [tax due diligence](/q/tax-dd).
- **Working with the QoE.** Where an SBA loan requires a QoE, it must be independent, done for the lender, and not prepared by or for the seller.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-qoe-not-for-seller] It reconciles the seller's accountant-prepared statements, tax returns, internal statements and IRS transcripts into normalized earnings.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-qoe-reconcile] Your CPA can read it with you and raise follow-up questions.
- **Working capital.** Helping set the working capital target and definitions. See [the peg](/q/peg).

**Your lender package**

Under SOP 50 10 8.1 (effective October 1, 2026), owners of 20% or more give a personal financial statement dated within 90 days,[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-owner-financial-statement] and the lender runs a global cash flow analysis, including your personal finances, at 1:1 coverage.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-global-cash-flow] Your CPA can prepare or check these and your personal tax returns. The SBA business valuation, by contrast, is ordered by and prepared for the lender,[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-valuation-for-lender] so your CPA doesn't prepare it.

**At and after closing**

- **Price allocation.** In an asset purchase, both buyer and seller report the sale on IRS Form 8594,[^irs-form-8594-who-files] and the price is allocated across asset classes using the residual method, which sets your tax basis in each asset.[^irs-form-8594-residual-method] The allocation is negotiated with the seller and decided with your CPA.
- **Entity and tax setup.** The type of entity determines which tax return the business files.[^irs-business-structures] Your CPA advises on the choice, elections and payroll setup. A buyer who takes over another employer's business doesn't use the seller's employer identification number.[^irs-pub-15-successor-ein] See [setting up your entity](/q/entity-setup).
- **Opening books and controls.** Opening balance sheet, chart of accounts, monthly closing, and the controls you'll use to run the business. See [financial controls](/q/financial-controls-kpis).

**Choosing one**

Look for a CPA who works with small businesses in your industry and has been through acquisitions. Ask how they charge, what they'll do in diligence and what they won't, and whether they can keep doing your books and taxes after closing. See [what advisers cost](/q/advisor-costs) and [who's on your team](/q/deal-team).
