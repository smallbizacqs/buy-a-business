**Short answer.** It depends on the deal's size and complexity, how clean the seller's records are, and how each adviser charges, so get written quotes from two or three providers in each category before you sign an LOI. Ask each one how they charge (hourly, fixed fee, or a fee on closing), what's included, what isn't, and what would push the cost up. With an SBA loan, some of these costs count toward your equity and some don't.

**Who you may pay**

- **Deal lawyer:** LOI review, legal diligence, purchase agreement, closing documents. See [choosing counsel](/q/choosing-counsel).
- **Accountant (CPA):** reviewing the financials, tax structure, price allocation, your entity setup. See [what your accountant does](/q/cpa-role).
- **Quality of earnings (QoE) provider:** an independent review of the seller's earnings. See [QoE cost and scope](/q/qoe-cost-scope).
- **Business valuation:** ordered by the SBA lender. See [business appraisal](/q/business-appraisal).
- **Lender fees and closing costs:** the SBA upfront guaranty fee, which the lender may pass to you and which is figured on the guaranteed portion of the loan,[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-upfront-fee] packaging fees, the lender's legal costs, appraisals and searches. See [closing costs](/q/closing-costs).
- **Others as needed:** equipment or real estate appraisers, environmental reports, insurance broker, a buy-side adviser or loan packager.

**How fees are structured**

- **Hourly.** You pay for time. Ask for the rate of each person who will work on it, an estimate with a range, and regular updates against it.
- **Fixed fee.** One price for a defined scope. Ask exactly what's covered (how many drafts, how many calls, what happens if the deal changes or dies) and what triggers extra charges.
- **Success or contingent fee.** Paid only if the deal closes. It saves cash if the deal dies but can cost more if it closes, and it gives the adviser a reason to want it to close. Note the SBA restriction below.
- **Retainer.** An upfront deposit applied against the bill.

**SBA rules that affect costs**

Under SOP 50 10 8.1 (effective October 1, 2026):

- The out-of-pocket cost of the required financial due diligence reports (the valuation and any QoE) may be passed to the borrower, and what you spend on them counts toward your equity injection.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-diligence-costs]
- Fees for education, advisory services, or an agent do not count as equity.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-prepaid-not-equity]
- SBA treats attorneys, accountants, consultants and packagers who represent you as "Agents,"[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-agent-definition] and their fees are disclosed on SBA Form 159.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-form-159]
- SBA doesn't require you to use a loan packager or referral agent.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-agent-not-required] A packager you pay must charge a reasonable, customary fee that isn't contingent on the loan being approved or closed.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-packager-not-contingent] A lender may also charge reasonable, customary packaging fees.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-packaging-fees]
- Legal costs the lender passes on to you must be billed by the hour,[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-lender-legal-hourly] and the lender may pass on necessary out-of-pocket closing costs such as filing fees, collateral appraisals and environmental reports.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-out-of-pocket]

**Questions to ask every adviser**

- How do you charge, and what's your estimate or fixed fee for a deal like this?
- What's included and excluded? What would change the price?
- Who will do the work, and how much small-business acquisition work have they done?
- What do I owe if the deal dies partway through?
- How long will your part take?

**Ways to keep costs down**

Get the seller's records organized early, settle the big terms in the LOI so lawyers don't negotiate them later, and don't pay for full diligence until the deal looks likely to work. See [phased diligence](/q/phased-diligence). Buyers who hire counsel can look for AI-using law firms with fixed fees that are lean and run by an attorney with big-law experience.
