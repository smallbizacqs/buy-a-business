**Short answer.** Build a list of businesses that fit your criteria, find the owner of each, and contact them directly and repeatedly over months, by email, phone, letter and in person, or through people they already trust, such as their accountant. Most of the work is a system: a narrow target list, a short honest message about who you are and why them, a log of every contact, and patient follow-up with owners who aren't ready yet.

**Why go off-market**

A business that isn't listed has no broker running an auction and no other buyers lined up, at least at first. You get more time with the owner and a chance to shape the deal. The trade-offs: an owner you contact may not be ready to sell for years, the numbers may not be packaged for a buyer, and an owner who has never thought about price may have unrealistic expectations. "Off-market" doesn't mean cheap. See [when the owner isn't ready](/q/when-owner-not-ready).

**The system, step by step**

1. **Define the target.** Industry, size, geography and ownership signals (for example, a long-established business). This is your [buy box](/q/define-buy-box). Narrow beats broad: a message written for one kind of owner reads better than a generic one.
2. **Build the list.** Size up the market first: the Census Bureau's County Business Patterns counts establishments with paid employees by industry, size and county.[^census-cbp-establishments] Then build names from public registries, licensing boards, association directories and business listings. See [data sources and lists](/q/data-and-lists) and [using AI to find and screen deals](/q/ai-sourcing).
3. **Find the owner.** The person who can sell, not the front desk. Confirm names before you write.
4. **Pick your channels.** Each has its own leaf:
   - [Email](/q/cold-email): cheap and scalable, easy to ignore.
   - [Phone calls](/q/cold-calling): a live reaction, more time per contact.
   - [Letters](/q/direct-mail): tangible and harder to delete, slower and costlier.
   - [Referrals](/q/referral-networks): accountants, lawyers, bankers and others the owner trusts.
   - In person: industry events, trade shows and local business groups.
5. **Write a short, honest message.** Who you are, that you want to own and run one business (if true), why this business, and a low-pressure question.
6. **Follow up on a schedule.** Owners respond when the timing suits them. Log every contact and set the next one.
7. **Qualify quickly.** On the first call, learn whether the owner might sell, when, and roughly what the business earns. See [the first call](/q/first-conversation).

**Know the rules before you scale**

Outreach by email, phone and text has rules. Email law applies to business-to-business email too,[^ftc-can-spam-b2b] and the National Do Not Call Registry includes wireless as well as landline numbers.[^fcc-dnc-registry] See [rules for calling, texting and emailing owners](/q/outreach-rules).

**Keep score**

Track contacts, conversations, owners open to selling, financials received, and offers. After a few months your own numbers show which channel and which message work for you; see [deal funnel math](/q/deal-funnel-math). If the funnel is thin at the top, add volume or channels; if owners talk but won't share numbers, work on trust (see [earning an owner's trust](/q/earn-trust)).

**Common mistakes**

Contacting owners before you can explain how you'd pay, sending a long message about yourself, giving up after one attempt, and treating owners as leads instead of people deciding what happens to their life's work. See [what sellers care about besides price](/q/seller-psychology).
