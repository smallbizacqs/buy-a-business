**Short answer.** "Not yet" is a common answer and not the same as "no". Ask what "ready" would look like for them, agree on when to check back, and then actually check back. Keep the relationship warm without pressing for a decision.

**Find out what's behind "not ready"**

Owners say "not ready" for different reasons, and each points to a different next step:

- **Timing.** They plan to sell in a few years (a child finishing school, a lease ending, a target age). Note the date and the reason.
- **Uncertainty about life after selling.** Some owners don't know what they'd do next. Talking about how a transition could work (a period of consulting, a gradual handover) can matter more than price.
- **Price expectations.** They may believe the business is worth more than a buyer would pay. That gap rarely closes in one call.
- **They don't know you yet.** Trust takes repeated contact; see [earning an owner's trust](/q/earn-trust).

**Staying in touch**

- Agree on a specific time to reconnect, rather than "I'll be in touch".
- Keep notes after every contact so each follow-up builds on the last one.
- Send something useful or personal occasionally, not just "are you ready now?"
- Respect a clear "no". Following up after someone asks you to stop damages your reputation in a small industry.

**Your own search**

Owners who aren't ready rarely fit a short search timeline. Keep looking at other businesses in parallel, and treat these conversations as a pipeline that may produce a deal later.
