**Short answer.** There's no standard. A seller note's rate, length, amortization (how fast principal is paid down) and security are negotiated, and the right answer is whatever the business's cash flow can carry after the bank is paid, at a rate the seller accepts. If an SBA loan is involved, its rules set the outer limits: a note counted as equity can't be paid at all while the SBA loan is outstanding,[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-seller-debt-equity] and any note that is paid is counted in the coverage test.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-dsc-definition]

### The terms to negotiate

- **Interest rate.** Set by negotiation. Too low a rate can create tax issues: if a sale calls for later payments with little or no interest, the IRS may treat part of the payments as interest (unstated interest or original issue discount).[^irs-pub-537-unstated-interest] Have your CPA and the seller's check the rate.
- **Term and amortization.** How many years, and whether payments cover principal from the start or only interest at first. A longer term lowers the payment; a shorter one gets the seller paid sooner.
- **Balloon.** A large final payment lowers payments until then but creates a refinancing need on a fixed date. Make sure the business can realistically refinance or pay it.
- **Payment start date.** Payments can start at closing, after a delay, or, on an SBA standby note, only after the SBA loan is repaid.
- **Security.** Whether the note is secured by the business's assets, a pledge of your shares, or a personal guarantee, and where it ranks behind the bank. See [capital stack](/q/capital-stack).
- **Prepayment.** Your right to pay early without penalty, so you can refinance. See [paying off a seller note early](/q/seller-note-refinance-timing).
- **Default and offset.** What counts as a default, how long you have to cure it, and whether you can offset indemnity claims against what you owe. See [default and set-off](/q/set-off-default).

### What SBA rules change

Under SOP 50 10 8.1 (effective October 1, 2026):

- **Full standby notes.** To count toward your equity injection, the note must be subordinated and on full standby, with no principal or interest paid for the term of the 7(a) loan.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-seller-debt-equity] It may accrue interest to be paid after the 7(a) loan is repaid, and the seller must subordinate liens and take no action without the lender's consent.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-standby-terms] Such a note, with other "limited" sources, can supply no more than half of the required equity.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-equity-limited-sources] See [seller note standby](/q/seller-note-standby).
- **Paying notes.** A note that is paid while the SBA loan is outstanding counts in the debt service used for the coverage test.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-dsc-definition] If it's interest-only, the lender treats it as if it amortized over no more than 10 years.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-interest-only-debt]
- **A cap on total debt.** All debt in the deal, including seller debt not on full standby, is limited to the business valuation and must be covered by cash flow.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-total-debt-cap]
- **Refinancing later.** A seller note can be refinanced with SBA financing only after 36 months in place and current, and only if the payment improves by at least 10%.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-seller-note-refi-terms]

### How to land on numbers

1. Model the bank payment first, then see how much cash flow is left for the seller after a cushion for a bad year. See [build a deal model](/q/deal-model).
2. Offer the seller a trade: a higher rate for a longer term, or a security interest for a lower rate.
3. Put the term sheet in the LOI so the lender sees it early, and make the note's final terms match the lender's standby or subordination agreement.

Seller financing basics and how much sellers carry are in [why and how much will a seller finance](/q/seller-financing-basics).
