**Short answer.** Searching where you already live is simpler: you know the market, you can be in the business every day, and owners can meet you. Searching more widely gives you more deals to choose from, but if you'll run the business yourself, you'll have to live near it. Decide before you start whether you'd move, for what kind of deal, and with what limits, so a good deal elsewhere doesn't make the decision for you.

**The case for local**

- **You'll probably be there every day.** If you'll run the business yourself, expect to be on site, at least at first. A lender writes up who will manage daily operations onsite, or how they'll be managed if the owners aren't there daily.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-management-experience]
- **You understand the market.** Local wages, competitors, customers and landlords.
- **Owners trust neighbors.** Being able to meet in person and say you live nearby helps with sellers who care about their employees and community.
- **Diligence is easier.** You can visit often, talk to customers and watch the business work. The SBA lender also has to make and document a site visit to the business being acquired.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-site-visit]

**The case for widening the search**

- **More deal flow.** A metro area or a region has more businesses in your size and industry than one town.
- **Better fit.** The right industry and size may simply not be for sale near you this year.
- **Price and competition** vary by market.

**If you'd move for a deal**

1. **Set the rules now.** Which places your household would accept, what has to be true about the deal, and the latest point you'd decide (for example, before signing a letter of intent).
2. **Plan the timing.** Moving before closing risks moving for a deal that falls through; moving after can mean commuting through diligence and the transition. One approach is to visit repeatedly before signing and move around closing.
3. **Check what changes with the state.** Which state, county and city licenses a business needs depends on its activities and location,[^sba-licenses-permits] and the buyer has to get needed licenses from the current owner or apply for them.[^sba-buy-business-licenses] Taxes, employment rules and non-compete enforcement also vary by state; ask a local CPA and lawyer early.
4. **Budget for it.** Moving costs, a possible gap in household income, and time to learn a new market.

**Buying far away and not moving**

Owning a business you don't run day to day means hiring and trusting a manager from day one. It also limits SBA financing if you'd hand control to an outside management company: under SOP 50 10 8.1 (effective October 1, 2026), a business that gives a third-party manager sole discretion over operations is an ineligible passive business.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-passive-management-agreement] See [run it yourself or hire a manager](/q/operator-vs-absentee).

**How to write it into your search**

In your [buy box](/q/define-buy-box), state your home radius, any second region you'd move to, and the conditions for moving. Tell brokers and owners clearly, since an out-of-area buyer raises an obvious question: "Will you actually live here and run it?" Have a specific answer.
