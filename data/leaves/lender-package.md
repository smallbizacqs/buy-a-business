**Short answer.** For an SBA 7(a) acquisition loan (a bank loan partly guaranteed by the U.S. Small Business Administration), expect to give the lender information about you (application forms, a personal financial statement, your tax returns and résumé), about the business (the seller's financial statements and tax data, the lease), and about the deal (the purchase agreement, and later the valuation). Ask your lender for its exact checklist on day one and keep everything in one folder.

**What the SBA rules list.** Under SBA SOP 50 10 8.1 (effective October 1, 2026), Appendix 20:

- **SBA Form 1919** (Borrower Information Form): each co-borrower completes and signs its own.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-form-1919]
- **Personal financial statements** (SBA Form 413 or the lender's equivalent) from every owner of 20% or more and proposed guarantors, dated within 90 days of submission; business financial statements must be within 120 days.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-owner-financial-statement]
- **For buying a business**: the purchase agreement, the business valuation, a pro forma balance sheet (a projected balance sheet as of the transfer date), and the seller's financial statements for the last 3 fiscal years.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-application-business-purchase] The lender's file must include the purchase agreement with all schedules and amendments.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-purchase-agreement-in-file]
- **A copy of the lease**, if there is one.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-application-lease-copy]
- **Tax transcripts**: the lender gets these from the IRS using Form 4506-C or Form 8821, and will tell you who needs to sign what.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-transcript-follow-up]

**What lenders commonly add:** a résumé showing your management or industry experience, a short business plan and projections, a sources-and-uses table (where every dollar comes from and goes), proof of where your down payment comes from, and your personal tax returns.

**Tips:** make sure the seller's numbers match across the listing, the financial statements and the tax returns before you submit; explain any gaps in writing; and answer the lender's questions quickly, since missing documents slow everything down.
