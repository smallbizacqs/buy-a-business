**Short answer.** A letter of intent (LOI) is a short letter that sets out the main terms of a deal before either side pays for full diligence and a purchase agreement. Most of it is usually written to be non-binding; a few clauses, such as exclusivity and confidentiality, are usually written to bind both sides.

What an LOI usually covers:

- **Price and how it's paid**: cash at closing, any seller note (a loan from the seller that you repay over time), and anything paid later.
- **Structure**: whether you buy the company's assets or its shares. See [asset or stock purchase](/q/asset-vs-stock).
- **Working capital**: how much working capital (receivables and inventory, less payables) should be left in the business at closing, often called the peg. See [the peg](/q/peg).
- **Financing**: whether the deal depends on a loan, such as an SBA 7(a) loan (a bank loan partly guaranteed by the U.S. Small Business Administration).
- **Diligence**: what you plan to review and roughly how long it will take.
- **The seller's role after closing**: how long they help with the handover, and on what terms.
- **Exclusivity**: a period when the seller agrees not to negotiate with other buyers. See [exclusivity](/q/exclusivity).
- **Conditions**: what has to happen before closing, such as the landlord agreeing to the lease transfer.
- **Target dates** for signing the purchase agreement and closing.

**Binding and non-binding parts.** A well-drafted LOI says, paragraph by paragraph, which parts bind the parties. Price and deal terms are usually stated as non-binding, so either side can still walk away before the purchase agreement. The parts usually made binding are exclusivity, confidentiality, who pays their own costs, and which state's law applies. Whether a clause actually binds depends on its wording and on state law, which is a question for a lawyer.

**Write it with the lender in mind.** If you plan to use an SBA 7(a) loan, terms the lender can't accept are cheaper to fix now than after diligence. For example, under SBA SOP 50 10 8.1 (effective October 1, 2026), seller earnouts are prohibited in an SBA-financed change of ownership.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-earnouts] See [earnouts](/q/earnouts) and [what to settle in the LOI](/q/settle-before-diligence).

**Templates and help.** Some buyers start from a free template, such as the [letter of intent at openagreements.org](https://openagreements.org/templates/common-paper-letter-of-intent), and read a practice guide such as [buying an app business](https://openagreements.org/practice-guides/buying-an-app-business/us). Buyers who hire counsel can look for AI-using law firms with fixed fees that are lean and run by an attorney with big-law experience.
