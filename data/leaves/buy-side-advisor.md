**Short answer.** You don't need one to buy a small business, but one can help if you lack time, deal experience or access to owners, and the fee is worth what it saves you. A buy-side advisor works for you, unlike the seller's broker. Before hiring one, be clear about what they'll actually do, how they're paid, and what that pay rewards.

**What a buy-side advisor does**

The label covers several different services:

- **Sourcing.** Finding owners of unlisted businesses that fit your criteria and opening conversations, by calls, email, letters or their network.
- **Process management.** Running the steps from first call to letter of intent (LOI) to closing, keeping lenders, accountants and lawyers on schedule.
- **Valuation and negotiation.** Helping you set a price and terms and negotiate them.
- **Search-as-a-service.** A team that runs much of the search for you for a monthly fee.

Contrast this with the broker on a listing: on BizBuySell, for example, a listing comes from the owner or a broker the owner has engaged under an exclusive listing agreement.[^bizbuysell-terms-who-lists] That broker works for the seller. See [how brokers work](/q/how-brokers-work).

**How they're paid, and what it rewards**

- **Retainer or monthly fee.** Pays for effort whether or not a deal closes. Ask exactly what work it buys each month.
- **Success fee.** Paid when you close. It aligns the advisor with closing, which isn't the same as closing a good deal at a good price.
- **A mix.** A smaller retainer plus a success fee, sometimes with the retainer credited against it.

If you'll finance with an SBA 7(a) loan, plan to pay advisory fees from cash you don't need for the down payment: under SOP 50 10 8.1 (effective October 1, 2026), fees for advisory services or paid to an agent don't count as equity.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-prepaid-not-equity] Ask your lender how it treats any fee paid from loan proceeds.

**Questions to ask before you hire**

1. Which deals have you worked on for buyers, at what size and in what industries? Can I speak with those buyers?
2. Who exactly will do the work, and how much of their time will my search get?
3. How will you reach owners, and in whose name? If they send email for you, you remain responsible for complying with email law; the FTC says you can't contract away that responsibility.[^ftc-can-spam-vendor-responsibility] See [outreach rules](/q/outreach-rules).
4. What is the fee, when is it due, and what happens if I find a deal myself or a deal comes from a broker?
5. Do you take fees from sellers or other parties on the same deals?
6. What licenses or registrations do you hold, if any are required for the work you'd do in my state?
7. How do I end the engagement, and do you claim a fee on deals after it ends (a "tail")?

**When it tends to make sense**

When your time is worth more than the fee, when you're buying in an industry where the advisor has real relationships, or when you'd otherwise not do outbound sourcing at all. When it doesn't: when you'd pay mostly for things you can do yourself, like reading listings and sending a buy box to brokers.

**Alternatives**

Do the sourcing yourself, and hire specialists only for the parts that need them: an accountant for the numbers, a lawyer for the documents, a lender for financing. See [who should be on my acquisition team](/q/deal-team) and [advisor costs](/q/advisor-costs).
