**Short answer.** Plan it with the seller, tell everyone close to closing (often on closing day or the next working day), and have the seller there to introduce you. Say plainly what's changing and what isn't, especially pay, jobs and who they report to, and don't promise what you can't keep. Key employees may need to know earlier, with the seller's agreement and under confidentiality.

**Before the announcement:**

- Agree with the seller who says what, and when. Have the seller go first.
- Decide what you can honestly say about jobs, pay, benefits and schedules.
- Prepare answers for the obvious questions: Is my job safe? Will my pay change? Who do I go to now? Why did you buy this business?
- Have payroll and benefits ready, so the first payday under you goes smoothly.

**The message:** who you are and why you bought the business, what stays the same, what you'll do first (mostly listening), and how to reach you. Meet people one-on-one in the first weeks.

**Rules that can apply:**

- **Layoffs.** If you plan any, the federal WARN Act requires 60 days' written notice of a plant closing or mass layoff by a covered employer,[^ecfr-20-cfr-639-4-60-day-notice] generally one with 100 or more employees as the rule counts them.[^ecfr-20-cfr-639-3-employer] In a sale, the seller is responsible for notice up to the sale date and the buyer after it.[^ecfr-20-cfr-639-4-sale-of-business] Some states have their own rules; ask your lawyer.
- **Paperwork for transferred employees.** Decide whether to complete new Forms I-9 for all acquired employees or keep the seller's forms,[^uscis-m274-acquisitions] and collect new Forms W-4 unless an IRS alternative procedure applies.[^irs-pub-15-successor-w4]
