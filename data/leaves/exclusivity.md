**Short answer.** Exclusivity (also called a no-shop) is the period after the letter of intent (LOI) when the seller agrees not to negotiate with other buyers. Ask for long enough to finish diligence, get loan approval and sign the purchase agreement on a realistic timeline, and agree in advance how it can be extended. There's no fixed right length; it depends on what has to happen in your deal.

**Build the length from your own timeline.** List what has to happen before closing and how long each step takes in your deal:

- Financial diligence, and a quality of earnings (QoE) report if you need one. A QoE is an accountant's independent check of whether the earnings are real and repeatable. For initial acquisitions financed with an SBA 7(a) loan (a bank loan partly guaranteed by the U.S. Small Business Administration) with a business purchase price of $3 million or more, SBA SOP 50 10 8.1 (effective October 1, 2026) requires the lender to get one.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-qoe-threshold]
- The lender's process: underwriting, the business valuation, and SBA approval. A lender with delegated SBA authority may finish the valuation and QoE after the SBA loan number is issued, as long as they're under way and done before closing.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-plp-valuation-timing]
- Third-party steps you don't control: landlord consent to the lease, license or permit transfers, consent from key customers or suppliers.
- Drafting and negotiating the purchase agreement.

Ask your lender and advisors how long each step usually takes for them, then add a cushion.

**Terms to agree in the LOI:**

- **Start and end dates**, and whether it ends early if either side walks away.
- **Extension**: automatic if you're still working in good faith, or only by written agreement.
- **Scope**: no soliciting, no responding, no sharing information with other buyers.
- **Notice**: the seller tells you if another buyer approaches.

**The seller's view.** An owner who takes the business off the market loses other buyers. A short period with a clear extension path, and steady visible progress from you, usually builds more trust than asking for the longest period you can get.
