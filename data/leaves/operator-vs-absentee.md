**Short answer.** Running it yourself keeps the manager's salary in your pocket and puts you close to customers, staff and cash, which matters most in the first year. Putting in a general manager frees your time but costs a salary out of the same cash flow that pays the debt and you, adds the risk that the manager leaves or underperforms, and gets closer scrutiny from an SBA lender. If you want a hands-off role eventually, one approach is to run the business first, then hire a manager once you understand it.

### What it does to the numbers

Small business earnings are commonly quoted as seller's discretionary earnings (SDE), which adds the owner's pay back into profit. If you hire a manager, their salary, benefits and payroll taxes come out of SDE before anything is left for debt or for you. Price the deal on earnings after a market-rate manager, not on SDE. See [owner replacement cost](/q/owner-replacement-cost) and [SDE vs EBITDA](/q/sde-vs-ebitda).

That also affects borrowing. For an SBA 7(a) loan, under SOP 50 10 8.1 (effective October 1, 2026), the lender needs historical debt coverage of at least 1.25 to 1 for an initial acquisition.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-dsc-ratio] A manager's salary reduces the cash flow available to meet it, and the lender still checks that your compensation covers your living expenses.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-owner-compensation] See [affordability](/q/affordability).

### What the lender looks at

- **Who runs it daily.** The credit memo describes the principals' involvement in daily onsite management, or how daily operations will be managed if they aren't there daily.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-management-experience] Expect to show the manager's experience, pay and incentives.
- **No fully passive structures.** A business that hands a third-party management company sole discretion over its operations is an ineligible passive business.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-passive-management-agreement]

### Running it yourself

- You learn how the business really works, which helps you spot problems in the seller's numbers and in the team.
- Employees and customers see a committed owner during the change of ownership. See [keeping employees](/q/employee-retention) and [keeping customers](/q/customer-retention).
- You carry the hours. See [hours and lifestyle](/q/hours-lifestyle).

### Putting in a manager

- **Where they come from.** An existing employee who already runs things, or an outside hire. An insider knows the business; an outsider may bring skills the business lacks.
- **Risks.** The manager becomes a key person. If they leave, you're back in the seat. See [key employee risk](/q/key-employee-risk).
- **Controls.** Without daily presence, you need reporting, bank controls and regular reviews. See [financial controls and KPIs](/q/financial-controls-kpis).
- **Incentives.** Pay tied to results the manager controls, and clarity about what they can decide without you.

### Questions to decide it

- Does the business throw off enough to pay a manager, the debt and you?
- Is there someone in the business today who could run it?
- Would you be comfortable taking over if the manager left in year one?
- If you plan to keep another job, see [keeping your day job](/q/keep-day-job). If the business depends on the seller personally, see [owner dependence](/q/owner-dependence).
