**Short answer.** A nondisclosure agreement (NDA), also called a confidentiality agreement, is a contract in which you promise to keep the seller's information confidential and use it only to evaluate the purchase. It's normally the price of seeing financials or the business's name. Read it before signing: some NDAs include terms beyond confidentiality, such as a ban on contacting employees or customers, or a promise not to hire staff, that can last for years.

**What a seller's NDA usually covers**

- **What's confidential.** Usually everything shared, including the fact that the business is for sale.
- **Permitted use.** Only to evaluate the deal.
- **Who you can share with.** Your advisers, lenders and investors, usually on condition that they keep it confidential too.
- **Return or destruction.** What you must do with the information if you walk away.
- **Term.** How long the obligations last.

**Terms to read closely**

- **Non-solicitation.** A promise not to hire the business's employees or approach its customers or suppliers for a period. Check the length and whether it would stop you from competing or working in the industry.
- **No-contact clauses.** A ban on talking to employees, customers, suppliers or the landlord without the seller's consent. Normal before a deal, but you'll need consent later for diligence.
- **Lenders and investors.** Make sure you can share information with the people financing the deal.
- **Remedies.** Clauses about injunctions, damages or legal fees.
- **Who the counterparty is.** The seller, the broker, or both.

**Signing many NDAs**

Keep a log of every NDA you sign, its term and any non-solicitation period. If you search in one industry, overlapping non-solicits can add up.

**Do you need a lawyer?** Some buyers sign a broker's standard NDA after reading it carefully, and some start from templates (for example, openagreements.org). Buyers who hire counsel can look for AI-using law firms with fixed fees that are lean and run by an attorney with big-law experience.
