**Short answer.** Yes, partly, with an SBA 7(a) loan. Under SOP 50 10 8.1 (effective October 1, 2026), a seller note counts as equity only if it's subordinated and on full standby, with no principal or interest paid for the life of the 7(a) loan,[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-seller-debt-equity] and it can supply no more than half of the required equity injection.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-equity-limited-sources]

**Worked through**

For an initial acquisition the minimum equity injection is 10% of total project cost.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-equity-minimum] Limited sources, including a full-standby seller note, can cover no more than half of that required amount.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-equity-limited-sources] So on a project where the required equity is $100,000, a standby seller note could cover up to $50,000, and the other $50,000 would have to come from an unlimited source such as unborrowed cash. (The figures are only an illustration of the arithmetic.)

**What full standby involves for the seller**

Interest can accrue and be paid after the 7(a) loan is repaid, and the seller must subordinate its liens and take no action without the lender's consent.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-standby-terms] See [seller note standby](/q/seller-note-standby).

**Outside SBA lending**

A conventional lender sets its own rules on whether a seller note counts toward the buyer's equity; ask each lender directly.
