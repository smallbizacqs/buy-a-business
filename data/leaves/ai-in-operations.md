**Short answer.** Use AI where the business already spends a lot of staff time on repetitive work with text, documents or data: answering routine inquiries, drafting quotes and emails, summarizing calls, entering data from invoices, scheduling, and pulling reports. Start with one well-defined task, keep a person reviewing the output, measure whether it actually saves time or improves results, and protect customer and employee data. Fix the underlying process and data first; AI on top of a messy process can produce messy results faster.

### Where to look

Walk through a week of the business and note tasks that are frequent, rule-based and text- or data-heavy. Candidates in a service or distribution business:

- **Front office.** Answering common questions, booking appointments, taking after-hours messages, routing leads.
- **Sales.** Drafting quotes and proposals from templates, writing follow-up emails, summarizing customer calls.
- **Back office.** Reading invoices and receipts into the accounting system, matching payments, chasing overdue receivables with draft reminders.
- **Operations.** Scheduling and dispatch suggestions, writing up job notes, turning technician voice notes into reports.
- **Management.** Summarizing financial reports, answering questions about your own data, drafting procedures and training materials.

Some software the business may already use (accounting, CRM, scheduling, phone systems) now includes AI features, which can be easier to try than a new tool.

### How to start

1. **Pick one task** with a clear before-and-after measure: hours spent, response time, error rate, quotes sent.
2. **Document the current process.** If people do it different ways, settle on one first.
3. **Run a short trial** with the people who do the work now. They know the exceptions.
4. **Keep a human in the loop.** Someone reviews anything that goes to a customer, sets a price, commits the business or affects pay.
5. **Measure and decide.** Keep it, change it or drop it based on the results, not on enthusiasm.

### Protect data and the business

- **Know where data goes.** Read the terms of each AI tool for how it stores and uses what you put in. Avoid pasting customer, employee or financial information into tools whose terms you haven't checked.
- **Contracts.** Some customer contracts restrict how you handle their information or require confidentiality. Check before routing their data through a new service.
- **Accuracy.** AI tools can produce confident, wrong answers. Don't let them send pricing, legal or safety information unchecked.
- **Records and access.** Use business accounts the company controls, not employees' personal accounts, so you keep access when people leave.

### Bring the team along

Employees may worry that automation means job cuts. Be clear about the goal, whether that's freeing time for better work, handling growth without overtime, or faster service, and involve them in choosing and testing. The people who do the work will find uses you won't.

### Keep expectations grounded

Results vary with the business, the task and how well the process was defined beforehand. Treat vendor claims of time saved as something to test in your business, not a forecast.
