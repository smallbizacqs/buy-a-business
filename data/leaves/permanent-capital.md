**Short answer.** Permanent capital is money that doesn't have to be returned on a fixed date, so the owner can hold a business indefinitely instead of planning a sale. A traditional private equity fund has a set life, so it buys with an exit in mind; a permanent-capital vehicle (a holding company, a family office, or a fund with no end date) can keep a business for decades and pay investors from its cash flow. For a buyer, the difference shows up in how investors get paid, how much debt makes sense, and what you're promising the seller.

### Fixed-life funds versus buy-and-hold

| | Fixed-life fund | Permanent capital / buy-and-hold |
|---|---|---|
| Investor money | Committed for a set period, then returned | Stays invested with no set end |
| How investors get paid | Mostly from a sale or recapitalization | From distributions of cash flow, sometimes later liquidity options |
| Pressure to sell | Built in by the fund's end date | None by design |
| How debt fits | More leverage can lift returns over a short hold | Less leverage helps a business survive bad years without a forced sale |
| What sellers hear | "We'll grow it and sell it" | "We'll keep it" |

These are tendencies, not rules. Each vehicle's documents set its real terms.

### Where buy-and-hold shows up in small-business buying

- **Holding companies** that buy and keep several businesses. See [should I build a holdco](/q/holdco).
- **Family offices**, which invest a family's own money and can hold indefinitely. A family office with more than $5 million under management that meets the rule's other conditions is an accredited investor.[^ecfr-17-cfr-230-501-family-office] See [family office capital](/q/family-office-capital).
- **Long-hold search funds.** A search fund is a vehicle in which investors back an entrepreneur to find, buy, run and grow one company.[^stanford-gsb-search-fund-definition] Some searchers raise money on the expectation of holding long-term; Stanford's 2026 study added data on long-duration enterprises.[^stanford-gsb-2026-search-fund-study]
- **Self-funded owners**, who by default hold until they choose to sell.

### What changes for you as the buyer

- **Investor returns.** Without a planned sale, investors need another way to get paid: regular distributions, a preferred return paid from cash flow, or a right to sell their stake back after some years. Agree which, in writing, before you take their money. See [investor terms](/q/investor-terms).
- **Debt.** A business you'll keep through recessions can't carry the leverage of a five-year flip. Model a bad year and check you still cover payments. See [stress-testing a deal](/q/stress-test).
- **Your own exit.** Indefinite for investors doesn't mean you must stay forever. Decide how you'd hand off the CEO role or sell your share.
- **The seller pitch.** "We won't sell your company" can matter to a founder. Only say it if your capital and your documents actually allow it.

### Questions to ask a permanent-capital investor

1. Is the capital truly evergreen, or is there a date when your own investors want liquidity?
2. How do you expect to be paid if there's never a sale?
3. Who decides on a sale or recap if circumstances change?
4. How much debt are you comfortable with on a business you'll hold indefinitely?
