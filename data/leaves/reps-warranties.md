**Short answer.** Representations and warranties ("reps") are the seller's written statements of fact about the business in the purchase agreement: the financial statements are accurate, taxes are paid, the seller owns the assets free of liens, there's no undisclosed litigation, and so on. They give you a legal remedy if a statement turns out to be false, and asking for them makes the seller disclose problems before closing. Ask for reps that cover what you relied on to set the price.

**Reps buyers commonly ask for:**

- Authority to sign and sell, and no conflicts with other agreements.
- Title to the assets, free of liens, and that the assets are enough to run the business.
- Financial statements fairly present the business, and no undisclosed liabilities.
- Taxes filed and paid.
- Material contracts listed and in good standing; no notice of termination from key customers or suppliers.
- Compliance with laws; licenses and permits in place.
- Employees and contractors: correct classification and pay, and no pending claims.
- No litigation, threatened or pending.
- Intellectual property, data and systems owned or properly licensed.
- No material change since the last financial statements.

**How sellers narrow them, and what to watch:**

- **Disclosure schedules.** Exceptions the seller lists are carved out of the reps. Read every schedule; it's often where problems appear.
- **Knowledge qualifiers.** "To the seller's knowledge" limits a rep to what the seller knew. Agree whose knowledge counts and whether that includes what they should have known.
- **Materiality.** "In all material respects" filters out small inaccuracies.
- **Survival.** How long after closing you can make a claim.

**Reps and indemnification work together.** A false rep matters only if the agreement says what you can recover and how. See [indemnification](/q/indemnification), [escrow and holdbacks](/q/escrow-holdback) and [representations and warranties insurance](/q/rw-insurance). Your lawyer drafts and negotiates these; the business points (what you relied on) come from you and your diligence.
