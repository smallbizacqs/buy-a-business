**Short answer.** Under SOP 50 10 8.1 (effective October 1, 2026), a Standard 7(a) loan is capped at $5 million,[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-max-loan] and the SBA's guaranty to one business and its affiliates is capped at $3.75 million.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-guaranty-percentage] Bigger deals add other money alongside it: a conventional loan sharing the lender's lien (pari passu), a seller note, more equity, or investors.

**Pari passu financing**

Pari passu means two loans share the same collateral position equally. The SOP doesn't treat a non-SBA loan sharing the lien equally with the 7(a) loan as a prohibited "piggyback" structure, as long as the non-SBA loan's maturity isn't shorter than the 7(a) loan's.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-pari-passu] This lets a lender pair an SBA loan with a conventional loan on a larger purchase.

**Other ways larger deals are financed**

- **A seller note.** Part of the price paid later to the seller. See [seller financing](/q/seller-financing-basics).
- **More equity.** Your cash or investors' cash. The 10% minimum for an initial acquisition still applies to the whole project.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-equity-minimum] See [raising money from investors](/q/raise-from-investors).
- **Conventional or cash-flow lending without an SBA guaranty.** See [conventional vs. SBA](/q/conventional-vs-sba).

**The overall cap still applies**

However it's split, total acquisition debt (including seller debt not on full standby) is capped at the business valuation and must be covered by cash flow.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-total-debt-cap]
