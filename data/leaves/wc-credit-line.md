**Short answer.** Yes, two ways. Under SOP 50 10 8.1 (effective October 1, 2026), the acquisition loan itself can include working capital,[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-coo-working-capital-use] and you can also get a separate revolving line of credit, conventional or SBA-guaranteed, alongside it.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-loc-alongside] The lender must analyze whether the business will have enough working capital for at least the next 12 months,[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-working-capital-adequacy] so it's better to raise this early than to discover a cash squeeze after closing.

### Option 1: working capital inside the term loan

- **Allowed use.** A change-of-ownership loan may include working capital and your out-of-pocket costs such as the valuation and QoE.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-coo-working-capital-use]
- **Ten-year term.** Working capital loans can't run longer than 10 years,[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-maturity-intangibles] and in a blended loan with real estate, working capital gets a 10-year term.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-real-estate-structure]
- **It raises your equity.** The equity injection is measured against total project cost, which includes every cost to become operational except lines of credit and 504 loans.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-equity-project-cost] Financed working capital in the term loan adds to that base; a separate line of credit doesn't.
- **Large amounts need a reason.** If half or more of the loan is working capital, the lender must explain why in its credit memo.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-working-capital-50pct]

Working capital in a term loan is cash you pay back monthly over years whether or not you draw on it. It's a cushion, not a revolver.

### Option 2: a revolving line alongside the loan

You may get a line of credit, conventional or government-guaranteed, to support working capital as part of the acquisition.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-loc-alongside] Things to know:

- **Funding part of the price on the line.** The lender may put the part of the purchase related to working assets (receivables and inventory) on a line of credit.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-working-assets-line] If the line takes the first lien on those assets, between 20% and 50% of the line's day-one availability must be drawn to fund part of the purchase.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-loc-day-one] That means you start with the line partly used.
- **SBA Express lines.** An SBA Express loan is capped at $500,000, and an SBA Express line can't exceed 10 years including any term-out.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-express-loc]
- **Collateral.** The SBA acquisition lender must take a lien on receivables and inventory, though they count for no more than 10% of book value in its collateral test.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-ar-inventory-collateral] A line lender may want a first lien on those same assets, so the lenders have to agree who holds which lien.

### How to size it

1. Start from the business's monthly cash cycle: how long receivables take to collect, how much inventory it carries, when payroll and suppliers are paid, and any seasonal dip.
2. Add the cash the purchase agreement leaves in the business. See [net working capital](/q/what-is-nwc) and [the peg](/q/peg).
3. Ask the lender to show the payment and equity effect of each option.

This page is about borrowing. The working capital the seller delivers at closing is a separate negotiation in the purchase agreement.
