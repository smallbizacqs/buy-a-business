**Short answer.** The main ways out are selling to an outside buyer (an individual, a competitor or other strategic buyer, or a private equity firm), selling to employees or partners, passing the business to family, selling gradually with seller financing, or, as a last resort, closing and selling the assets. Which ones are open to you depends mostly on how well the business runs without you and on how clean its records are. Planning years ahead widens the options.

**The options:**

- **Outside buyer.** An individual buyer (who may use an SBA loan), a company in your industry, or a private equity firm. See [selling to private equity or a strategic buyer](/q/sell-to-pe).
- **Employees or partners.** SBA 7(a) loan (a bank loan partly guaranteed by the U.S. Small Business Administration)s can finance a sale between existing owners or to current employees, under conditions in the SBA rules.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-owner-buyout] SBA's change-of-ownership categories also include employee stock ownership plans (ESOPs) and cooperatives.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-change-types]
- **Family.** Transferring a family business can carry legal effects such as estate and gift tax obligations,[^sba-family-transfer-taxes] so start with a CPA and an estate lawyer.
- **Gradual sale or seller financing.** You're paid over time, which can widen the pool of buyers but leaves you exposed to how the business does.
- **Close and liquidate.** Selling the assets and winding down, if no buyer or successor appears.

SBA's business guide notes that owners have several options for transferring ownership,[^sba-transfer-ownership] and suggests getting a business valuation before marketing the business to buyers.[^sba-sell-sales-agreement]

**What makes a business easier to sell:** earnings that hold up to a quality of earnings review (an accountant's independent check of the earnings), a team that runs it without you, no single customer you can't lose, documented processes, and clean contracts, leases and licenses. These are the same things you checked when you bought it. See [making the business run without you](/q/professionalize-systems) and [how sale proceeds are taxed](/q/exit-taxes).
