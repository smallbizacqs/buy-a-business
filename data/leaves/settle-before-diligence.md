**Short answer.** Settle in the letter of intent (LOI) every term that moves money or decides whether the deal works for you: price and how it's paid, deal structure, the working-capital target, seller financing terms, the seller's role after closing, and the main conditions to closing. Anything left "to be agreed" gets negotiated after you've spent money on diligence and the seller has more leverage.

**Terms worth pinning down:**

- **Price and its parts**: cash at closing, any seller note (amount, interest, term, when payments start), and any amount paid later.
- **Asset or stock purchase.** It changes which liabilities come with the business and how both sides are taxed. See [asset or stock](/q/asset-vs-stock).
- **Working capital.** How the target (the peg) will be measured, even if the exact number is set during diligence. See [the peg](/q/peg).
- **What's included and excluded**: cash, debt, real estate, vehicles, named equipment.
- **The seller's transition**: how long, how many hours, and how they're paid.
- **Conditions**: financing, lease assignment, license transfers, key employee or customer matters.

**If you're using an SBA 7(a) loan,** a few SBA rules shape these terms, so check them before you sign. An SBA 7(a) loan is a bank loan partly guaranteed by the U.S. Small Business Administration (SBA). Under SBA SOP 50 10 8.1 (effective October 1, 2026):

- In an initial acquisition, the seller can't stay on as an officer, owner or employee, but the business may hire the seller as a consultant for up to 24 months in total.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-seller-consultant]
- Seller debt counts as equity only if it is subordinated to the lender and on full standby, meaning no principal or interest payments for the life of the SBA loan.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-seller-debt-equity]
- Seller earnouts are prohibited.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-earnouts]

Your lender confirms how these apply to your deal; SBA rules change, so check the current SOP.
