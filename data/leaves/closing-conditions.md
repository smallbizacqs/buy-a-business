**Short answer.** Closing conditions are the things that must be true or done before you're obliged to close: your loan funds, the landlord consents, key licenses and contracts transfer, the seller's representations are still true, and nothing materially bad has happened. They matter most when signing and closing happen on different days. When everything happens on the same day, as in many small deals, the same items become a checklist that must be complete before anyone signs.

**Conditions buyers commonly ask for:**

- **Financing**: the loan closes and funds. With an SBA 7(a) loan (a bank loan partly guaranteed by the U.S. Small Business Administration), SBA SOP 50 10 8.1 (effective October 1, 2026) says the lender must have its collateral and every condition in its credit memo met, such as the equity injection, standby agreements and appraisals, before or at disbursement.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-conditions-before-disbursement]
- **Lease**: the landlord's consent to assignment, or a new lease, on acceptable terms.
- **Third-party consents**: for key customer, supplier or franchise contracts that can't be assigned without consent.
- **Licenses and permits** needed to operate.
- **Reps still true at closing**, and the seller has done everything it promised to do before closing.
- **No material adverse change**: nothing has happened that seriously harms the business.
- **Key people**: agreements signed with employees you can't run the business without, if you negotiated that.
- **Deliverables**: the seller's lien releases, payoff letters, signed transition and non-compete agreements.

**What happens if a condition fails.** The agreement should say whether you can walk away, extend the closing date, or waive the condition, and what happens to any deposit. See [closing day](/q/closing-day) and [key purchase agreement terms](/q/apa-key-terms).
