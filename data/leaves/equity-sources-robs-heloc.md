**Short answer.** All three can work, with different risks. Savings are the simplest: unborrowed cash counts in full toward an SBA equity injection.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-equity-unlimited-sources] A home equity line of credit (HELOC) can count if you can show you'll repay it from something other than the business's cash flow,[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-equity-unlimited-sources] but you're putting your house behind the deal. A rollover as business start-up (ROBS) uses retirement money without paying tax up front, but the IRS calls these plans questionable,[^irs-robs-definition] and SBA lenders have extra requirements for them. Get a CPA and an ERISA (retirement plan) lawyer involved before you move retirement money.

### Savings

Cash that isn't borrowed, including a gift, can supply all of the required equity on an SBA loan.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-equity-unlimited-sources] For an initial acquisition that's at least 10%.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-equity-minimum] Keep enough outside the deal for living costs and a business emergency. SBA's credit-elsewhere test lets owners keep reasonable reserves for medical, education and retirement needs.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-personal-liquidity]

### Home equity line of credit (HELOC)

A HELOC lets you borrow against your home equity; if you can't repay it, the lender could foreclose on your home.[^cfpb-heloc-definition]

- **Does it count as SBA equity?** Borrowed money counts only as a personal loan to a guarantor that's repaid from a source other than the business's cash flow; your salary from the business doesn't qualify.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-equity-unlimited-sources] For example, a spouse's income or other assets. Ask your lender how it will document this.
- **Your house may already be in the deal.** If the business doesn't fully secure the SBA loan, the lender must take available equity in personal real estate owned by 20% owners and guarantors.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-personal-real-estate] A HELOC used for the down payment and a lien from the SBA lender can both sit on the same home. See [your home as collateral](/q/collateral-home).
- **Rate risk.** HELOCs usually carry adjustable rates.[^cfpb-heloc-definition] Model the payment at higher rates.

### ROBS (rollovers as business start-ups)

**How it works.** The IRS describes ROBS as an arrangement in which business owners use retirement funds to pay for business start-up costs: the individual rolls existing retirement savings into a new plan tax-free, and the plan uses that money to buy the stock of a new C corporation.[^irs-robs-definition] The corporation then has the cash to buy the business.

**What the IRS has said.** The IRS says ROBS plans aren't considered abusive tax avoidance, but are questionable because they may benefit only one person.[^irs-robs-definition] Its compliance project found that, despite some successes, most ROBS businesses it reviewed had failed or were on the road to failure, with high rates of bankruptcy and liens.[^irs-robs-results] Many sponsors didn't understand that the plan is a separate entity with its own requirements.[^irs-robs-separate-plan]

**What SBA lenders require.** Under SOP 50 10 8.1 (effective October 1, 2026), a business owned by a ROBS plan may get an SBA loan if the plan complies with IRS, Treasury and Labor Department rules.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-robs-eligible] Lenders must weigh that a badly run plan can be disqualified, with tax consequences that could affect repayment.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-robs-risk] The plan sponsor must give a full guaranty regardless of ownership, and loan proceeds can't pay plan setup costs.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-robs-requirements]

**The trade-off.** You don't pay tax on a withdrawal up front, but your retirement savings ride on one business, and administration errors can create the very tax you were avoiding.

### How to decide

1. Add up what you need: the equity, closing costs, and reserves after closing.
2. Ask what each source puts at risk: your savings, your house or your retirement.
3. Get a CPA to compare the after-tax cost of each, and an ERISA lawyer to review any ROBS structure and its ongoing obligations.
4. Ask your ERISA lawyer what filings and administration a ROBS plan needs every year, and who will do them.
5. Show the lender your plan early.
