**Short answer.** A multiple is a price for risk and durability. Buyers pay more for earnings that are larger, more predictable, spread across many customers, not dependent on the owner, and growing. They pay less when earnings are small, lumpy, concentrated, tied to the owner or shrinking. Each factor below either raises the chance the earnings continue after you buy, or lowers it.

**The principle**

The IRS's business valuation guidelines tie the discount or capitalization rate (the inverse of a multiple) to the nature of the business, its risk, its industry and the stability of its earnings.[^irs-irm-4-48-4-valuation-factors] They also point appraisers to the economic and industry outlook, the financial condition of the business and its earning capacity.[^irs-irm-4-48-4-valuation-analysis] A lender's quality of earnings review asks the same question from the other side: how sustainable the revenue is, including customer concentration, contract continuity and whether revenue and margins are likely to hold after the sale.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-qoe-revenue-quality]

**What tends to push a multiple up**

- **Size.** Larger earnings can attract more buyers and financing options, and one lost customer or employee matters less.
- **Recurring revenue.** Contracts, subscriptions and service agreements that renew. See [recurring revenue](/q/recurring-revenue).
- **Diversified customers.** No single customer whose loss would hurt badly. See [customer concentration](/q/customer-concentration).
- **A team that runs it.** A manager and staff who handle daily work without the owner. See [owner dependence](/q/owner-dependence).
- **Steady or rising margins** and a clean trend over several years.
- **Clean records.** Books that match tax returns and bank deposits.
- **Transferable assets.** Leases, licenses and contracts that pass to a buyer without trouble.
- **A healthy industry** with steady demand.

**What tends to pull it down**

- Earnings that depend on the owner's relationships, license or labor.
- One or two customers or suppliers carrying the business. See [supplier concentration](/q/supplier-concentration).
- Declining revenue or margins. See [TTM and trends](/q/trends-ttm).
- Large near-term equipment spending. See [capex](/q/capex-equipment).
- Key employees likely to leave. See [key employee risk](/q/key-employee-risk).
- Exposure to technology change, regulation or the economy. See [AI disruption](/q/ai-disruption) and [macro exposure](/q/macro-exposure).
- Messy or unverifiable books.

**How to use this**

When a seller or broker cites a multiple, ask which of these factors justify it. When you make an offer, name the specific factors that lower your price, with evidence. That makes your number a reasoned position rather than an opening bid, and it gives the seller things they could fix. For the multiples themselves, see [what multiple to pay](/q/what-multiple).
