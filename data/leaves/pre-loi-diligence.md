**Short answer.** Enough to make an offer you won't need to walk back: an understanding of how the business makes money, a first look at the financial statements and tax returns, and a rough check that the price can be financed. Full diligence (the detailed verification of everything) comes after the letter of intent (LOI), the usually non-binding offer letter that sets price and terms.

**Why do some checking first**

An LOI price is a promise in practice even when it isn't legally binding. Cutting the price later costs trust; see [retrades](/q/retrade). The more you check before the LOI, the less likely a surprise forces a change.

**What buyers commonly look at before an LOI**

- **Financial statements and tax returns.** If you plan to use an SBA 7(a) loan, the lender will later work from the business's three most recent year-end financials,[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-three-years] the latest interim statement compared with the same period a year earlier,[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-interim-statements] and the seller's numbers verified against IRS records.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-seller-tax-verification] (All under SOP 50 10 8.1, effective October 1, 2026.) Asking for these early tells you whether the deal can be financed as described.
- **A rough coverage check.** Whether the earnings can carry the loan payments at the lender's minimum debt service coverage ratio (DSCR): 1.25:1 for an initial acquisition under the same SOP.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-dsc-ratio] See [DSCR](/q/dscr).
- **The owner's role.** What the owner does each week and who would do it after the sale.
- **Concentration.** How much revenue comes from the largest customers and suppliers.
- **Obvious red flags.** See [early red flags](/q/early-red-flags).

**What usually waits until after the LOI**

A quality of earnings (QoE) review, bank-statement reconciliation, legal review of contracts and leases, and customer and employee conversations. These cost real money and take the seller's time, so they generally follow an agreed price and an exclusivity period.

## What practitioners say
- A buyer on The Operating System Show runs a three-hour first meeting with the owner covering the business's past, present and future, before any LOI.[^c3707cf]
