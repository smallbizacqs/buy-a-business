**Short answer.** For an SBA 7(a) acquisition loan (a bank loan partly guaranteed by the U.S. Small Business Administration), yes: the lender needs a business valuation, done by an independent accredited appraiser and prepared for the lender. For a business purchase price of $350,000 or less, the lender may do its own valuation instead, unless buyer and seller are closely related. If the price is higher than the valuation, the difference has to come from equity.

**The SBA rules.** Under SBA SOP 50 10 8.1 (effective October 1, 2026):

- The valuation must be requested by and prepared for the lender; the lender can't use one prepared for the buyer or the seller.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-valuation-for-lender]
- It must come from a "qualified source": an accredited professional who regularly values businesses, is independent of the loan production function, and has no conflict of interest.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-qualified-source]
- For a business purchase price of $350,000 or less, the lender may perform its own valuation unless there is a close relationship between buyer and seller.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-valuation-350k]
- If the price exceeds the valuation, the difference must be made up by equity.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-valuation-gap-equity]
- All debt in the deal, including seller debt not on full standby, is capped at the valuation.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-total-debt-cap]
- The cost may be passed to the borrower and can count toward the equity injection.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-diligence-costs]

**Why it matters for your offer.** A valuation below your price creates a gap you or the seller must fill: more of your cash, a lower price, or seller debt on full standby. Raise this possibility in the letter of intent, so you aren't renegotiating from scratch. See [when to change the price after the LOI](/q/retrade).

**Without an SBA loan,** no outside valuation is required, but a conventional lender may ask for one.
