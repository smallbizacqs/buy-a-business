**Short answer.** A business broker is hired by the seller to market the business, find buyers and help get a deal closed. In a typical listing the seller pays the broker's fee, often on a successful sale, so the broker works for the seller even while being helpful to you. Ask any broker how they're paid. Treat a broker as the seller's representative: courteous, useful, and not your adviser.

**The broker's role**

- **Engagement.** The seller signs a listing agreement with the broker. On BizBuySell, for example, a listing can be posted only by the owner or by a licensed broker or agent the owner has engaged under an exclusive listing agreement.[^bizbuysell-terms-who-lists]
- **Marketing.** The broker prepares a short anonymous summary (a teaser) and a longer confidential information memorandum (CIM), posts the listing, and sends it to buyers on their list.
- **Screening buyers.** The broker asks buyers to sign a nondisclosure agreement (NDA) and often asks for proof of funds and background before sharing details. See [looking credible to brokers](/q/broker-credibility).
- **Running the process.** Arranging calls and visits, collecting offers, and keeping the deal moving through diligence and closing.

**Whose side they're on**

- The broker's duty and pay run to the seller. Assume anything you tell the broker can reach the seller.
- Brokers do want deals to close, so they can be a useful go-between on timing, documents and practical problems.
- A broker's valuation or "adjusted earnings" figure is part of the marketing. Verify it yourself. See [add-backs](/q/add-backs).

**Buy-side help**

If you want someone working for you, that's a separate role: a buy-side adviser you hire and pay. See [buy-side advisors](/q/buy-side-advisor). You can also ask a broker which license, if any, they hold.
