**Short answer.** A partner brings more capital, skills you lack, someone to share the load and a second view on every deal. The costs are a smaller ownership stake, a business that has to support two owners, and the risk that you disagree on something important with no easy way out. If you partner, settle roles, decision rights, pay and how either of you can leave in writing before you look at deals.

### What a partner adds

- **Money.** Two sets of savings can buy a larger business or leave a bigger reserve.
- **Complementary skills.** For example, one partner strong on operations and people, the other on finance and sales.
- **Coverage.** Someone to split the search work, diligence and the first year's problems with.
- **Judgment.** A partner can stop you from falling in love with the wrong deal.

### What it costs

- **Ownership and pay.** You each own less, and the business must pay two people. For an SBA 7(a) loan, under SOP 50 10 8.1 (effective October 1, 2026), the lender checks that each owner's compensation covers their obligations and living expenses.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-owner-compensation] Two salaries need a bigger business.
- **Two guarantees.** Under the same SOP, every individual owning 20% or more gives an unlimited personal guarantee.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-personal-guaranty] In a 50/50 deal, both partners guarantee the whole loan, not half of it. See [personal guarantees](/q/personal-guarantee).
- **Disagreement.** Partners can split over strategy, effort, money or a change in life plans. Without agreed terms, a split can stall the business.

### What to agree before you search

- **Roles.** Who is chief executive, and who has the final say in which areas? Two co-equal bosses can confuse employees.
- **Equity and effort.** How ownership reflects money put in versus work done, and what happens if one partner stops working in the business.
- **Decisions.** Which decisions need both partners, and how you break a deadlock.
- **Pay.** Salaries, distributions and when either changes. How owners are paid depends on the entity; partners in a partnership take distributions or guaranteed payments rather than W-2 wages.[^irs-paying-yourself-partners] The entity also decides which tax return the business files.[^irs-business-structures] Your CPA can explain the options.
- **Exits.** What happens if one partner wants out, becomes disabled or dies: who can buy the shares, at what price and how it's paid for. These terms belong in an operating or shareholder agreement drafted by a lawyer.
- **Search costs.** How you split expenses before a deal closes, and what happens if you part ways mid-search.

See [entity setup](/q/entity-setup) and, for co-investing with another searcher on one deal, [co-GP and joint ventures](/q/co-gp-jv).

### Going solo

Solo buyers keep full control and ownership and only one salary has to come out of the business. They also carry every decision and every bad day alone. Solo owners can build support another way: a strong second-in-command, an advisory board or a peer group. See [peer groups and boards](/q/peer-groups-board).

### Test before you commit

Work on something real together first: a few deal reviews, a mock LOI, a weekend building a model. Talk about money, family plans and how you each handle stress. If the hard conversations are awkward now, they'll be harder with a loan outstanding.
