**Short answer.** "Cash-free, debt-free" means the seller keeps the business's cash and pays off its debt at closing, and you get the business with a normal level of working capital. The price assumes no debt comes with it. Most of the negotiation is over what counts as "debt": loans clearly do, but other obligations, such as customer deposits, unpaid taxes or accrued bonuses, may also need to be treated as debt or as part of working capital.

**How it works:**

- **Cash**: the seller takes it out before or at closing, except any amount the agreement says stays in, such as cash in registers or a minimum operating balance.
- **Debt**: bank loans, equipment loans, credit lines and similar are paid off from the seller's proceeds at closing, and the lenders release their liens.
- **Working capital**: a normal level stays in the business, measured against the peg. See [the peg](/q/peg).

**Items to define in the agreement** (as debt, as working capital, or excluded): customer deposits and deferred revenue (work you'll owe that customers already paid for), accrued payroll and bonuses, unpaid taxes for periods before closing, equipment leases, unpaid amounts owed to the owner or family, and credit card balances.

**With an SBA 7(a) loan.** Under SBA SOP 50 10 8.1 (effective October 1, 2026), paying off the business's existing debt in an initial acquisition is part of the change-of-ownership transaction, not a separate refinancing, and must meet the change-of-ownership rules.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-debt-payoff] Your lender's closing statement will show each payoff. An SBA 7(a) loan is a bank loan partly guaranteed by the U.S. Small Business Administration (SBA).

## What practitioners say
- Darin Pierson, a CPA, says customer prepayments (deferred revenue) are a debt the buyer inherits in a cash-free, debt-free deal, so they belong in the working-capital adjustment.[^cba4593]
