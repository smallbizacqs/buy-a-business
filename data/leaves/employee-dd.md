**Short answer.** Check who works there and on what terms, whether they're paid correctly, whether workers called contractors should really be employees, and whether the paperwork (payroll tax filings, Forms I-9) is in order. Find out which people the business can't run without. Problems here can become your liabilities, especially in a stock purchase.

**What to ask for:**

- A census: every employee and contractor, role, start date, pay, hours, and whether exempt from overtime.
- Payroll reports and payroll tax filings, reconciled to the financial statements.
- Written agreements: offer letters, employment, commission or bonus plans, non-competes, confidentiality and invention assignments.
- Benefits: health plan, retirement plan, accrued paid time off and how it's paid out.
- Claims: past or pending complaints, wage claims, agency audits.

**Worker classification.** Workers wrongly treated as contractors can leave the business owing back taxes and penalties. The IRS looks at behavioral control, financial control, and the type of relationship to decide whether someone is an employee or a contractor.[^irs-worker-classification] Your CPA or employment lawyer can review the contractors list; state tests can be stricter.

**Forms I-9.** After an acquisition, the employer can treat acquired employees as new hires and complete new Forms I-9 for all of them,[^uscis-m274-acquisitions] or keep the previous owner's forms, in which case it is liable for any errors or omissions on them.[^uscis-m274-acquisitions-option-b] Diligence tells you which option is safer.

**Taxes after closing.** A successor employer gets new Forms W-4 from transferred employees unless an IRS alternative procedure applies.[^irs-pub-15-successor-w4]

**Layoffs.** If you plan any layoffs, the federal WARN Act may require 60 days' written notice before a plant closing or mass layoff.[^ecfr-20-cfr-639-4-60-day-notice] It covers employers with 100 or more employees (counted as the rule defines),[^ecfr-20-cfr-639-3-employer] and in a sale the seller is responsible for notice of closings or mass layoffs up to the sale date, and the buyer after it.[^ecfr-20-cfr-639-4-sale-of-business] States can have their own rules.

**Key people.** Ask who holds the customer relationships, the licenses and the know-how, and whether they know about the sale. Plan with the seller when and how to talk to them. See [telling employees](/q/announce-to-employees).
