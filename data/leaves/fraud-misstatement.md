**Short answer.** Diligence finds two kinds of problems. The first is numbers that are wrong or presented aggressively without being outright fraud: revenue that is overstated or booked in the wrong period, expenses that are missing or moved, debts that aren't on the balance sheet, and personal spending presented as add-backs that the business can't actually drop. The second is deliberate fakes, such as altered bank statements or invented customers. Both are caught the same way: by checking the seller's numbers against records the seller doesn't control.

### The kinds of problems diligence finds

- **Inflated revenue.** Sales recorded twice, recorded before the work is done, or that include one-time or related-party income presented as recurring. Revenue in the internal books that never reached the bank account is the classic warning sign.
- **Understated costs.** Owner or family labor that isn't paid, rent below market because the owner also owns the building, vendors paid personally, or maintenance put off to make the year look better.
- **Hidden debts and obligations.** Unpaid taxes, merchant cash advances, equipment financing, customer deposits for work not yet done, or legal claims. See [hidden liabilities and lawsuits](/q/litigation-liabilities).
- **Personal spending in the books.** Cars, travel, family phones and meals run through the business. Some of this is a fair add-back (a cost that goes away when the owner leaves); some is a real business cost labeled personal to raise earnings. See [add-backs](/q/add-backs).
- **Books that don't agree with each other.** Internal profit and loss statements that differ from the tax returns, sometimes with an explanation that arrives only after you ask.

### How each one gets caught

The common thread is comparing the seller's documents against outside records.

- **Tax transcripts.** In an SBA-financed change of ownership, the lender must verify the seller's financial data against IRS records.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-seller-tax-verification] Lenders must get tax return transcripts and reconcile any discrepancies with the financial data they were given.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-transcript-reconcile] A transcript comes from the IRS, so it shows what was actually filed.
- **Bank statements.** Tying deposits to reported revenue, month by month, is the strongest single test. The method is covered in [proof of cash](/q/proof-of-cash).
- **A quality of earnings report.** A QoE is an accountant's review of whether reported earnings are real and recurring. Under SOP 50 10 8.1 (effective October 1, 2026), the lender must obtain one for SBA 7(a) initial acquisitions and business expansions with a business purchase price of $3 million or more.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-qoe-threshold] It must reconcile the accountant-prepared statements, tax returns, internal statements and IRS transcript data into one normalized earnings figure,[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-qoe-reconcile] include a cash proof that ties bank statements to the income statement and tax returns,[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-qoe-cash-proof] and assess revenue quality, including customer concentration and contract continuity.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-qoe-revenue-quality]
- **Add-backs the lender won't accept.** Cash flow adjustments the lender hasn't analyzed and justified don't count toward the coverage test,[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-unsupported-adjustments] so weak add-backs tend to surface in underwriting even if you missed them.
- **Customer and vendor checks.** Calling top customers, matching invoices to payments, and confirming vendor balances catch invented sales and unrecorded bills. See [customer calls](/q/customer-dd-calls).

### What to do when you find something

Ask the seller to explain it in writing, and decide whether it's an honest mistake, an aggressive presentation, or something worse. A small error can be fixed with a price adjustment. A pattern of numbers that only hold up until you check them says something about everything else you've been told. The choice between repricing and ending the deal is covered in [findings that kill or reprice a deal](/q/deal-killers) and [retrading](/q/retrade). The purchase agreement's representations and indemnity are where you get protection for what diligence didn't catch; see [reps and warranties](/q/reps-warranties).
