**Short answer.** Show the seller your math rather than arguing about the number. Build your price from verified earnings, the cost of replacing the owner, required capital spending and what the loan payments allow, then walk the seller through it. If the gap is about how much risk you take, terms such as seller financing can bridge part of it. If the gap is about the business being worth less than they think, no structure fixes that, and walking away may be the right answer.

**First, find out where the number came from**

Ask, politely, how they arrived at it. Common sources: a broker's estimate, a friend's sale, an industry rule of thumb, what they need for retirement, or earnings that include add-backs you can't verify. Each calls for a different response. A retirement number is a personal need, not a value; an inflated add-back is a fact you can check together.

**Explain your price, item by item**

1. Start from the earnings shown on the tax returns.
2. Show which add-backs you accept and which you don't, and why. See [add-backs](/q/add-backs).
3. Subtract the cost of replacing the owner's work. See [owner replacement cost](/q/owner-replacement-cost).
4. Account for equipment that needs replacing. See [capex](/q/capex-equipment).
5. Show what the cash flow can carry in loan payments. See [how much you can afford](/q/affordability).

Keep the tone factual. "Here's what a bank will lend against" is easier for a seller to hear than "your business isn't worth that."

**The lender is a reality check for both sides**

Under SOP 50 10 8.1 (effective October 1, 2026), SBA describes the business valuation as helping the buyer decide whether the asking price is supported by an independent qualified source.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-valuation-purpose] If the price exceeds that valuation, the difference must come from the buyer's equity,[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-valuation-gap-equity] and any extra limited-source funds used to fill the gap must be on full standby (no payments during the life of the SBA loan).[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-price-above-valuation-standby] All debt, including seller debt not on full standby, is capped at the valuation.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-total-debt-cap] An independent number can move a seller in a way your opinion can't.

**Structures that can close a smaller gap**

- **Seller financing.** The seller gets their price over time and shares the risk. With an SBA loan, a seller note counts toward your equity only if it's subordinated and on full standby.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-seller-debt-equity] See [price vs. terms](/q/price-vs-terms).
- **Performance-linked terms.** Seller earnouts are prohibited in an SBA 7(a) acquisition, though buyer rebates tied to performance are allowed.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-earnouts] See [buyer rebates](/q/buyer-rebate) and, outside SBA deals, [earnouts](/q/earnouts).
- **Excluding assets** the seller values highly, such as real estate or a vehicle, from the deal.

**When to walk**

If the seller won't engage with the numbers, or the gap is larger than any structure can carry, say thank you and leave the door open. Sellers' expectations sometimes change after a listing sits unsold. See [why listings don't sell](/q/why-listings-dont-sell) and [owners who aren't ready](/q/when-owner-not-ready).
