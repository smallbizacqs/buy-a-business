**Short answer.** The useful ones teach you something specific, connect you with people who have actually bought and run businesses, and are honest about what they cost and how they make money. Free options (books, forums, government-funded small business advising, peer conversations) cover a lot of ground. Before paying for a course, coaching or a membership, ask who teaches it, whether they earn money from steering you to deals, lenders or brokers, and what past students say.

### Kinds of programs and communities

- **University courses and student clubs.** Business schools may offer acquisition courses, case studies and clubs. Usually for enrolled students; some publish free material. See [the MBA path](/q/mba-path).
- **Executive education.** Short paid programs aimed at working professionals.
- **Online courses and cohorts.** Recorded lessons, live sessions and templates, from short and cheap to long and expensive.
- **Coaching and mentoring.** One-to-one or small-group help through a search or a deal.
- **Deal-flow and data memberships.** Paid access to listings, owner lists or sourcing tools. See [data and lists](/q/data-and-lists).
- **Conferences.** Academic gatherings, investor events and industry trade shows. Trade shows in your target industry can be as useful as acquisition-focused events.
- **Online communities.** Forums, group chats and social media groups where buyers and owners trade notes. Quality varies, and many cost nothing to join.
- **Peer groups for owners.** Small groups that meet regularly after you own a business. See [peer groups and boards](/q/peer-groups-board).
- **Government-funded advising.** The SBA and its network of partners offer free or low-cost counseling and training locally.[^sba-local-assistance-counseling]

### What to ask before you pay

- **Who teaches it?** Have they bought and run a business themselves? The FTC notes there's no licensing requirement to be a business coach and that scammers often lie about credentials.[^ftc-coaching-scams-credentials]
- **How do they make money?** From your fee alone, or also from referral fees, deal fees or equity in deals they steer you to? A conflict isn't disqualifying, but you should know.
- **What exactly do you get?** Hours of live time, access to people, templates, reviews of your deals. Get it in writing.
- **What do past students say?** Ask for names you can contact, including people who didn't buy a business.
- **What's the refund policy?**
- **What would you learn for free?** Compare against books, public material and conversations with owners.

### Red flags

- **Guaranteed outcomes.** The FTC warns that an offer promising guaranteed income, large returns or a "proven system" is likely a scam.[^ftc-coaching-scams-red-flags]
- **Pressure to decide now.** The FTC notes honest business opportunities don't need high-pressure tactics; an offer good today should be good tomorrow.[^ftc-coaching-scams-pressure]
- **Upsells.** A low-cost entry that leads to ever more expensive tiers.
- **Promises of easy financing or no-money-down deals.** See [little money down](/q/little-money-down).
- **Success stories you can't verify.**

### Getting value from free communities

- Ask specific questions and share what you've learned.
- Treat advice as views, not facts; check rules against official sources.
- Never post a seller's confidential information. See [NDAs](/q/nda).

This guide doesn't recommend or rank programs. Judge each one against the questions above.
