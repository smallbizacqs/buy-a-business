**Short answer.** Under Rule 506(c) you may advertise, but every investor must be accredited and you must take reasonable steps to verify that. Under Rule 506(b) you may not use general solicitation or advertising, but you can include a limited number of non-accredited investors who are financially sophisticated. Both are exemptions under the SEC's Regulation D that let you raise money without registering the offering.

When you sell ownership in a deal (or in a search fund) to investors, you're selling securities, and federal securities law applies.

**Rule 506(b): no advertising**

- No general solicitation or general advertising.[^ecfr-17-cfr-230-502-general-solicitation] The rule's examples include any advertisement, article, notice or other communication published in media or broadcast.[^ecfr-17-cfr-230-502-examples]
- No more than 35 purchasers in any 90-day period, counted as Rule 501(e) provides;[^ecfr-17-cfr-230-506-b-purchasers] accredited investors are excluded from that count.[^ecfr-17-cfr-230-501-accredited-excluded]
- Each non-accredited purchaser must have enough financial and business knowledge, alone or with a representative, to evaluate the investment.[^ecfr-17-cfr-230-506-b-sophistication]
- If you sell to any non-accredited investor, you must give them specified information a reasonable time before the sale.[^ecfr-17-cfr-230-502-information]

**Rule 506(c): advertising allowed**

- General solicitation is permitted.[^ecfr-17-cfr-230-502-general-solicitation]
- All purchasers must be accredited investors.[^ecfr-17-cfr-230-506-c-accredited]
- You must take reasonable steps to verify each purchaser's accredited status, not just accept a checkbox.[^ecfr-17-cfr-230-506-c-verification]

**Who is an accredited investor (individuals)**

Among other tests: net worth over $1,000,000 alone or with a spouse or spousal equivalent,[^ecfr-17-cfr-230-501-accredited-net-worth] not counting the primary residence as an asset,[^ecfr-17-cfr-230-501-primary-residence] or income over $200,000 (or $300,000 joint) in each of the last two years with a reasonable expectation of the same this year.[^ecfr-17-cfr-230-501-accredited-income]

**Both rules**

- File Form D with the SEC within 15 calendar days after the first sale.[^ecfr-17-cfr-230-503-form-d]
- Neither is available if the issuer or certain people involved are disqualified "bad actors."[^ecfr-17-cfr-230-506-bad-actor]
- Check your state's requirements too.

**"Advertising" can be broader than it sounds.** A public post, podcast episode or web page about your raise may fall within those examples, which matters if you planned to rely on 506(b). Decide which rule you're using before you talk about the raise in public, and ask counsel where the line falls.

**Do you need a lawyer?** Securities rules are technical, so check the current text of each rule before relying on it. Some buyers start from templates (for example, openagreements.org); buyers who hire counsel can look for AI-using law firms with fixed fees that are lean and run by an attorney with big-law experience.
