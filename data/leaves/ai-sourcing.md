**Short answer.** Yes, for much of the groundwork: AI tools can help build target lists from public information, sort businesses against your criteria, summarize websites and reviews, and draft personalized first messages. They can't tell you what a private business earns or whether the owner will sell, and they make confident mistakes. Verify what they produce, approve every message yourself, and remember the outreach rules apply to AI-written messages and AI voices too.

**Where AI helps**

- **Building the list.** Searching the web and public directories for businesses in an industry and area, and pulling names, addresses, websites and years in business into a table. Start from a market count, such as the Census Bureau's County Business Patterns figures on establishments by industry, size and county,[^census-cbp-establishments] so you know roughly how complete your list is.
- **Enriching records.** Finding the owner's name on the business's website or public filings, noting services offered, and flagging signs of size or age (number of locations, staff pages, founding year).
- **Screening against your buy box.** Scoring each business on your criteria and explaining why, so you can review the edge cases. See [define your buy box](/q/define-buy-box).
- **Reading listings.** Summarizing a listing or teaser, pulling out the stated earnings and asking price, and listing questions to ask the broker.
- **Drafting outreach.** A first draft of an email or letter that mentions something specific to each business, for you to edit.

**Where it falls short**

- **Private financials.** Revenue and profit estimates for private businesses are guesses. Treat them as a sorting signal, never as a fact.
- **Wrong or stale facts.** AI tools can invent details, mix up businesses with similar names, or rely on old information. Spot-check a sample of every list against the source.
- **Owner intent.** Whether an owner would sell comes from a conversation, not data.
- **Tone.** Messages that read as machine-written can cost you credibility with owners who care who buys their business.

**Rules that still apply**

- **Email.** An AI-drafted email has to meet the same requirements as any other; for example, its subject line must accurately reflect the content.[^ftc-can-spam-subject-line] If a tool or service sends on your behalf, you can't contract away responsibility for compliance.[^ftc-can-spam-vendor-responsibility]
- **Voice.** The FCC says AI-generated voice calls are illegal unless the person has agreed to receive them or the caller is exempt.[^fcc-ai-voice-calls] Don't use AI voice agents for cold calls to owners without legal advice.
- **Data use.** Follow each website's and data source's terms on automated collection.

See [outreach rules](/q/outreach-rules).

**Protecting confidential information**

Once you sign an NDA (nondisclosure agreement) and receive a seller's financials, check what the NDA lets you share and whether the AI tool's terms let it keep or train on what you upload. Using AI on diligence materials is covered in [AI-assisted diligence](/q/ai-assisted-diligence).

**A practical workflow**

1. Write your criteria in plain language.
2. Have the tool build and score a first list from public sources, with a source link for each record.
3. Review the top of the list yourself and spot-check the rest.
4. Draft messages with AI, then rewrite each in your own voice and send it yourself.
5. Log results and refine the criteria and the prompts.

See also [data sources and lists](/q/data-and-lists) and [interns and sourcing teams](/q/interns-and-teams).
