**Short answer.** Only if you want the seller note to count toward your equity injection. Under SOP 50 10 8.1 (effective October 1, 2026), a seller note counts as equity only if it's subordinated to the lender and on full standby, meaning no principal or interest is paid for the entire term of the 7(a) loan.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-seller-debt-equity] A note that isn't used as equity can be paid during the loan, but then it counts as debt in the lender's coverage and debt-cap tests.

**What "full standby" means**

- No payments of principal or interest for the life of the SBA loan.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-seller-debt-equity]
- Interest may accrue, be added to the note and be paid off after the 7(a) loan is repaid in full.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-standby-terms]
- The seller subordinates any lien to the lender's and takes no action against the business or the collateral without the lender's consent.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-standby-terms]

**How much of the equity it can cover**

A full-standby seller note is a limited equity source: together with other limited sources it can supply no more than half of the required equity.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-equity-limited-sources] See [equity injection](/q/equity-injection).

**A seller note that isn't on full standby**

- It counts toward the cap on total acquisition debt (the business valuation) and must be covered by cash flow.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-total-debt-cap]
- If it's interest-only, the lender counts it as if it amortized over no more than 10 years when testing coverage.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-interest-only-debt]
- Seller debt from the acquisition can be refinanced once it has been in place and current for 36 months.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-seller-note-refinance]

**Why it matters in negotiation**

Full standby can mean the seller receives nothing on the note for the full SBA term, which is a lot to ask. Decide early whether you need the note as equity (standby) or as ordinary subordinated debt (paid), and run the coverage both ways. The note's exact terms are for your lawyer and the lender to settle.
