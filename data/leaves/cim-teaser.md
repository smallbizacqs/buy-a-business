**Short answer.** A teaser is a one- or two-page anonymous summary of a business for sale; a confidential information memorandum (CIM) is the longer marketing document you get after signing a nondisclosure agreement (NDA). Both are written to sell the business. Read them for what they claim, then list what they leave out or can't prove, and turn that list into your questions and document requests.

**Reading a teaser**

A teaser usually gives the industry, region, revenue, an earnings figure (SDE or EBITDA), asking price and reason for sale. Use it only to decide whether to sign the NDA: does the size, location and type fit your [buy box](/q/define-buy-box)?

**Reading a CIM**

Read it in this order:

1. **The numbers.** Revenue and earnings by year, and how the "adjusted" earnings were built from the profit and loss statement (P&L). Every add-back should be listed. See [add-backs](/q/add-backs).
2. **Customers.** Concentration, contracts, and how customers are won.
3. **People.** Who does what, including the owner. See [owner dependence](/q/owner-dependence).
4. **Operations.** Location, lease, equipment, suppliers, licenses.
5. **The story.** Why the owner is selling and the "growth opportunities" (treat these as ideas, not value you're paying for).

**What's commonly missing**

- Tax returns, and a reconciliation between the tax returns and the P&L.
- Monthly results, which show seasonality and recent trends.
- The owner's actual weekly role and pay.
- Customer-level revenue, churn and contract terms.
- Capital spending needs and the condition of equipment.
- Debts, liens, lawsuits and lease terms.

**Lining your requests up with the lender's**

If you plan to use an SBA 7(a) loan, under SOP 50 10 8.1 (effective October 1, 2026) the lender will work from the three most recent year-end financial statements,[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-three-years] the latest interim statement compared with the same period a year earlier,[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-interim-statements] and the seller's numbers verified against IRS records.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-seller-tax-verification] Asking for the same documents early tells you whether the CIM's numbers will hold up.
