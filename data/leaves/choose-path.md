**Short answer.** The paths differ in who pays for the search, who puts up the purchase equity, how much of the business you end up owning, and who you answer to. Self-funded buyers pay their own way and keep most of the ownership and risk; search fund searchers are paid by investors and earn a smaller stake over time; independent sponsors find a deal first and raise money for that one deal; a holding company buys several businesses under one owner group. Your savings, your appetite for risk and personal guarantees, and whether you want partners decide the fit.

**The four paths, briefly**

- **Self-funded search.** You cover your own living costs and search expenses, then buy with your own equity, usually plus a bank loan and often a seller note. See [self-funded search](/q/self-funded-search).
- **Traditional (investor-backed) search fund.** Investors pay a salary and search costs, then get the first chance to fund the purchase. See [investor-backed search fund](/q/traditional-search-fund).
- **Independent sponsor.** You search on your own dime and, once you have a deal under letter of intent, raise equity for that deal alone. See [independent sponsors](/q/independent-sponsor).
- **Holding company.** A company set up to own several businesses over time. See [holdco](/q/holdco).

**Questions that sort the options**

- **Money.** How much can you put in without risking your household? With an SBA 7(a) loan, an initial acquisition needs at least 10% equity, under SOP 50 10 8.1 (effective October 1, 2026).[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-equity-minimum]
- **Risk.** Are you willing to sign a personal guarantee? Under the same SOP, every individual owning 20% or more of the borrower gives an unlimited one.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-personal-guaranty]
- **Control and ownership.** Do you want to own most of the business, or trade ownership for investor money and support?
- **Income during the search.** Can you go months without a salary? See [search budget](/q/search-budget).
- **Raising money.** Selling ownership to investors means complying with securities rules; for example, outside Rule 506(c) you can't advertise the raise.[^ecfr-17-cfr-230-502-general-solicitation] See [Rule 506(b) or 506(c)](/q/506b-506c-securities).
- **Size.** Investor-backed paths tend to target larger businesses than a buyer can afford alone.

Paths can mix: a self-funded buyer may bring in a few investors for part of the equity.
