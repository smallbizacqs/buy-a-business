**Short answer.** They often differ for ordinary reasons: different accounting methods, depreciation rules, timing, and owner expenses. Each difference should be explainable line by line. For an SBA loan, the tax returns anchor the numbers: the lender checks the seller's financial data against IRS records and must reconcile any gap. A profit on the P&L that the tax returns don't support generally can't be counted.

**Normal reasons the two differ**

- **Cash vs. accrual.** The tax return may be on a cash basis (income when received) while the internal books use accrual (income when earned), or the other way around.
- **Depreciation.** Depreciation spreads the cost of equipment and property over the years it's used,[^irs-pub-946-depreciation] and tax rules can allow faster write-offs than the books use.
- **Year-end entries.** The accountant's adjustments for inventory, accruals or prepaid expenses may never make it back into the bookkeeping system.
- **Owner and personal items.** Expenses classified one way for taxes and another internally.
- **Timing.** A P&L for the trailing twelve months won't line up with a calendar-year return.

**Reasons that should worry you**

- Revenue on the P&L that's higher than on the return, with no timing or method explanation.
- Different numbers for the same year in different versions of the P&L.
- Returns not filed, or filed late, for recent years.
- An explanation that boils down to "we didn't report everything." See [unreported cash income](/q/cash-business).

**What the SBA lender relies on**

Under SOP 50 10 8.1 (effective October 1, 2026), SBA's tax verification checks whether the financial statements a lender receives agree with the tax returns filed with the IRS.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-tax-verification-purpose] In a change of ownership, the lender must verify the seller's financial data,[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-seller-tax-verification] obtain tax transcripts and reconcile any discrepancies,[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-transcript-reconcile] and check the numbers behind the business valuation against the seller's IRS transcripts.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-valuation-verified]

Where a quality of earnings (QoE) report is required, it must reconcile the accountant-prepared statements, tax returns, internal statements and IRS transcripts into one normalized earnings figure,[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-qoe-reconcile] document cash-versus-accrual differences,[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-qoe-revenue-quality] and include a cash proof tying bank statements to the income statement and tax returns.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-qoe-cash-proof]

**What a buyer should do**

1. Ask for three years of returns and the matching year-end P&Ls and balance sheets.
2. Build a simple bridge for each year: P&L net income, then each difference, ending at taxable income on the return.
3. Ask the seller's accountant to explain any item you can't bridge.
4. Tie revenue to bank deposits for a few months. See [proof of cash](/q/proof-of-cash).

Your own accountant can build or check the bridge; see [what your accountant does](/q/cpa-role).
