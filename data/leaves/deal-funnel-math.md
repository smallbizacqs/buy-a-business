**Short answer.** Usually more than one. A buyer screens out many businesses for each offer made, not every offer is accepted, and an accepted offer can still fall apart in diligence or financing. There's no official ratio; the useful exercise is to track your own funnel so you can see where deals drop out and how much activity you need.

**The stages of a typical funnel**

1. **Businesses reviewed.** Listings, broker emails, owners you contacted.
2. **Conversations.** First calls or meetings with an owner or broker.
3. **Serious looks.** You've signed a nondisclosure agreement (NDA) and seen financials.
4. **Letters of intent (LOIs) submitted.** Offers stating price and terms.
5. **LOIs signed.** The seller accepts and grants exclusivity.
6. **Closed.** After diligence, financing and the purchase agreement.

**Where deals drop out**

- At screening: wrong size, industry or location, or earnings that don't support the price.
- At the offer: price or terms the seller won't accept, or another buyer.
- After a signed LOI: diligence findings, a lender decline, a lease or license that won't transfer, or a seller who changes their mind.

**Using your own numbers**

Count each stage weekly. After a few months you'll know your own conversion rates, which tell you how many conversations you need for each LOI and whether the bottleneck is sourcing, screening or closing. Plan your [search budget](/q/search-budget) and [timeline](/q/search-timeline) with the possibility that a signed deal still doesn't close.
