**Short answer.** Some insurers sell personal guarantee insurance: a policy that reimburses part of what you lose if a lender calls your guarantee after the business fails. It doesn't replace or limit the guarantee itself; the lender can still pursue you, and the policy pays you back under its own terms, up to its limits. Whether it's worth the premium depends on the coverage, the exclusions and how much risk you'd otherwise carry. Get several quotes and read the policy wording, not just the summary.

### What it is and isn't

- **It isn't a change to the loan.** For an SBA 7(a) loan, under SOP 50 10 8.1 (effective October 1, 2026), every individual owning 20% or more of the borrower gives an unlimited full guarantee.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-personal-guaranty] An insurance policy you buy doesn't change that obligation to the lender. See [personal guarantees](/q/personal-guarantee).
- **It isn't the insurance the lender requires.** When a 7(a) loan isn't fully secured, the SOP requires life insurance equal to the collateral shortfall on the principal of a business that depends on one owner,[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-life-insurance-shortfall] and loans over $50,000 need hazard insurance on the collateral.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-hazard-insurance] Those protect the lender. Guarantee insurance, if you buy it, protects you.

### Questions to ask before buying a policy

- **What triggers a payout?** Policies differ. Is it the lender's formal demand on your guarantee, a court judgment, or something else? What proof does the insurer need?
- **How much does it pay?** As a share of your loss, with what maximum, and whether the limit falls as the loan is paid down.
- **When does cover start?** Ask about any waiting period after the policy starts during which a default isn't covered.
- **What's excluded?** Fraud, problems that existed before the policy, defaults you cause on purpose, personal assets you pledged separately, other loans.
- **What does it cost over time?** The premium each year, whether it changes, and the total over the years you expect to hold it.
- **Can you cancel it, and what happens on a refinance or sale?**
- **Who is the insurer?** Its financial strength and its record of paying claims. Ask for references from policyholders who made claims.
- **Does the lender need to know or consent?** Some policies need the lender's cooperation to process a claim.

### Alternatives and complements

- **Smaller debt.** Putting in more equity, or financing more with a seller note, shrinks what you guarantee to the bank. See [capital stack](/q/capital-stack).
- **A bigger cushion.** A deal sized well above the minimum coverage ratio is less likely to default. See [stress testing](/q/stress-test).
- **Asset planning.** How your state treats different personal assets is a question for a lawyer. See [asset protection](/q/asset-protection).
- **Household agreement.** Talk through the worst case with your spouse or family. See [talking it through with family](/q/spouse-family).

### A caution

A policy that covers part of a loss can make a risky deal feel safer than it is. Decide whether the deal makes sense without the insurance first. See [what happens when a deal fails](/q/failure-stories).
