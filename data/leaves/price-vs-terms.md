**Short answer.** The headline price is only one part of what a deal is worth to each side. How much is paid at closing, how much later, at what interest, under what conditions, and who carries which risks all change the real value. A seller who wants a high number may accept it paid partly over time; a buyer can sometimes agree to a higher price in exchange for terms that cost less or carry less risk. Compare offers by what each side actually receives and risks, not by the headline.

**The levers**

- **Cash at closing** vs. paid later. See [cash at close](/q/cash-at-close).
- **Seller note**: amount, interest rate, length, payment start date, and whether payments can be paused or reduced if the business struggles. See [seller note terms](/q/seller-note-terms).
- **Performance-linked pieces**: a rebate to the buyer (allowed with SBA financing) or an earnout to the seller (not allowed with SBA financing).[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-earnouts]
- **Working capital and what's included**: a lower working capital target or more equipment included is value even if the price doesn't change.
- **Seller's transition help and non-compete.**
- **Holdbacks and escrow** that protect you after closing.
- **Timing**: a faster close, or a closing date that suits the seller's tax year.

**What each side may care about**

Sellers may care about the headline number, certainty of closing, cash at closing, taxes, and their employees. Buyers care about cash needed, debt payments, and protection if something turns out wrong. Ask the seller directly what matters most; the answer tells you which levers are cheap for you and valuable to them.

**How to compare offers**

Put each version in a simple model with payments by year. A higher price with a long, low-interest seller note can cost you less per year than a lower price paid in cash, and can be worth less to the seller once you account for time and risk. Look at:

- Cash you need at closing.
- Total debt payments per year, and coverage in a bad year. See [stress-testing a deal](/q/stress-test).
- Total paid over time.
- What happens if the business underperforms.

**With an SBA 7(a) loan**

Under SOP 50 10 8.1 (effective October 1, 2026):

- A seller note counts toward your equity only if it's subordinated and on full standby, with no principal or interest paid for the life of the loan.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-seller-debt-equity] Standby debt may accrue interest that's paid after the 7(a) loan is repaid.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-standby-terms]
- Interest-only seller debt not on full standby is counted for coverage as if it amortized over no more than 10 years.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-interest-only-debt]
- All debt, including seller debt not on full standby, is capped at the business valuation.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-total-debt-cap] A price above the valuation must be covered by your equity,[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-valuation-gap-equity] so "your price, my terms" has a ceiling.
- Acquisition seller debt can be refinanced with SBA financing after it's been in place and current for 36 months.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-seller-note-refinance]

**Watch out for**

- A higher price that only works if nothing goes wrong.
- Terms that look generous but that your lender won't accept.
- Agreeing to a price now and hoping to cut it in diligence. See [retrades](/q/retrade).
