**Short answer.** An MBA can help: it gives you finance and management training, classmates and alumni who may invest or advise, and, at some schools, courses and clubs focused on buying businesses. It isn't required to buy a business or to get an SBA loan, and the cost in tuition and lost salary reduces the money you'll have for the purchase. It matters most if you plan to raise money from investors for a search fund; it matters least for a small self-funded deal.

### What the degree adds

- **Skills.** Accounting, valuation, negotiation and managing people, all used in a search and in running a company.
- **Network.** Classmates, alumni and faculty who can introduce you to investors, lenders, sellers and advisors.
- **Structure.** Some programs offer courses on acquisitions, case studies and student groups where searchers compare notes.
- **Credibility with investors.** Investors in investor-backed search funds look at a searcher's background; a business degree is one signal among several. See [investor-backed search funds](/q/traditional-search-fund) and [what investors want](/q/what-investors-want).

### What it doesn't add

- **Lender approval.** For an SBA 7(a) loan, under SOP 50 10 8.1 (effective October 1, 2026), the credit memo describes the applicant's industry experience and management team and who will run daily operations.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-management-experience] A degree supports that story; it doesn't replace management experience.
- **Seller trust.** A seller is likely to ask how you'll treat their employees and customers; where you studied matters less in that conversation. See [earning an owner's trust](/q/earn-trust).
- **Operating know-how.** Classes don't teach you to schedule a crew or calm an upset customer. See [owner-operator skills](/q/operator-skills).

### The cost side

Tuition, any loans, and two years of lost salary all reduce your savings for the equity and the search. Student loan payments also count in the lender's look at your personal obligations. Compare that with buying sooner using savings you already have. See [readiness](/q/readiness).

### Timing choices

- **Search during the MBA.** You can learn and build relationships while enrolled, though you'll have less time and money.
- **Search right after.** In an investor-backed search fund, investors pay a salary during the search, which can suit a recent graduate.
- **Work first, buy later.** A few years of operating or management work can make you a stronger buyer and borrower.
- **Skip it.** You can learn through reading, peers and advisors instead. See [programs and communities](/q/eta-programs-communities).

### Questions to ask yourself

- Which path am I most likely to take: self-funded, investor-backed or with partners? See [choosing your path](/q/choose-path).
- Would I still want to buy a business if I didn't do the MBA?
- What specifically would the degree give me that I can't get another way?
