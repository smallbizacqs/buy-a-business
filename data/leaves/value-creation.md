**Short answer.** A business's value is roughly its earnings times a multiple, less debt, so you raise it in three ways: grow the earnings, make those earnings more dependable (which buyers reward with a higher multiple), and pay down debt. In practice that means growing profitable revenue, improving pricing and margins, reducing dependence on you and on any one customer, building a management team and clean financial records, and keeping enough cash in the business to fund it.

### How value is measured

Buyers of small businesses value them as a multiple of earnings, using SDE (seller's discretionary earnings: profit plus the owner's pay and certain other add-backs) for smaller businesses or EBITDA (earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation and amortization) for larger ones. See [SDE vs EBITDA](/q/sde-vs-ebitda) and [what multiple to pay](/q/what-multiple). Your equity is that value minus the debt you owe. So the levers are:

1. **Earnings.** Grow profit.
2. **Multiple.** Make the earnings less risky and easier for a buyer to take over.
3. **Debt.** Pay it down from cash flow.

### Lever 1: grow earnings

- **Pricing.** Can be a quick lever, if prices are below value. See [raising prices](/q/raise-prices).
- **Profitable growth.** Sell more to existing customers, add services, fix lead follow-up, then add channels. See [growing sales](/q/sales-marketing-growth).
- **Margins.** Job costing, purchasing, reducing rework and callbacks, scheduling crews more efficiently.
- **Overhead.** Cut costs that don't support revenue, without starving the business of what it needs.
- **Add-ons.** Buying a smaller competitor or a complementary business. See [add-on acquisitions](/q/add-on-acquisitions).

### Lever 2: make earnings more dependable

Buyers pay more for earnings they believe will continue. Things buyers look for:

- **Less owner dependence.** A management team that runs daily operations. See [making the business run without you](/q/professionalize-systems).
- **Recurring revenue.** Service agreements, contracts, subscriptions or repeat customers.
- **Customer spread.** No single customer whose loss would sink the business. See [customer concentration](/q/customer-concentration).
- **Clean, timely financial statements** that hold up to a quality of earnings review. See [reporting and financial controls](/q/financial-controls-kpis).
- **Documented processes, transferable licenses, and clean contracts and leases.**
- **Size.** Larger businesses can attract a wider pool of buyers, including private equity. See [multiple arbitrage](/q/multiple-arbitrage) for how far this goes.

### Lever 3: reduce debt

Paying down acquisition debt increases your equity directly. Weigh it against reinvesting the cash in growth, and against the terms of your loans. See [refinancing or recapitalizing](/q/refinance-recap).

### Build a plan

1. Write down the current earnings, the multiple a buyer might pay and why, and your debt.
2. List the few changes that would most increase earnings or reduce risk over three to five years.
3. Turn each into quarterly priorities with an owner and a number to track.
4. Review the plan at least yearly, and get an outside view of value from time to time.

### Common mistakes

- Chasing revenue at low margins.
- Cutting costs that customers or employees notice, which can reduce earnings later.
- Ignoring owner dependence until it's time to sell.
- Messy financial records that a buyer's diligence will discount.
