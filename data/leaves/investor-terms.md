**Short answer.** The terms you'll meet in a small-business buyout put investors' money back first, plus a preferred return (a minimum annual return that may accrue until paid), before the buyer or sponsor shares in profits through a "carry" or "promote." How much ownership you keep, what return hurdle investors get, and what fees you can charge are negotiated deal by deal; no rule sets them. What you can actually pay investors is also limited by your lender, especially on an SBA loan.

### The building blocks

- **Equity split.** Who owns what at closing, based on who contributed cash. Your own cash can buy ownership on the same terms as investors' cash.
- **Preferred equity or preferred return.** Investors are paid a set return, for example a percentage per year on their money, before common owners share in profits. It can be paid in cash as earned, or accrue and be paid on a sale or refinancing.
- **Return of capital.** Investors get their original investment back before profits are split.
- **Carry or promote.** After capital and preferred return, the sponsor gets a larger share of profits than its cash contribution alone would earn. The promote can step up as investor returns pass higher hurdles.
- **Fees.** Closing fees and ongoing management fees to the sponsor, paid by the company.
- **Governance.** Board seats, information rights, and investor approval for big decisions: selling the company, taking on debt, changing the CEO, paying the sponsor more.

### A "waterfall," in words

A waterfall is the order in which cash goes out. A simple version: (1) return investors' capital, (2) pay their accrued preferred return, (3) split what's left between investors and the sponsor by the agreed percentages, which may shift in the sponsor's favor once investors hit a higher return. Ask for a spreadsheet that runs your actual deal through the waterfall at several exit values so everyone sees the same numbers.

### How the paths differ

- **Traditional search funds.** Investors fund the search and the acquisition, and the searcher earns equity in tranches. See [searcher equity](/q/searcher-economics).
- **Independent sponsors.** Promote, fees and co-investment are negotiated with each deal's investors. See [the independent sponsor model](/q/independent-sponsor).
- **Self-funded buyers with a few investors.** Can be simpler: common equity pro rata, or investors in preferred units with a fixed return and a share of upside.

### What lenders allow

Under SOP 50 10 8.1 (effective October 1, 2026), if investor money is used to meet the SBA equity injection, investors can receive only distributions covering their tax on the business's income until the 7(a) loan is paid off.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-investor-distributions] Investor money beyond the injection can receive normal distributions, subject to the lender's agreements.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-additional-equity-distributions] A passive minority investment counts as equity only if there's no agreement to repay it or make distributions to recover it before the SBA guaranty is released.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-minority-investor] A cash preferred return can collide with these rules. Investors owning 20% or more must also personally guarantee the loan.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-personal-guaranty] Conventional lenders set their own limits through covenants.

### Questions to settle before you pitch

1. Is the preferred return paid in cash or accrued, and is it compounding?
2. What happens to the preferred return if the lender blocks distributions?
3. What fees will you take, and when?
4. Which decisions need investor approval?
5. What happens if you leave or are removed as CEO?
6. How and when can investors get liquidity?

If you sell to anyone who isn't an accredited investor under Rule 506(b), you must give them specified information a reasonable time before the sale.[^ecfr-17-cfr-230-502-information] See [raising from investors](/q/raise-from-investors) and [what investors look for](/q/what-investors-want).
