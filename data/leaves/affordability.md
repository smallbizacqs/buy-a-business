**Short answer.** Work backward from the cash flow. Take the earnings a lender will accept, divide by the required coverage ratio to get the most you can spend on loan payments each year, turn that into a loan amount using the term and rate, then add your equity and any seller financing. That total is roughly the most the deal can carry. Then check that what's left after loan payments still pays you a living and leaves a cushion.

**Step by step (SBA 7(a) loan)**

Under SOP 50 10 8.1 (effective October 1, 2026):

1. **Start with the earnings the lender uses.** Debt coverage is EBITDA (earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation and amortization) divided by all debt payments after the deal,[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-dsc-definition] tested on the last fiscal year or the average of the last two.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-dsc-ratio] Your salary has to be covered too: the lender checks that your pay supports your obligations and living expenses.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-owner-compensation] If the seller's figures include the owner's pay, deduct a market salary for you or your manager first.
2. **Divide by the coverage ratio.** For an initial acquisition, coverage must be at least 1.25:1.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-dsc-ratio] Earnings divided by 1.25 is the most you can pay in total debt service each year.
3. **Subtract other debt payments.** Seller notes that aren't on full standby count, and interest-only seller debt is treated as if it amortized over no more than 10 years.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-interest-only-debt]
4. **Convert payments to a loan amount.** A 7(a) acquisition loan amortizes over no more than 10 years with no balloon, except for a special purpose property;[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-change-of-ownership-term] only a real estate portion may amortize longer, up to 25 years.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-real-estate-structure] Use a loan calculator with the rate your lender quotes. 7(a) loans can be variable, set as a base rate plus a fixed spread,[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-variable-rate] so test a higher rate too.
5. **Add equity and seller financing.** The minimum equity for an initial acquisition is 10%,[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-equity-minimum] measured on total project cost, which includes closing costs and working capital, not just the price.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-equity-project-cost]

**An illustration**

Suppose a business shows $500,000 of earnings after paying a manager. Divided by 1.25, that allows $400,000 a year of total debt payments. If a seller note will cost $50,000 a year, $350,000 is left for the bank loan; plug that into a 10-year amortization at your quoted rate to find the loan size. The numbers are hypothetical; the method is the point.

**Limits that can bind first**

- **The valuation.** All debt, including seller debt not on full standby, is capped at the independent business valuation.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-total-debt-cap]
- **Your cash.** If the deal falls short on coverage, more equity can shrink the loan and its payments.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-extra-equity-dsc]
- **Your life.** Leave room for capital spending, a bad quarter, and your own pay.

The ceiling is not a target. A deal priced at the ceiling has no room for anything going wrong; see [stress-testing a deal](/q/stress-test). For the ratio itself, see [DSCR](/q/dscr); for take-home pay, see [what owners earn](/q/owner-take-home).
