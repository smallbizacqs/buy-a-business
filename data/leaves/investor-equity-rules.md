**Short answer.** Under SOP 50 10 8.1 (effective October 1, 2026), anyone, person or entity, who owns 20% or more of the borrower must give an unlimited personal guaranty, and the lender can require guaranties from smaller owners too.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-personal-guaranty][^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-entity-guaranty][^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-other-guarantors] Every owner, however small, must pass SBA's citizenship test.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-citizenship-owners] Investor money counts toward your equity injection, but a passive minority investment can cover only part of it and its returns are restricted until the loan is repaid.

### Who must guarantee

- **20% owners.** Every individual with direct or indirect ownership of 20% or more gives an unlimited full guaranty,[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-personal-guaranty] and so does every entity at 20% or more.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-entity-guaranty] Indirect means ownership through an LLC or fund counts.
- **Spouses.** A spouse owning under 20% must guarantee in full when spouses and minor children together own 20% or more.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-spouse-guaranty]
- **Anyone the lender chooses.** The lender or SBA can require full or limited guaranties from others regardless of ownership, such as a minority owner critical to operations.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-other-guarantors]
- **Recent owners.** Someone who owned 20% or more in the six months before the application still has to guarantee even after dropping below 20%, unless they completely divest.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-six-month-lookback]

Guarantors who own 20% or more may also have to pledge personal real estate if the business assets don't fully secure the loan.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-personal-real-estate] Investors who expect to be passive should know this before they size their stake. Staying under 20% avoids the automatic guaranty, but the lender can still require one.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-other-guarantors]

### Every owner must be eligible

The citizenship and residency rule covers 100% of direct and indirect owners and required guarantors.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-citizenship-owners] A single non-citizen investor, even at 2%, can make the deal ineligible. See [the citizenship rule](/q/citizenship-rule).

### How investor money counts as equity

- **As your own cash.** Cash that isn't borrowed, including a gift, can supply all of the required injection.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-equity-unlimited-sources] SBA treats any owner receiving shares for cash as an equity investment, and the lender reviews its terms.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-external-capital-review]
- **As a "minority equity investment."** A non-controlling minority investor has under 20% and no control, and the investment can't be subject to any agreement to repay it or make distributions to recover it before the SBA guaranty is released.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-minority-investor] This sits in SBA's "limited" category, which together can supply no more than half of the required injection.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-equity-limited-sources]
- **Distributions are capped.** When investor money is used to meet the injection, the investor can receive only distributions to cover their tax on the business's income until the 7(a) loan is paid off.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-investor-distributions] Equity beyond the required injection can receive normal distributions, subject to the lender's agreements.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-additional-equity-distributions]
- **The lender reads the deal terms.** The lender must underwrite and review the terms of all other debt and equity in the transaction.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-external-capital-review] Preferred returns, redemption rights and puts in your operating agreement will be read against these rules.

### Practical steps

1. Share your draft cap table and operating agreement with the lender before you close investors.
2. Map each investor's percentage against the 20% guaranty line and the citizenship test.
3. Write the distribution limits into the investor terms so no one expects cash the SBA rules won't allow.
4. Have a securities lawyer handle the raise itself. See [Rule 506(b) or 506(c)](/q/506b-506c-securities) and [investor terms](/q/investor-terms).
