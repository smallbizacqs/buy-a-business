**Short answer.** On closing day the purchase agreement and the closing documents are signed, the lender funds the loan, money moves to the seller and to pay off the seller's debts, and the business becomes yours. In small deals, signing the purchase agreement and closing usually happen on the same day. Most of the work happens in the days before: every condition met, every document final, and a closing statement showing where each dollar goes.

**When signing and closing happen on different days.** You sign first and close later when something can only happen after there's a signed agreement, or takes time you don't want to spend unsigned. Common cases:

- A license or permit transfer that needs an agency's approval, such as a liquor license.
- A landlord, franchisor or key customer who will only consent once there's a signed deal.
- A lender that wants the signed agreement before it finishes approval or schedules closing.
- Regulatory approvals or notices that must happen before ownership changes.

In a split closing, the agreement needs closing conditions, a deadline, and rules for how the business is run in between. See [closing conditions](/q/closing-conditions).

**Before closing day:**

- The lender's conditions are met. With an SBA 7(a) loan (a bank loan partly guaranteed by the U.S. Small Business Administration), SBA SOP 50 10 8.1 (effective October 1, 2026) says the lender must have its collateral and every condition in its credit memo, such as the equity injection, standby agreements and appraisals, before or at disbursement.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-conditions-before-disbursement]
- Payoff letters from the seller's lenders, so their liens are released.
- A closing statement (sources and uses, or funds flow) agreed by buyer, seller and lender.
- Insurance bound in your company's name, and bank accounts open.

**On the day:**

1. Sign the purchase agreement (if not already signed) and closing documents: bill of sale, assignment of contracts and lease, seller note, consulting and non-compete agreements.
2. Sign the loan documents.
3. Funds move. An SBA lender may hold loan funds in escrow for no more than 5 business days to facilitate closing.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-closing-escrow]
4. You get keys, passwords, bank and payment-processor access, and the files.

**Right after:** tell employees and key customers as you planned with the seller. See [telling employees](/q/announce-to-employees) and [day-one readiness](/q/day-one-readiness).
