**Short answer.** Send short, personal, plain-text emails to the owner by name: who you are, why their business, and one easy question. Follow up a few times over weeks, stop when asked, and send at a volume your domain can handle. Treat every message as if U.S. email law applies: honest sender and subject line, your postal address, and a clear way to opt out. This is general information, not legal advice, and rules vary; see [outreach rules](/q/outreach-rules).

**The rules, in brief**

The federal CAN-SPAM Act covers email whose primary purpose is the commercial advertisement or promotion of a commercial product or service.[^ftc-can-spam-commercial-message] Whether an email offering to buy someone's business falls in that definition is a legal question, so the simple course is to follow its requirements anyway. There's no exception for business-to-business email.[^ftc-can-spam-b2b] The FTC's compliance guide lists, among others:

- **Honest subject lines.** The subject line must accurately reflect the content of the message.[^ftc-can-spam-subject-line]
- **A postal address.** The message must include your valid physical postal address.[^ftc-can-spam-postal-address]
- **Opt-out.** You must honor an opt-out request within 10 business days.[^ftc-can-spam-opt-out]
- **Responsibility stays with you.** Hiring someone to send for you doesn't move your legal responsibility.[^ftc-can-spam-vendor-responsibility]

Each violating email can carry a penalty of up to $53,088, according to the FTC.[^ftc-can-spam-penalty] State laws can add to these rules.

**Writing the email**

- **Subject:** plain and specific, such as the business name and a short question. No tricks.
- **First line:** why you're writing to them in particular.
- **Who you are:** one or two sentences. An individual buyer who wants to own and run one business reads differently from a fund that buys dozens; say which you are, truthfully.
- **The ask:** a question that's easy to answer, such as whether they've thought about what happens to the business when they step back, or whether a short call would be welcome.
- **Signature:** full name, phone, postal address, and a line saying they can reply "no thanks" and you won't write again.
- **Leave out:** price talk, attachments, long biographies, and claims you can't back up (for example, implying financing is approved when it isn't).

**Cadence**

A first email and a few follow-ups spaced a week or more apart, each short and adding something (a different angle, a question, an offer to call instead). Then pause and try again months later if they haven't opted out. Log every message and every reply, and honor every "stop" immediately on all channels, not just email.

**Getting delivered**

- **Authenticate your domain.** Gmail requires every sender to set up SPF or DKIM authentication (records that prove mail from your domain is really from you).[^google-gmail-sender-authentication]
- **Watch complaints.** Gmail tells senders to keep their reported spam rate below 0.3%.[^google-gmail-sender-spam-rate] Short, relevant emails to a well-chosen list keep complaints down.
- **Start slowly** on a new domain or mailbox and keep volume modest.
- **Avoid** link-heavy messages, images and attachments in first contact.
- **Clean your list.** Verify addresses before sending; bounces hurt your reputation with mail providers.

**Where email fits**

Email is cheap and easy to scale, and easy to ignore. Pair it with [calls](/q/cold-calling) and [letters](/q/direct-mail), and build the list carefully first: see [data sources and lists](/q/data-and-lists).
