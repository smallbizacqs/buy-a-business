**Short answer.** Businesses tend to hold up in recessions when customers need what they sell regardless of the economy, revenue recurs under contracts or routine schedules, the customer base is spread across many accounts, and costs can flex down when sales fall. Against AI, the sturdier businesses are those whose value is delivered in person and on site, depends on licensed or skilled hands, or rests on local trust and relationships. No business is immune to either. Test the specific target's history and exposure rather than relying on its category.

### Traits that help in a recession

- **Non-discretionary demand.** Repairs, required inspections, health and safety, basic maintenance. Customers can delay a new kitchen; they can't delay fixing a burst pipe.
- **Recurring revenue.** Service contracts, subscriptions and scheduled routes keep billing when new sales slow. See [recurring revenue](/q/recurring-revenue).
- **Many small customers.** No single customer's cutback sinks the business. See [customer concentration](/q/customer-concentration).
- **Maintenance over new installs.** Fixing and servicing what customers already own can hold up when they put off buying something new.
- **Flexible costs.** Variable labor, low fixed overhead and modest debt give room when revenue drops. See [stress testing](/q/stress-test).
- **Pricing power.** A business that can pass on cost increases keeps its margins when inflation and recession overlap.

### Traits that help against AI

- **Physical, on-site work.** Installing, repairing, cleaning, inspecting and transporting all happen in the physical world.
- **Licensed or skilled labor.** Work that requires a credentialed person, or skill built over years, is harder to automate.
- **Trust and relationships.** Local reputation, long-standing accounts and personal service.
- **Using AI as a tool, not facing it as a competitor.** A business that can adopt AI for scheduling, quoting or billing may become more efficient. See [AI in operations](/q/ai-in-operations).

Businesses whose core output is information work done on a screen (routine content, basic bookkeeping, data entry, simple design, first-line customer support) face more risk that software does the job cheaper. See [AI disruption](/q/ai-disruption).

### How to test a specific business

- **Look at past downturns.** If the business is old enough, ask for revenue and margins through earlier recessions and through 2020. Adjust for one-time items. See [COVID and PPP normalization](/q/covid-ppp-normalization).
- **Map the customer base.** Which customers are discretionary spenders, and which can't do without the service?
- **Ask what customers would do without it.** If they'd do it themselves, use software, or skip it in a bad year, that's exposure.
- **Check the cost structure.** How quickly could costs fall if revenue fell by a meaningful share?
- **Watch the macro links.** Housing, interest rates, energy prices or one industry's health can drive demand. See [macro exposure](/q/macro-exposure).

### A caution

Resilience can be priced in. Businesses with these traits may attract more buyers and higher multiples. Pay for resilience you've verified, not for a label. See [what multiple to pay](/q/what-multiple) and [the boring business case](/q/boring-business-thesis).
