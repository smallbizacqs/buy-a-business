**Short answer.** Receivables, payables, inventory and customer deposits are usually handled together as net working capital: they stay with the business, and the price is adjusted after closing if their combined total differs from the agreed target (the peg). Each one still needs its own diligence and its own rule in the purchase agreement. Customer deposits need special care, because they are work you owe after closing for money the seller already collected.

**Receivables (money customers owe).** Review an aging report (receivables sorted by how overdue they are). Agree how old or disputed receivables are treated, and who keeps what's collected after closing.

**Payables (money owed to suppliers).** Check for late payments that the seller let build up before closing, and agree which payables you take on.

**Inventory.** Plan a physical count at or near closing, agree how it's valued, and agree how slow-moving, damaged or obsolete stock is treated.

**Customer deposits and deferred revenue.** These are payments for work not yet done. Agree whether they reduce the price, count in working capital, or are paid over to you at closing, and get a list by customer.

**Financing.** Under SBA SOP 50 10 8.1 (effective October 1, 2026), for an SBA 7(a) acquisition a lender may finance the part of the purchase that relates to working assets (such as receivables and inventory) on a line of credit.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-working-assets-line] Ask your lender how it would handle this in your deal. An SBA 7(a) loan is a bank loan partly guaranteed by the U.S. Small Business Administration (SBA).

See [what net working capital is](/q/what-is-nwc), [cash-free, debt-free](/q/cash-free-debt-free) and [contractor work in progress](/q/wip-backlog).

## What practitioners say
- Darin Pierson, a CPA, says customer prepayments (deferred revenue) are a debt the buyer inherits in a cash-free, debt-free deal, so they belong in the working-capital adjustment.[^cba4593]
