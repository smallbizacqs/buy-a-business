**Short answer.** The lender sets your 7(a) rate, fixed or variable, up to an SBA maximum; for a variable-rate loan over $350,000 the cap is the base rate plus 3%.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-max-variable-rates] SBA charges an upfront guaranty fee on the guaranteed part of the loan, and the lender can pass it to you.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-upfront-fee] An acquisition loan amortizes over no more than 10 years, except any real estate in the deal.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-change-of-ownership-term] Rates and fees change by SBA notice, so get current figures from SBA and your lender before you build a model on them.

### Interest rates

Under SOP 50 10 8.1 (effective October 1, 2026), a 7(a) loan may have a fixed or variable rate, negotiated with the lender up to SBA's maximum; SBA publishes the maximum fixed rate in the Federal Register.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-interest-rate-negotiated] SBA posts its current maximum fixed rates on its FTA wiki.[^sba-7a-terms-rates]

For variable rates, the cap is a base rate (prime or SBA's optional peg rate) plus a spread that depends on loan size: 6.5% for loans of $50,000 or less, 6% up to $250,000, 4.5% up to $350,000 and 3% above that.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-max-variable-rates] The spread written into your note stays fixed for the life of the loan unless you agree in writing to change it, so your rate moves with the base rate.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-variable-rate] The cap is a ceiling. Ask each lender for its actual spread, how often the rate resets, and whether a fixed-rate option is available.

### Guaranty fees

- **Upfront fee.** SBA charges the lender a fee on each 7(a) loan, figured on the guaranteed portion rather than the full loan, and the lender may pass it to you.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-upfront-fee] The SOP's maximum chart for loans over 12 months is 3% of the guaranteed portion for loans of $150,001 to $700,000, and 3.5% of the guaranteed portion up to $1 million plus 3.75% above that for loans of $700,001 to $5 million.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-upfront-fee-chart]
- **These are maximums, and SBA resets them each year.** SBA publishes the upfront fee and the lender's annual service fee each fiscal year in an Information Notice, and the annual fee can't be charged to you.[^sba-7a-terms-fees-notice] SBA can also make temporary changes for a given fiscal year,[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-upfront-fee] so check the current notice instead of relying on the chart.

### Terms (maturity)

A 7(a) loan for a change of ownership amortizes over no more than 10 years and can't have a balloon, special purpose properties aside.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-change-of-ownership-term] If the deal includes real estate, only the real estate portion may run longer, up to 25 years.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-real-estate-structure] See [financing the building](/q/real-estate-in-deal).

### Prepayment

A 7(a) lender may not charge its own prepayment fees, origination or broker fees, or points.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-prohibited-fees] SBA itself charges a fee only on loans of 15 years or longer, when you prepay more than 25% in a year during the first three years: 5%, 3% or 1% of the prepayment, depending on the year.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-subsidy-recoupment-fee] A 10-year business-only acquisition loan isn't subject to it.

### Other costs at closing

The lender may charge reasonable packaging fees[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-packaging-fees] and pass through out-of-pocket costs such as filing fees and collateral appraisals.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-out-of-pocket] It must itemize its fees in writing and tell you that you don't have to pay for services you don't want.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-fee-itemization]

### What to ask each lender

- The spread over prime, the reset frequency, and the fixed-rate option.
- The upfront fee under the current fiscal-year notice, and whether it's being financed in the loan.
- An itemized list of every fee you'll pay at closing.
- Whether any part of the loan runs 15 years or more, which brings in the prepayment fee.
