**Short answer.** They are the same 7(a) program in smaller sizes. Under SOP 50 10 8.1 (effective October 1, 2026), a 7(a) Small loan is a term loan of $350,000 or less, and an SBA Express loan is a loan of $500,000 or less made only by a lender with SBA Express authority.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-small-express-definition] To buy a business, both follow the same change-of-ownership rules as a larger 7(a) loan, including the coverage test and the 10% equity injection. What differs is mostly the lender's paperwork and process, a lighter valuation rule at a price of $350,000 or less, a smaller SBA guaranty on Express loans, and a narrower collateral search.

### The two tiers

- **7(a) Small.** Capped at $350,000 per project, counting other 7(a) loans made within 90 days. If the loan is increased above $350,000, it becomes a Standard 7(a) loan with the full rules.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-small-loan-max] SBA guarantees up to 85% of a 7(a) Small loan of $150,000 or less and 75% of a larger one.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-small-express-guaranty]
- **SBA Express.** Capped at $500,000.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-express-loc] Express loans are processed only under the lender's delegated authority, and the lender makes the credit decision without prior SBA review.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-express-delegated] SBA guarantees 50% of an Express loan.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-small-express-guaranty] SBA describes Express as a way to reduce government forms and procedures for smaller, less complex loans.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-express-purpose]
- **Both.** Lenders may largely use their own forms, credit memos, notes, collateral documents and servicing documents.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-small-express-lender-documents]

With only half of an Express loan guaranteed, the lender keeps more of the risk, so its own credit appetite matters more.

### What stays the same for a business purchase

A purchase financed with either tier follows the SBA's change-of-ownership appendix (Appendix 15), with only a short list of changes.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-small-express-coo] So:

- **Coverage.** There is no lower debt service coverage standard for a small loan.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-small-express-coo-dsc] For an initial acquisition the minimum is 1.25:1 on the last fiscal year or the average of the last two.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-dsc-ratio] Debt service coverage (DSC) is cash flow available for debt payments divided by those payments. See [DSCR](/q/dscr).
- **Equity.** An initial acquisition still needs a 10% equity injection, which can't be reduced.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-equity-minimum] See [equity injection](/q/equity-injection).
- **Term.** An acquisition loan amortizes over no more than 10 years, outside special purpose property, and can't have a balloon payment.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-change-of-ownership-term]
- **Guaranty.** Every individual owning 20% or more gives an unlimited personal guaranty.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-personal-guaranty] See [personal guarantee](/q/personal-guarantee).
- **Rate caps.** Maximum variable rates are set by loan size, with higher allowed spreads on smaller loans.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-max-variable-rates]

### What differs

**Valuation.** If the business purchase price is $350,000 or less, the lender may do its own valuation instead of hiring an outside valuation professional, unless the buyer and seller are closely related.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-valuation-350k]

**Collateral.** An acquisition loan under either tier must meet Appendix 15's "fully secured" test, but the lender doesn't have to count other commercial real estate held by the borrower or guarantors.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-small-express-coo-collateral] Where business assets fall short, the general rule is that the lender takes available equity in personal real estate of 20% owners and guarantors,[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-personal-real-estate] so ask early whether your home will be pledged. See [home as collateral](/q/collateral-home). Outside acquisitions, loans of $50,000 or less need no collateral under either tier,[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-small-collateral][^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-express-collateral] but an acquisition still faces the fully secured test.

**Insurance.** For 7(a) Small and Express loans, whether you need life insurance follows the lender's own written policy.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-small-express-life-insurance] An Express lender may skip hazard insurance on a small loan if it documents why.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-hazard-express-exception]

### Deciding whether a small loan fits

The tiers suit deals where all the SBA financing fits under the cap. If you might need more, such as a working capital loan within 90 days, plan for a Standard 7(a) loan from the start. See [SBA 7(a) basics](/q/sba-7a-basics) and [loan limits](/q/loan-limits-pari-passu).

Questions to ask each lender:

- Which tier would you use, and do you hold SBA Express authority?
- Will you value the business yourselves or order an outside valuation?
- Whose loan documents will I sign, and what covenants and reporting will they require after closing? See [what the lender expects after closing](/q/lender-after-closing).
- Will you require life insurance, and on whom?
- What fees apply? SBA sets its guaranty fees each fiscal year by Information Notice.[^sba-7a-terms-fees-notice] See [rates, fees and terms](/q/rates-fees-terms).

SBA rules change; confirm with your lender and the current SOP.
