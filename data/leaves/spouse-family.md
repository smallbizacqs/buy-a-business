**Short answer.** Put the real numbers in front of your spouse or partner early: how much cash goes in, what the personal guarantee can reach, whether the home could be pledged, how long income may dip and what the hours will be. Agree on limits and a worst-case plan together before you make an offer, so the decision is shared rather than sprung on them at closing.

### What to put on the table

- **Cash at risk.** The equity you'll put in, plus search, diligence and closing costs, and what's left as a household reserve. See [readiness](/q/readiness).
- **The guarantee.** For an SBA 7(a) loan, under SOP 50 10 8.1 (effective October 1, 2026), every individual owning 20% or more gives an unlimited personal guarantee.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-personal-guaranty] If the business fails and the collateral falls short, the lender can pursue personal assets. See [personal guarantees](/q/personal-guarantee).
- **Whether your spouse signs.** A spouse who owns less than 20% must also guarantee in full when spouses and minor children together own 20% or more.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-spouse-guaranty] And the personal financial statement owners give the lender includes a spouse's assets.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-owner-financial-statement]
- **The house.** When business assets don't fully secure the loan, the lender must take available equity in personal real estate owned by 20% owners and guarantors,[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-personal-real-estate] though it need not take real estate with less than 25% equity.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-real-estate-equity-threshold] See [collateral and your home](/q/collateral-home).
- **Income.** Your pay may drop in the first period of ownership. See [the pay dip after closing](/q/j-curve).
- **Time.** Long weeks during diligence and the first year. See [hours and lifestyle](/q/hours-lifestyle).

### How to have the conversation

- **Start before you're attached to a deal.** It's easier to agree rules in the abstract than to say no to a business you love.
- **Use a simple downside case.** What happens to the household if the business earns a third less than expected? If it fails? Write it down.
- **Agree limits.** The most you'll put in, whether the house can be pledged, the deal size, and what would make you both walk away. See [when to walk away](/q/walk-away).
- **Agree a check-in.** A regular time to talk about how the search, and later the business, is going.
- **Bring them in.** Invite your partner to meet the lender, the seller or your advisors if they want to.

### Questions to answer together

- How long can we live on one income, or on savings?
- Who handles childcare, caregiving and home duties when the business needs more?
- How would we feel about selling or pledging assets if things go badly?
- How does our state treat marital property and debts? That's a question for a lawyer in your state; see [asset protection](/q/asset-protection).
- Is there insurance that would help? See [insuring a guarantee](/q/pg-insurance).

### Wider family

If parents or relatives are lending or investing, put terms in writing and treat them like any other investor, including what happens if the business struggles.
