**Short answer.** List every job the owner does today, price each one at what you would pay someone else to do it (salary, payroll taxes and benefits), and subtract that total from the seller's discretionary earnings. What's left is what the business earns for an owner who doesn't work in it. If you plan to do some of the jobs yourself, the cost is still real: it's the pay you need to live on.

**Why this matters**

Seller's discretionary earnings (SDE) add the owner's whole salary and perks back to profit. That shows what one owner-operator takes home, not what the business earns after paying for the work. If the owner runs sales, estimates jobs and keeps the books, those jobs don't disappear when they leave. See [SDE vs. EBITDA](/q/sde-vs-ebitda) and [add-backs](/q/add-backs).

**How to build the number**

1. **Map the roles.** Ask the owner to walk through a typical week and month: selling, scheduling, quoting, hiring, purchasing, bookkeeping, fieldwork. Owners often carry two or three jobs.
2. **Price each role at market.** Use local job postings, recruiter conversations and pay surveys for your area. A general manager, an estimator and a part-time bookkeeper each have their own market rate.
3. **Add employer costs.** Employers pay their own share of Social Security and Medicare taxes on top of wages,[^irs-employment-taxes-employer-share] plus unemployment taxes, workers' compensation, benefits and any bonus you'd need to offer.
4. **Subtract from SDE.** The result is close to EBITDA (earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation and amortization) with a hired team in place.

**If you'll do the work yourself**

Your own pay still has to come out of the business. Under SOP 50 10 8.1 (effective October 1, 2026), an SBA lender may adjust cash flow for owner compensation,[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-adjustment-items] but must justify each adjustment as prudent, necessary and supported by ongoing operations.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-adjustment-justification] Any adjustment to owner pay must also be backed by a global cash flow analysis showing you can meet your obligations on that pay,[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-owner-comp-global] and your pay must be enough to cover your obligations and living expenses.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-owner-compensation] So you can't assume a token salary to make the numbers work.

**What a quality of earnings report looks for**

When a QoE (an independent review of the seller's earnings) is required for an SBA loan, it must document owner compensation that is above or below market.[^sba-sop-50-10-8-1-qoe-revenue-quality] An owner who pays themselves little looks more profitable on paper; the gap to a market salary is a cost you will carry.

**Common mistakes**

- Pricing one manager for an owner who does three jobs.
- Forgetting the owner's unpaid spouse or family member who answers phones or does the books.
- Counting the owner's pay as profit and then also planning to draw a salary.
- Using a national average when your local market pays more.
