**Short answer.** Letters can work as one channel among several. A letter is harder to delete than an email and can reach owners who screen calls, but it's slower and costs more per contact, and there's no official data on response. Mail a short, personal letter to the owner by name, follow it with a call or email, and track every response so you learn what works for you.

**Why letters**

- **They get opened.** A letter addressed to the owner by name, especially with a handwritten envelope, stands out from bills and catalogs.
- **They're easy to keep.** An owner who isn't ready today may file it and call later.
- **They set a tone.** A thoughtful letter suggests a careful buyer.

The downsides: printing and postage for each piece, days to deliver, and no live reaction. A letter also can't answer questions, so it should invite the next step.

**Running a campaign**

1. **Build the list.** Businesses that fit your [buy box](/q/define-buy-box), with the owner's name and a business mailing address. See [data sources and lists](/q/data-and-lists).
2. **Write one page.** Who you are, why their business, what you're hoping for (a conversation, not a sale), and how to reach you. Plain language, no hype, no price.
3. **Personalize.** The owner's name, the business name, and one specific detail. Sign by hand if you can.
4. **Send to the business address and mark it personal.** Owners sometimes don't want staff to know they'd consider selling; a "personal and confidential" envelope respects that.
5. **Follow up.** A call or email a week or two after the letter arrives turns a letter into a conversation. See [cold calls](/q/cold-calling) and [cold email](/q/cold-email).
6. **Track responses.** Give each mailing batch a code (a dedicated phone number, email address or web page) so you know which letters and lists produce replies.
7. **Mail in waves.** A smaller batch first, adjusted from the replies, then the next.

**Formats**

- **Typed letter, hand-signed:** quick to produce and still personal.
- **Handwritten letter or note:** the most personal, and the slowest to produce. Services that print in a handwriting style exist; be honest if asked.
- **Postcard:** cheapest and fastest to read, but public; anyone handling the mail can see it, which can embarrass an owner who'd sell quietly.

**Costs**

Printing, envelopes, postage, list data and your time. Get quotes from a printer or mail house and the post office for your volume rather than relying on a rule of thumb.

**Rules**

Email rules such as CAN-SPAM are about electronic mail;[^ftc-can-spam-commercial-message] a paper letter isn't governed by them. Telemarketing rules may apply to the calls you make to follow up, and many states have their own telemarketing laws.[^ftc-tsr-state-laws] Keep letters truthful, and if an owner asks you to stop, stop on every channel. See [outreach rules](/q/outreach-rules).

**Common mistakes**

Mass-produced letters that could go to anyone, long letters about the buyer, mentioning a price, and sending once with no follow-up.
