This stage is for sizing up a specific business: what it really earns, what it's worth, how much you can borrow against it, and which risks change the price. You're here once you have a business's financials in hand and are deciding what to offer. Earnings are usually expressed as seller's discretionary earnings (SDE), which adds back one owner's pay, or EBITDA (earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation and amortization).

## Real earnings

Start by rebuilding the earnings: check add-backs, the owner's real role and its cost, and whether the tax returns, books and bank statements agree. Unusual years and capital spending needs belong in the picture too.

## What it's worth

A valuation multiple is price divided by earnings. What you can sensibly pay depends on how reliable and durable those earnings are and on what a lender will finance, not on a single "market" number.

- [How is a small business valued: multiples, cash flow or assets?](/q/valuation-methods)
- [How do I handle an unrealistic asking price?](/q/asking-price-gap)

## What you can borrow

Lenders size a loan from historical cash flow and a minimum debt service coverage ratio (DSCR). Work out what the business can carry, then stress-test it for a bad year.

- [What debt service coverage do lenders require, and how is it calculated?](/q/dscr)
- [How much can I pay for a business given the loan payments?](/q/affordability)
