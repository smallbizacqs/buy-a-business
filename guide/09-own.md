You're here from the day after closing. The first 100 days are about keeping what you bought: the employees, the customers and the cash, while the seller hands over what only they know. Most of the work is listening and learning how the business really runs, and making only the changes that are urgent or clearly safe.

## The handover

The seller's transition period is when they introduce you to customers and suppliers, explain how the work gets done, and step back. Set its length by a written list of what has to be handed over, and agree how the seller describes their role. With an SBA 7(a) loan (a bank loan partly guaranteed by the U.S. Small Business Administration) for an initial acquisition, the seller can stay only as a consultant, for up to 24 months.

## The first 100 days

Take control of cash first: bank access, who approves payments, and a weekly cash report. Then learn the business from employees and customers before changing prices, people or systems. Put the post-closing deadlines on a calendar, such as the working-capital true-up, escrow release dates and tax filings.

## Running it

After the first few months, the job becomes running and improving the business: a management rhythm, the right team, steady sales, and support for yourself. Running a business on your own can be isolating, and some owners join peer groups or set up an advisory board. Understanding why acquisitions fail after closing helps you avoid the common traps.
