You're here when diligence is done, the loan is approved or nearly so, and the purchase agreement is being finalized. In small deals, signing the purchase agreement and closing usually happen on the same day. They happen on different days when something can only follow a signed agreement, such as a license transfer that needs an agency's approval or a landlord who will consent only to a signed deal. This stage covers the agreement's key terms and what has to be ready for closing day.

## The purchase agreement

The purchase agreement is the binding contract: what you're buying, which liabilities you take, the price and its adjustments, the seller's representations and warranties (statements of fact about the business), and indemnification (the seller's promise to cover losses if those statements prove wrong). Much of the real negotiation happens in its schedules, the lists of assets, contracts and exceptions. Some buyers start from a free template, such as the [online business asset purchase agreement at openagreements.org](https://openagreements.org/templates/openagreements-online-business-asset-purchase-agreement), and its [asset purchase practice guide](https://openagreements.org/practice-guides/asset-purchase/us).

## Closing day

Closing is when documents are signed, the lender funds, the seller's debts are paid off, and the business becomes yours. Most of the work is in the days before: every lender condition met, a closing statement showing where each dollar goes, your company formed with its bank account open, and insurance and payroll ready for day one. Plan the handover of keys, passwords and accounts with the seller.
