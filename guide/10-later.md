You're here once the business is stable under you and you're asking what's next: making it more valuable, buying more businesses, or eventually selling. The same things that made the business easier to buy, such as earnings that hold up, a team that runs it without the owner, and clean records, also make it easier to grow, finance and sell. None of these paths is guaranteed to work; each adds risk as well as opportunity.

## Growing value

A business is worth more when it earns more and depends less on you. That usually means documented processes, managers who can make decisions, reliable financial reporting, and a broad customer base. This work is also the groundwork for any later sale or refinancing.

- [How do I increase the value of the business after buying it?](/q/value-creation)

## Buying more

An add-on acquisition is buying another business to combine with yours; a roll-up is a strategy of buying several in the same industry. They can add scale, but they also add debt, integration work and management strain. Claims about how much value roll-ups create vary widely, so test them against your own numbers.

## Selling

Exit options include selling to an outside buyer, selling to employees or partners, passing the business to family, selling gradually with seller financing, or closing and selling the assets. Refinancing can also take money out without selling, at the cost of more debt. Taxes on a sale depend on the structure, so bring in a CPA years before you plan to sell.
