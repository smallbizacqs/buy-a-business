You're here once the letter of intent is signed and exclusivity has started. This is confirmatory due diligence: checking that the earnings, contracts, people and risks are what you were told, while your lender underwrites the loan. You're also deciding, with evidence, whether to go ahead, change the terms, or walk away, and lining up everything the lender needs to approve and close.

## Financial due diligence

Financial diligence tests whether the earnings you paid for are real and likely to continue, by matching bank deposits, tax returns and financial statements. A quality of earnings (QoE) report is an independent accountant's version of this work; for purchases financed with an SBA 7(a) loan (a bank loan partly guaranteed by the U.S. Small Business Administration) where the business purchase price is $3 million or more, SBA rules require the lender to get one for an initial acquisition. Stage your spending so the cheap checks that could kill the deal come first.

## Legal due diligence

Legal diligence checks whether what you're buying will actually transfer to you, and what liabilities come with it: contracts, the lease, licenses and permits, lawsuits, intellectual property and regulatory issues. Many of these need a third party's consent, such as a landlord, a licensing agency or a key customer, so start them early. Your lawyer leads this work.

## Operational due diligence

Operational diligence looks at how the business actually runs: the people and payroll, the customers, where new work comes from, the systems and records, and insurance. It's where you find out how much depends on the seller personally. Talk to employees and customers only when and how you've agreed with the seller.

## Go or walk away

Some findings mean the business won't earn what you're paying; others are real but measurable and call for a new price or structure. Decide before diligence what would make you walk, so deal fatigue doesn't decide for you. With an SBA loan, findings can change what the lender will lend, which forces the question.

## The lender's process

The lender needs documents about you, the business and the deal, plus an independent business valuation, before it can approve the loan. How long approval takes depends on the lender, whether it can approve under its own delegated SBA authority, and how quickly everyone responds. Ask your lender for its checklist and recent timelines on day one.
